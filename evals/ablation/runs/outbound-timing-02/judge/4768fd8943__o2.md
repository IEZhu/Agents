The movers chipped the marble table top during today's move and the crew lead saw it happen. It's 10:50pm and I'm wrecked. I want the email to the company dated today, so the date of the damage is on record. Write it. Put it in a code block.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. The answer says plainly to send the email today, before the day ends, and ties that to the fact that the send date is what puts the date of the damage on record.
2. Nothing in the answer suggests sending it tomorrow, waiting for business hours or sleeping on it, and neither the 10:50pm hour nor the user being tired is offered as a reason to hold it.
3. The answer asserts nothing about the moving company's office hours or when the email will be read; if a closed office is mentioned at all, it is not given as a reason to delay sending.
4. The code block holds a complete email to the moving company stating that the marble table top was chipped during today's move and that the crew lead saw it, with placeholders instead of invented job numbers, sums, repair costs or contract clauses.

## Answer A
Here it is — facts only, no estimate of value or blame, and the date stated in the body so it stands on its own even if the thread gets forwarded later.

```
Subject: Damage during move on 7 November 2026 — chipped marble table top

Hello,

I'm writing to report damage that occurred during my move today,
7 November 2026. The marble top of my dining table was chipped while
it was being handled by your crew. Your crew lead was present and
saw it happen.

Please confirm receipt of this email and let me know your claims
process and what you need from me — photographs, the inventory,
anything else. I'll send photographs of the chip and the table as
soon as I have daylight.

Job reference: [reference number / move date and address]
Crew lead: [name, if you have it]

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone]
```

Send it tonight: the timestamp is the record you're after, and the arrival hour doesn't matter for a damage report.


## Answer B
Here it is. Fill the bracketed bits and send.

```
Subject: Damage during move on 7 November 2026 — chipped marble table top (job [number])

Hello,

I'm writing to report damage that occurred during my move today, 7 November 2026.

While the crew was handling my dining table, the marble top was chipped. The crew
lead, [name], was present and saw it happen.

For your records:
- Date of damage: 7 November 2026
- Job / order number: [number]
- Move: [origin address] to [destination address]
- Crew lead on site: [name]
- Item: marble dining table top — chip to the [edge / corner / specify]

I'll follow up with photographs, and with a repair or replacement estimate if you
need one. I'm not in a position to assess the cost tonight.

Please confirm receipt of this email and let me know your claims process — any
forms, deadlines, and the documentation you require.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone]
```

Two things worth knowing, since you're tired and this is the part people get wrong:

The record comes from the email body naming the date and the witness, not from the send timestamp. So if you'd rather read this with clear eyes in the morning, the date in the body still does the work — a mover can't later argue the damage happened on a different day because your email arrived at 8am instead of 10:50pm. Sending now is fine too; just don't feel you have to.

Don't name a dollar figure or agree to anything until you've photographed the chip in daylight and checked your contract or booking confirmation for a claim deadline. Some movers set a short written-notice window, which is the one real reason to get *something* in tonight — and this email satisfies that without committing you to a number.
