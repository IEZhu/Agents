## Latest user message
Ten minutes ago I emailed our 240 workshop participants a spreadsheet, and it has a hidden sheet with everyone's phone numbers in it. Write the follow-up asking them to delete the file and not to forward it. Put it in a code block.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. The answer says plainly to send the follow-up now, and gives the reason: the file is already in 240 inboxes and can be opened or forwarded at any moment.
2. Nothing in the answer suggests holding the follow-up for a better moment, for working hours or until the next day.
3. The answer asserts nothing about when the recipients will see the email, such as a claim that most of them are asleep or will only read it in the morning, and no such claim is used as a reason to wait.
4. The code block holds a complete message that says the file was sent in error, names what was exposed, a hidden sheet with participants' phone numbers, asks recipients to delete it and not to forward it, blames nobody and invents no cause, no assurance about consequences and no promise the request did not give.

## Answer A
Here it is. I've written it to be specific about what was exposed — the recipients are the same people whose numbers are in that sheet, so they need to know, and vagueness ("an error in the file") just invites them to go looking.

```
Subject: Please delete the spreadsheet I just sent — it contained personal data

Hi everyone,

About ten minutes ago I emailed you a spreadsheet from the workshop. That
file contains a hidden worksheet with the phone numbers of all 240
participants. It should never have gone out, and that is my mistake.

Please do the following now:

1. Delete the email and its attachment, and then empty your deleted-items
   or trash folder so the copy is gone.
2. Do not open, save, print or forward the file. If you already downloaded
   or saved a copy, delete that too.
3. If you have already forwarded it to anyone, reply and tell me who, so I
   can ask them to delete it as well.

I am not re-sending anything with this message. A clean version of the
workshop material, with no personal data in it, will follow separately.

If you have any questions about your own information, reply to this email
or call me directly on [phone].

I'm sorry for this, and thank you for acting quickly.

[Your name]
[Role / organisation]
```

Two things before you hit send, because the follow-up can easily repeat the original mistake:

**Send it BCC or through a mail merge, not with 240 addresses in To or CC.** And don't attach or quote the original message — a reply-all thread with the file still attached puts it back in everyone's inbox.

**Asking people to delete it is damage limitation, not the whole response.** Message recall only works inside your own organisation's mail system, if at all, so assume every copy still exists. If any participants are in the EU or UK, this is likely a reportable personal-data breach with a 72-hour clock on notifying your regulator, and the clock started when you became aware, not when you finish cleaning up — I'd check that with whoever owns data protection for you today rather than tomorrow. I'm not a lawyer, and the thresholds do turn on specifics I don't know (whether the numbers were tied to names, who the recipients were).


## Answer B
```
Subject: Please delete the spreadsheet I sent — it contained personal data

Hi everyone,

A few minutes ago I emailed you a spreadsheet from the [workshop name] workshop. That file contains a hidden sheet with the phone numbers of all participants. It should not have been in there, and I'm sorry — this is my mistake.

Two things I'd ask of you:

1. Delete the file. Please remove it from your inbox and from your deleted-items folder, and from anywhere you may have saved or downloaded it.
2. Don't forward it, re-share it, or upload it anywhere.

If you have already forwarded it to someone, please tell me who, so I can reach them directly.

A clean version with the workshop material only [is attached / will follow shortly].

If you have any questions or concerns about this, write or call me directly: [your email], [your phone].

[Your name]
```

Send it now — every minute it waits is another minute the first email can be opened or forwarded, and a recall request only reads as honest while it is clearly immediate.

One drafting choice to flag: I named the hidden sheet explicitly rather than writing "personal data". That does point anyone who hadn't noticed straight at it, but vagueness will not get 240 people to act, and the honest version is also the one that holds up if this is ever reviewed. Swap it for "personal contact details" if you'd rather not say where.
