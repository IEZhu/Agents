3. The message rejects the design doc plainly and keeps both reasons, that the invalidation path is unspecified and that no cache rewrite is happening this quarter, without being softened into an open question about whether to proceed.

## Answer A
```
Nils,

I'm rejecting the cache rewrite design doc. Two reasons, and they're different in kind.

1. The invalidation path is unspecified. The doc doesn't say what invalidates an
entry, who or what triggers the invalidation, or how reads in flight behave while
it happens. That's the core of a cache design, not an appendix — as it stands the
doc can't be reviewed on its merits.

2. We are not rewriting the cache this quarter. The priorities are set and this
isn't on the list. That reason is on me: you should have had the quarter's scope
before you spent time on the design, and I didn't give it to you. I'm not going to
pretend the doc failed on that point — it was the wrong work to be doing, and that
was my call to make and I made it late.

Practical consequence: don't rework the doc. Keep it. If the rewrite gets on a
roadmap later, the invalidation section is where you start, and I'd expect that
part specified before anything else gets written.

We'll talk it through in our next 1:1 and settle what you pick up instead. Nothing
in this needs a reply from you before then.
```

## Answer B
Two things before the text itself.

**Don't send it today.** He's off until the 18th. Sending it now gets you one of two outcomes: he reads it on holiday and spends a week chewing on a rejection he can't act on or argue with, or he reads it on the 18th anyway — in which case you gained nothing by sending it on the 11th. Queue it to land the morning he's back. The one exception: if the "no cache rewrite this quarter" decision is going to be communicated to the team before the 18th, then he should not hear it secondhand from a teammate, and a short note now beats that.

**The two reasons don't have equal standing, and the message shouldn't pretend they do.** "We're not rewriting a cache this quarter" is a priorities call you own. If he wrote a design doc for work that was already off the quarter, he burned that time because the decision wasn't communicated to him in time. That's your gap, not his, and the message should say so — not as softening, as accuracy. The unspecified invalidation path is the part that's actually his.

```
Nils — this is waiting for you for when you're back. Nothing to do on it before then.

The cache rewrite design doc is a no. Two separate reasons, and only one of them is about the doc.

First, and this one is mine: we are not rewriting the cache this quarter. I had made that call and had not told you before you wrote the doc. You spent that time because I didn't say it in time. That was avoidable and I'm sorry for the waste.

Second, the doc itself: the invalidation path is unspecified. Who invalidates, on what event, in what order, and what a reader sees mid-invalidation — none of it is in there. That gap would have sent the doc back for another round even if the rewrite were on the roadmap.

Where it lands: the doc stays, it doesn't get built. If the rewrite comes back next quarter, your draft is the starting point and the invalidation section is the first thing we write.

Book 30 minutes in our first 1:1 after you're back. I want to walk the invalidation design with you, and I want to hear where you think the quarter's priorities are wrong.
```

Two notes on what I did with it:

I kept the apology for the priorities gap and cut everything else that could read as cushioning. If you take the apology out, the message says his doc was rejected on its merits, which is only half true and he'll know it.

Don't let the 1:1 line become a formality. If he shows up with a case that the cache work should be in the quarter, you need to actually listen to it or you've taught him that asking is theatre.
