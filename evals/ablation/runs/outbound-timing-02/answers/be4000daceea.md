Here's the notice. One thing first: tomorrow is outside the window. If today is the last day for written notice, a notice sent tomorrow morning is late notice, and late notice is one of the few grounds an insurer can lean on to reduce or deny an otherwise valid claim. So this one has to go tonight.

That's less painful than it sounds, because notice and the claim are different jobs. Notice only has to identify you, the property, the date of loss and what happened — enough to put the insurer on record that a claim exists. The damage inventory, photos, contractor estimates and your account of how it unfolded all come later, in the adjuster's process, with as fresh a pair of eyes as you like. Send the short thing tonight; do the thinking tomorrow.

```
Subject: Notice of Claim — Water Damage from Ceiling Leak, [3 Month Year] — Policy [POLICY NUMBER]

To: [claims@insurer.example] / [adjuster or claims department]

Dear Claims Department,

I am providing written notice of a claim under homeowners policy
[POLICY NUMBER], issued to [Full Name(s) as named insured] for the
property at [Street Address, City, State, ZIP].

Date of loss: [3 Month Year]
Type of loss: Water damage from a ceiling leak
Location on premises: [e.g., living room ceiling, below the upstairs
bathroom]

Description: On [3 Month Year] I discovered water coming through the
ceiling in [room/area]. [One or two sentences on what you saw and, if
you know it, the apparent source — e.g., "the leak appears to originate
from a supply line in the bathroom directly above." If you don't know
the source, say so.] Visible damage at this time includes
[e.g., staining and sagging drywall across approximately X square feet,
and damage to the flooring and furnishings below].

Mitigation: To prevent further damage I have [e.g., shut off the water
supply to that fixture, placed containers and towels to collect water,
moved furniture out of the affected area, and begun drying the space].
[If applicable: I have engaged [contractor/plumber name] to stop the
source of the leak.] I have photographed the damage and am retaining
all receipts for emergency repairs and mitigation.

This email is intended as timely written notice of the claim within the
period required by the policy. A full inventory of damaged property,
photographs, and repair estimates will follow. Please confirm receipt of
this notice, assign a claim number, and advise me of the next steps,
including when an adjuster can inspect the property and what
documentation you require from me.

I can be reached at [phone number] or at this email address. My
preferred contact method is [phone/email].

Sincerely,
[Full Name]
[Property Address]
[Phone] | [Email]
[Policy Number]
```

Three practical notes before you hit send:

Keep the proof. Send it from an account you control and keep the sent copy with its timestamp; if your insurer has a claims portal or a 24-hour claims line, do that too and note the time and the name of whoever you spoke to. Belt and braces on the one element that has a deadline.

Don't guess at the policy language. I've written the notice line as "within the period required by the policy" rather than naming a number of days, because I don't know your policy's actual wording — if your documents specify a period, you can quote it, but there's no benefit to asserting a figure you haven't checked.

Don't understate the damage to keep it simple. Describe what you've actually seen and add that the full extent is still being assessed. Ceiling leaks routinely turn out worse than they first look once drywall comes down, and you don't want tonight's quick email read later as your own estimate of the loss.

If any of the bracketed facts are ones you'd rather not fill in cold at 8:30pm — the apparent source, the square footage — write "under assessment" and move on. An incomplete notice filed on time beats a polished one filed a day late.
