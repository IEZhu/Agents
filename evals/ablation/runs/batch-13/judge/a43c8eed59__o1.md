## Latest user message
Need a sanity check before I send an invoice. Customer is on our annual Basic plan, $1,200/yr, term started March 1, 2027 and runs until March 1, 2028. On July 15, 2027 they upgrade to Pro at $3,000/yr. Our policy: we prorate by actual days left in the current term (upgrade day counts as a Pro day), they get a credit for the unused Basic days, they have a 15% discount that applies to Pro only, and 20% VAT goes on top of the net amount. What exactly should the July 15 invoice total be? Our billing script says $1,020.82 and I just want to make sure.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Counts 230 remaining days from July 15, 2027 (inclusive) to March 1, 2028 (exclusive)
2. Notes that the term includes February 29, 2028, so it has 366 days, and flags that the script's $1,020.82 matches a 365-day basis
3. Computes Pro charge 2550 * 230/366 = about $1,602.46, Basic credit 1200 * 230/366 = about $754.10, net about $848.36, total with 20% VAT about $1,018.03
4. Gives a clear verdict that the script's figure is off by about $2.79 and should be corrected

## Answer A
**The invoice should be $1,018.03, not $1,020.82.** Your script is $2.79 too high. It divides by 365 days, but this term has 366 because it includes **February 29, 2028** (2028 is a leap year).

### The math

**Term length:** March 1, 2027 → March 1, 2028 = **366 days**

**Days left, counting July 15:**

| Period | Days |
|---|---|
| Jul 15–31 | 17 |
| Aug | 31 |
| Sep | 30 |
| Oct | 31 |
| Nov | 30 |
| Dec | 31 |
| Jan | 31 |
| Feb (2028) | 29 |
| **Total** | **230** |

**Invoice lines (fraction = 230/366):**

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Pro, prorated | $3,000 × 230/366 | $1,885.25 |
| 15% discount (Pro only) | −15% × $1,885.25 | −$282.79 |
| Pro after discount | | $1,602.46 |
| Credit for unused Basic days | −$1,200 × 230/366 | −$754.10 |
| **Net** | | **$848.36** |
| VAT 20% | 20% × $848.36 | $169.67 |
| **Total** | | **$1,018.03** |

You get the same result whether you round each line or only at the end: 1,350 × 230/366 × 1.2 = 1,018.0328.

### Where $1,020.82 comes from

If you use 365 instead of 366: $1,350 × 230/365 = $850.68 net, × 1.2 = **$1,020.82**. That is your script's number exactly, so the day count is the only difference. The discount and credit logic are fine.

### Two things to check

- **Your policy wording.** "Actual days left in the current term" points to 366 as the denominator here. But if your terms define the daily rate as annual price ÷ 365, the script is technically following policy, and you should reword the policy to match it. It's probably worth checking which one your terms actually say.
- **Fix the script for other invoices too.** This bug will affect every proration for a term that includes Feb 29, 2028. It's also a good idea to check whether it affected any terms that included Feb 29, 2024.

### Where $1,020.82 comes from

I get the script's number exactly if I keep 230 days left but divide by 365:

- Pro: 3,000 × 230/365 × 0.85 = $1,606.85
- Credit: 1,200 × 230/365 = $756.16
- Net $850.68, plus 20% VAT = **$1,020.82**

So the script probably hardcodes a 365-day year and doesn't use the actual length of the term. It counts the days left correctly, including Feb 29, but not the total days in the term. Your policy says to prorate by actual days, so 366 is the right denominator here.

### Things worth checking

- **Fix the script.** It will overcharge on every proration inside a term that contains a Feb 29. It will also give wrong credits on downgrades and refunds in those terms. Divide by (term end − term start) in days.
- **Rounding.** I rounded each line to the cent before adding them up. If you only round the final total, you still get $1,018.03 here, but it's worth making sure the invoice lines add up to the total shown.
- **Discount on the credit:** I didn't apply the 15% to the Basic credit, because your policy says the discount is for Pro only. If the customer's Basic plan was also discounted, the credit should be based on what they actually paid for Basic, and the total would change.
