## Answer A
Probably not, at least not most of it. I can't open Drive links, so this answer is based on California law rather than your lease. A lease clause can't override these rules anyway.

- **Wear and tear:** Civil Code §1950.5 only lets a landlord deduct for damage *beyond* ordinary wear and tear. After 4 years, faded paint and worn carpet are normally wear and tear. Holes, stains or pet damage are different.
- **21-day deadline:** He must send an itemized statement within 21 days of move-out, which means by Sept 21. Any deduction over $125 also needs receipts or invoices. If he missed that deadline, he may have lost the right to keep anything.
- **Bad faith:** If he kept the money in bad faith, a court can award up to 2× the deposit as a penalty.

These figures are from memory, so check them against the current text of §1950.5.

**Next step:** Send a written demand that cites §1950.5. If he still won't pay, file in small claims court.


## Answer B
I couldn't open the Drive link, so this is based on California law in general, not your lease. Most likely, no.

- **Wear and tear:** Under Cal. Civ. Code §1950.5(b), a landlord can deduct only for damage beyond ordinary wear and tear. After four years, faded paint and worn carpet are usually ordinary wear. Even for real damage, he can usually charge only the share of the carpet's or paint's useful life that was left, not the full replacement cost.
- **Paperwork:** He had 21 days after Aug 31, so until Sept 21, to send an itemized statement. Any charge over $125 needs receipts or invoices attached (§1950.5(g)). This is recalled, not verified, so check the current text.
- **Penalty:** If he keeps the money in bad faith, he can owe up to twice the deposit on top of it (§1950.5(l)).

**Next step:** Send a written demand for the $900 that cites §1950.5. If he doesn't pay, file in small claims court.
