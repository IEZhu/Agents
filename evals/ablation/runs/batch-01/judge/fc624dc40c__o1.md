## Answer A
No Cura profile will make this mini print clean on your setup as it stands. Here's why, what I'd do instead, and the settings for the parts that can work.

## ⚠️ The problems

| Spec | vs. 0.4 mm nozzle | Result on FDM |
|---|---|---|
| Bow string 0.3 mm | Thinner than the nozzle | ❌ Cura will probably drop it, or it'll print as a broken wisp |
| Arrow shafts 0.35 mm | Thinner than the nozzle | ❌ Same. They'll come out missing, lumpy or snapped |
| Cloak 1.1 mm | About 2–3 lines | ✅ Fine |
| "Pre-supported" | Almost certainly **resin** supports (thin, pin-tip contacts) | ❌ In PLA those tips sag, fail, or fuse to the model. Don't use them |
| 4.8★ from 6 ratings, renders only, 0 makes | Tiny sample, nobody has proven it prints | ⚠️ **Risky**. You'd be the first one to print it |

The spec sheet (features under 0.4 mm, pre-supported, 28 mm scale) reads like a model designed for resin. The renders look great because resin can hold that detail. An FDM print will look noticeably softer even when it goes well.

## What to do before Saturday

1. **Check the files first.** Many MyMiniFactory designers include an unsupported version. If there is one, use it and let Cura build the supports. If there isn't, message the designer. They may have an FDM version or be willing to thicken the bow.
2. **Print one test mini today.** Don't start with a batch of 5 on an untested model and a deadline.
3. **Pick a plan for the bow and arrows:**
   - **Easiest:** accept that the string won't print. Cut it off in Meshmixer or Blender, or just let Cura drop it, then add a real string after printing. A strand of fishing line or thread glued on looks better than printed string anyway.
   - **Thicken them:** in Blender, scale the arrow shafts up to around 0.5–0.6 mm. They'll look a little chunky but will actually exist.
   - **Swap the nozzle:** a 0.2 mm nozzle handles this much better. It's a cheap part, but changing it and dialing it in takes time you may not have this week.
   - **Fallback:** if you know anyone with a resin printer, or can use a local print service, this model is exactly what resin is for.

## Cura settings (Ender 3 V2, 0.4 nozzle, PLA)

These are good starting values for miniatures, not tested on this model. Tune them on the test print.

**Quality**
- Layer Height: **0.08–0.12 mm** (0.08 for the best detail, 0.12 if print time gets tight)
- Initial Layer Height: 0.2 mm
- Line Width: 0.4 mm. Leave it there; going narrower on a 0.4 nozzle rarely helps.

**Walls and thin features**
- Wall Line Count: 3 (with a 1.1 mm cloak this effectively makes it solid)
- **Print Thin Walls: On**
- Minimum Feature Size / Minimum Wall Line Width: lower them a bit (e.g. around 0.2–0.3 mm) so Cura keeps small details instead of dropping them. Expect only partial success on the 0.3 mm parts.
- Infill: 20–100%. At this size the model is almost all walls anyway.

**Speed and cooling** (the most important part for minis)
- Print Speed: 25–30 mm/s, Outer Wall 15–20 mm/s
- Fan: 100% after the first layer
- **Minimum Layer Time: 10–15 s.** Otherwise the tip of the bow and the head melt into blobs.
- Nozzle temp: 195–200 °C (lower end of your PLA's range)

**Retraction** (Ender 3 V2 stock Bowden)
- Retraction Distance ~5 mm, Speed ~40–45 mm/s
- Combing: "Not in Skin" or "Within Infill"
- Enable **Z Hop When Retracted** (~0.2 mm) so the nozzle doesn't knock off thin parts

**Supports** (ignore the resin pre-supports)
- Support Structure: **Tree**
- Support Placement: Touching Buildplate if the pose allows it, otherwise Everywhere
- Overhang Angle: 55–60°
- Support Z Distance: about one layer height (e.g. 0.1–0.12 mm)
- Tree branch diameter: small (~2 mm), tip diameter ~0.8 mm
- Support Interface: Off, or roof only. On minis it tends to fuse to the model.

**Adhesion**
- A 25 mm base doesn't need a brim. A skirt is enough.

**Orientation**: stand it upright on its base. A slight backward tilt (10–15°) often reduces supports under the cloak and bow arm. Check it in Cura's preview.

## Batch tip

When you print all 5 at once, spread them across the bed. The nozzle moving between models gives each layer time to cool, which helps detail. If the test print comes out fine, set Minimum Layer Time a little lower for the batch. At 0.08–0.12 mm layers a batch of 5 minis like this probably takes most of a day. Check the time estimate in Cura before you commit so Saturday doesn't sneak up on you.

**Bottom line:** the body, cloak and base should come out fine with these settings. The bow string and arrow shafts will not print as designed on a 0.4 nozzle. Plan on modifying the model or adding them by hand, and print one test mini before promising 5.


## Answer B
Before we get to settings: this model, as designed, won't print cleanly on a stock Ender 3 V2 with a 0.4 mm nozzle, whatever you set in Cura. There are three problems, and it's better to find out now than at 2 a.m. the night before the game.

## ⚠️ What's working against you

| Spec | Value | FDM with 0.4 nozzle | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bow string | 0.3 mm | Narrower than one extrusion line (~0.4 mm) | ❌ Will be missing, or a stringy blob that snaps |
| Arrow shafts | 0.35 mm | Also below line width | ❌ Broken or missing, at best fuzzy |
| Cloak | 1.1 mm | About 2–3 lines | ✅ Fine |
| Base 25 mm / height 34 mm | — | Small, fits easily | ✅ Fine |
| Pre-supported | — | On MyMiniFactory, "pre-supported" almost always means **resin** supports (thin tips, meant for SLA) | ❌ Useless for FDM. Remove them and let Cura generate its own |
| 4.8★ from 6 ratings, renders only, 0 makes | — | Nobody has confirmed it prints at all, especially on FDM | ⚠️ Risky under our usual quality bar |

Scaling it up won't help. To bring the bow string to 0.4 mm you'd need roughly 135%, which makes the ranger about 46 mm tall and puts him out of scale with the rest of the party.

## What I'd actually do

1. **Get the unsupported version.** MyMiniFactory designers usually include one alongside the pre-supported file. Check the file list.
2. **Deal with the thin parts:**
   - Cut the bow string off (Meshmixer / Blender / the slicer's cut tool). After printing, replace it with a thread or thin fishing line glued at the tips. At this scale it looks better than plastic anyway.
   - For the arrows, either thicken them in Meshmixer to about 0.5–0.6 mm, or accept that they'll be stubby and fragile. Arrows in the quiver are usually fine to leave as they are.
3. **Print ONE test mini tonight, before you commit to all five.** If he comes out bad, you'll still have time for plan B.
4. **Plan B if the test fails:** a local resin printer (a friend, a makerspace, a print service). This model was designed for resin, and on resin the 0.3 mm details come out as intended.

## Cura settings (Ender 3 V2, PLA, 0.4 nozzle)

These are starting points for FDM miniatures, not verified values. Tune them on the test print.

**Quality**
- Layer Height: **0.08–0.1 mm** (0.12 if you're short on time)
- Line Width: 0.4 mm (you can try 0.35 for a bit more detail)
- Wall Generator: **Arachne** (default in Cura 5.x). It handles thin features better than Classic
- Print Thin Walls: **On**

**Walls / Infill**
- Wall Line Count: 2–3
- Infill: 15–20% (at 34 mm tall most of it will end up as walls anyway)
- Top/Bottom thickness: 0.6–0.8 mm

**Speed**
- Print Speed: **25–30 mm/s**
- Outer Wall: 15–20 mm/s
- Initial Layer: 15–20 mm/s

**Temperature / cooling**
- Nozzle: 195–205 °C (the lower end of your PLA's range)
- Bed: 55–60 °C
- Fan: 100% after the first layers
- **Minimum Layer Time: 10–15 s.** This is important on small models. Otherwise the tops of the head and arms melt.

**Retraction (Bowden)**
- Distance: ~5 mm, speed: ~40–45 mm/s. Standard values for the V2's Bowden setup (from memory, check against your stock Cura profile)
- Combing Mode: *Within Infill* or *Not in Skin*. It cuts down on stringing across the cloak

**Supports**
- Support Structure: **Tree**
- Placement: *Touching Buildplate* if the pose allows. If the arms or cloak hang over empty space, use *Everywhere*
- Overhang angle: 55–60°
- Branch diameter: minimum (~2 mm), tip ~0.8 mm
- Z Distance: 0.1–0.2 mm (a multiple of layer height). This makes the supports easier to remove from the cloak

**Adhesion**
- Brim or none. A 25 mm base holds on its own. Skirt for purging the nozzle

**Orientation:** base flat on the bed, face and bow facing the fan. Don't tilt it the way you would for resin.

## Timing

Printing all five on one plate is a good idea. Layer time goes up, cooling gets better, and there's less melting. But at 0.08–0.1 mm, five minis with supports could plausibly run somewhere around 10–20 hours (a rough estimate, look at Cura's preview). If Saturday is tomorrow, run the test mini right away and decide on resin vs FDM based on the result. If you're in a real hurry, use 0.12 mm. You'll see some layer lines, but primer and paint hide a lot of them.
