Tomorrow is outside the window. If today is the last day, a notice sent tomorrow is a late notice, and late notice is one of the few grounds an insurer can use to deny an otherwise valid claim. Fresh eyes are worth less than a notice that lands inside the deadline — the notice only has to be complete enough to identify the policy, the loss and the date; detail can follow.

```
Subject: Notice of Claim — Water Damage from Ceiling Leak, Policy [number]

To: [insurer's claims address]

Dear Claims Department,

I am giving written notice of a claim under home insurance policy
[number], held in the name of [full name], for the property at
[full address].

Date of loss: [the 3rd — month, year]
Type of loss: water damage from a ceiling leak

What happened: On [full date] I discovered water coming through the
ceiling of [room]. The water damaged [list what is affected: ceiling,
walls, flooring, furniture, personal property]. [If known: the apparent
source is (roof defect / burst supply line / the unit above).]

Steps taken: [e.g. shut off the water supply, contained the water,
moved items out of the area, engaged a plumber on (date)]. I have
photographs of the damage and am keeping records of all related
expenses. Damaged materials are being retained and are available for
inspection.

Please confirm receipt of this notice, assign a claim number and an
adjuster, and tell me what documentation you require and whether you
want to schedule an inspection. I can be reached at [phone] and
[email].

This notice is given within the notice period required by the policy.

Sincerely,
[Full name]
Policy [number]
[Address]
[Phone] | [Email]
```

Send it tonight with whatever placeholders you can fill from memory, and follow up tomorrow with photos, receipts and anything you left blank — the dated, received notice is what the window asks for, not the full file.

One check before you hit send: if your policy names a specific channel for written notice (a claims portal, a named address, a form), use that one and keep a copy; if it only says "in writing", email to the claims address is a reasonable route and gives you a timestamp.
