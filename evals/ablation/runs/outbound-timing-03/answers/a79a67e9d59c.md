Here it is. Fill the bracketed bits and it's ready to go.

```
Subject: Damage during move on 8 November 2026 — chipped marble table top (Job #[number])

Dear [Company name / contact],

I'm writing to report damage that occurred during my move today, 8 November 2026.
While the dining table was being [carried out of / loaded / carried into] the
[origin/destination] property, the marble top was chipped.

[Crew lead's name], the crew lead on site, saw it happen, and we spoke about it
at the time.

Details for your records:

- Date of damage: 8 November 2026
- Job / order number: [number]
- Addresses: [origin] to [destination]
- Item: marble-topped dining table
- Damage: chip to the [edge / corner] of the marble top, roughly [size]
- Witnessed by: [crew lead's name], crew lead

I have photographs taken today and can attach them or send them however suits
your process.

Please confirm receipt of this notice, and let me know how to file a damage
claim — any forms you need, the deadline for submitting them, and whether you
want to inspect the table in person before repair or compensation is assessed.

You can reach me on [phone] or at this address.

Regards,
[Your name]
[Phone]
```

Two things worth knowing before you hit send:

The date is on record because the body says it and because the crew lead witnessed it — not because of the send timestamp. So if you'd rather reread this with a clear head in the morning, you lose nothing; just keep the "today, 8 November 2026" wording as the date of the move. (Check that 8 November is still today where you are — it's nearly midnight.)

The one genuinely time-sensitive thing is the photos. Take them tonight, before anything gets moved, wiped or rearranged, with something in frame for scale. Flash is fine.
