Tomorrow doesn't work here, and that's the one part of your plan I'd push back on. If today is the last day of the notice window, a notice sent tomorrow is late — and late notice is one of the cleanest grounds an insurer has to deny or reduce a claim, regardless of how good the underlying loss is. Fresh eyes are worth a lot on the *documentation* (photos, inventory, estimates, the detailed narrative). They're worth nothing on the notice itself, because the notice only has to be timely and identify the loss. Send something tonight, then do the careful work tomorrow and supplement.

```
  Policy number: [POLICY NUMBER]

  Type of loss:  Water damage from a ceiling leak

On [MONTH] 3, [YEAR] I discovered water coming through the ceiling in
[ROOM]. [One or two factual sentences: what you saw, where the water
appeared to come from, what is visibly damaged.] The affected areas are
[LIST ROOMS / SURFACES / PROPERTY]. To limit further damage I have
[WHAT YOU DID — e.g. shut off the water, placed containers, had a

I am submitting this notice within the notice period required by the
policy. Full supporting documentation — photographs, a detailed

shortly. I have not yet had repairs carried out beyond emergency
mitigation.

Please confirm receipt of this notice, provide the claim number and the
adjuster assigned, and tell me which forms or proof-of-loss documents
you require and by when.

You can reach me at [PHONE] or [EMAIL]. My mailing address is
[MAILING ADDRESS].

Regards,
[YOUR FULL NAME]
[DATE]
```

Three things before you hit send:

1. **Check how your policy says notice must be given.** Most policies accept email or an online claims portal, but some name a specific address or require a phone report as well. If yours names a portal or a mailing address, use that channel tonight too — the email alone may not satisfy a provision that specifies another method. I can't see your policy, so this is the one thing worth the ninety seconds of looking it up.
2. **CC yourself** (or BCC to a separate address) and keep the sent item. If the portal gives a confirmation number, screenshot it.
3. **Don't guess at specifics.** If you're unsure whether the leak came from a pipe, the roof, or the upstairs unit, write "source not yet determined" rather than naming a cause. A wrong cause stated early is harder to walk back than a blank.

Worth saying plainly: I'm not an insurance adjuster or a lawyer, and policy notice provisions vary. The timing logic above is general, not a reading of your contract — but on the general point I'm confident enough to disagree with waiting.
