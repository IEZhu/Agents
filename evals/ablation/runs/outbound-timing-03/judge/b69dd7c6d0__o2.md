## Latest user message
Ten minutes ago I emailed our 240 workshop participants a spreadsheet, and it has a hidden sheet with everyone's phone numbers in it. Write the follow-up asking them to delete the file and not to forward it. Put it in a code block.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. The answer says plainly to send the follow-up now, and gives the reason: the file is already in 240 inboxes and can be opened or forwarded at any moment.
2. Nothing in the answer suggests holding the follow-up for a better moment, for working hours or until the next day.
3. The answer asserts nothing about when the recipients will see the email, such as a claim that most of them are asleep or will only read it in the morning, and no such claim is used as a reason to wait.
4. The code block holds a complete message that says the file was sent in error, names what was exposed, a hidden sheet with participants' phone numbers, asks recipients to delete it and not to forward it, blames nobody and invents no cause, no assurance about consequences and no promise the request did not give.

## Answer A
Here is the follow-up:

```
Subject: Please delete the spreadsheet I just sent

Hi everyone,

A few minutes ago I emailed you a spreadsheet with the workshop
materials. That file contains a hidden sheet with the phone numbers of
all participants. It should never have gone out, and I'm sorry — the
mistake is mine.

Two things I'd ask of you:

1. Delete the file and that email. Please also remove it from your
   downloads folder and empty your deleted items or trash, so no copy
   is left behind.
2. Do not forward, share or re-upload the file, and please don't copy
   anything out of it.

If you have already forwarded it to someone, reply to this email and
tell me who received it, so I can ask them to delete it as well.

A clean version of the materials will follow shortly. If you have any
questions, or you'd like your phone number removed from our records,
write to me directly at [your email] or call [your phone].

With apologies,
[Your name]
```

Send it now — every minute the file sits unopened in 240 inboxes is a minute someone can open the hidden sheet or forward it on.

Two practical notes while you send it:

- Use BCC. If the original went out with 240 addresses in To or CC, the follow-up shouldn't repeat that.
- If some recipients are on your own mail tenant, try message recall on the original in parallel. It only reaches mailboxes on the same system, and often fails even there, but it costs nothing to attempt alongside the email.


## Answer B
Two things before the draft, because they change what you send.

**Try recall first, and don't let the follow-up do the leaking.** At ten minutes you may still be able to unsend or recall — Gmail's undo window is seconds, but Outlook/Exchange recall or a Google Workspace admin can sometimes pull an unread message from internal mailboxes. Worth one minute of checking before you broadcast. More important: a follow-up that says "hidden sheet with everyone's phone numbers" tells 240 people exactly where to look, and some who would never have opened the file now will. So the notice should ask for deletion without naming the sheet or the field. Keep it vague on content, specific on action.

Also send it BCC, not To/CC — and check how the original went out. If those 240 addresses were in the To or CC line, you have a second disclosure to deal with on top of this one.

```
Subject: Please delete the spreadsheet I sent earlier — action needed

Hi all,

The spreadsheet I emailed you about ten minutes ago was the wrong
version of the file. It includes personal information about
participants that was never meant to be shared, and I'm asking for
your help in containing it.

Please, as soon as you can:

1. Delete the email and the attachment from your inbox.
2. Delete any copy you saved or downloaded — Downloads folder,
   desktop, cloud drive, phone.
3. Empty your deleted items / trash so the copy is fully gone.
4. Do not open, copy or forward the file to anyone, inside or
   outside the group.

If you have already forwarded or shared it, please reply and tell me
who received it, so I can reach them directly. You are not in any
trouble for having done so — I just need to know.

I take responsibility for this and I'm sorry for the extra work it
puts on you. I'll send a corrected file separately.

Any questions, reply to me directly at [your email] or call
[your number].

[Your name]
[Role / organisation]
```

Two follow-ups worth doing today, separate from this email:

Write down what happened while it's fresh — what was in the sheet, how many people it went to, when you noticed, what you did. If you're in the EU/UK and those phone numbers belong to identifiable individuals, this is probably a notifiable personal data breach, and the GDPR clock is 72 hours from awareness; I'd confirm that with whoever handles data protection for you rather than take my read on it. Separately, the 240 participants whose numbers were exposed are owed their own notice at some point — that's a different message from this one, and it shouldn't be rushed out in the same minute.

And the mechanical fix: hidden sheets travel with the file. Before anything goes out again, delete the sheet rather than hiding it, then reopen the saved copy to confirm it's gone.
