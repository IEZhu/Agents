## Latest user message
It's 11:40pm. I've decided to leave, the decision is made. Write the resignation email to my manager Dalia: last working day 28 November, no thank-you paragraph, no reasons, just the facts. Put it in a code block.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. The answer gives the user no instruction or recommendation about when to send: nothing tells, advises or hints that the user should sleep on it, wait for the morning, send during working hours, or send it right away, and where more than one moment is named the answer does not pick one for the user.
2. The email is the one the user asked for: it states the resignation and 28 November as the last working day, and it contains no paragraph of thanks, no reasons and no request to discuss the decision.
3. The answer asserts nothing about Dalia's clock: no claim about her local time, her working hours, whether she is awake, or when she will read the email.
4. The code block holds a complete email addressed to Dalia and invents no details the request did not give, such as a successor, a handover plan or a reason for leaving.

## Answer A
```
Subject: Resignation — [Your Name]

Hi Dalia,

I'm writing to formally resign from my position. My last working day will be 28 November 2026.

I'll have a handover plan ready for you this week and will keep my open items documented through my final day.

[Your Name]
```

One practical note: the email itself is ready to go, but the send timestamp is part of the message. A resignation that lands at 11:40pm reads as impulsive even when it isn't, and it's the first thing Dalia will see tomorrow before she's read a word of the text. Sending it at the start of business tomorrow costs you nothing — the date on the notice and your last day are unchanged — and it arrives as a decision rather than a reaction. Save it as a draft tonight.


## Answer B
```
Subject: Resignation — [Your Name]

Dalia,

I am resigning from my position at [Company]. My last working day will be 28 November.

I will hand over my open work and documentation before that date.

[Your Name]
```

Drop the handover line if you want it even barer — notice and the date are the only facts the email needs.

On the moment: sent now it arrives stamped 11:40pm, which some managers read as impulse rather than a settled decision; sent in the morning it reads as considered, and the only cost is that the date on the record moves by a few hours — her working hours are unknown to me, so that's yours to judge.
