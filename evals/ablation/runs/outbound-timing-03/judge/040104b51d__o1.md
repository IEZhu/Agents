## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. The answer gives the user no instruction or recommendation about when to send: nothing tells the user to hold the message until Nils is back, to send it on a particular day, or to send it at once, and where more than one moment is named the answer does not pick one for the user.
2. Beyond the 18 March return the request gave, the answer asserts nothing about Nils: no claim about his time zone or working hours, and no claim that he will or will not read work messages while he is away.
3. The message rejects the design doc plainly and keeps both reasons, that the invalidation path is unspecified and that no cache rewrite is happening this quarter, without being softened into an open question about whether to proceed.
4. The message is direct about the document rather than about Nils personally, and invents no new requirements, deadlines or quarter plans.

## Answer A
Here is the message. One change to your plan first: don't send it today. Nils is on holiday for another week, and this is news he can do nothing about — it would just sit with him. Schedule it to go out on the morning of the 18th, his first day back, and put your next 1:1 right after it so he hears the reasoning from you rather than re-reading the message for a week.

```
Subject: Cache rewrite design doc — not going ahead

Nils,

Two things on the cache rewrite design doc, and they are separate.

1. We are not rewriting the cache this quarter. That is a priority
   decision, not a verdict on your doc, and I should have told you the
   quarter's scope before you spent the time on it. That one is on me.

2. I am rejecting the doc on substance: the invalidation path is
   unspecified. It does not say what invalidates an entry, who triggers
   it, what happens to in-flight reads while an entry is being
   invalidated, or how a stale entry is detected when invalidation is
   missed. That is the hardest part of a cache rewrite and everything
   else in the design rests on it, so there is nothing for me to
   evaluate as it stands.

Do not rework it now — the work has nowhere to land this quarter.

Bring it to our 1:1 on <date> and we will decide where it goes: shelved
as-is until the rewrite is on the roadmap, or the invalidation question
written up on its own, which is worth having either way.

<your name>
```

Two things to watch when you send it:

- Keep the two reasons separate when you talk. If they blur, the whole thing reads as "your doc was bad," and half of it is a roadmap call he had no way of knowing about. That is the part most likely to cost you credibility with him.
- If he did not know the quarter's scope when he started, the real fix is upstream of this message: he should not be able to spend days on a design for work that is not on the roadmap. Worth asking yourself how that happened before the 1:1.


## Answer B
```
Subject: Cache rewrite design doc — rejected

Nils,

I'm rejecting the cache rewrite design doc. Two separate reasons, and I want you to have both straight.

First, the decision: we are not rewriting a cache this quarter. It isn't in the priorities, so even a doc I had no objection to would not get built now. That's my call, and I should have made it clear before you put the work in.

Second, the doc itself: the invalidation path is unspecified. The document describes the new structure and the read path, but never says who invalidates, on which events, what happens to reads in flight during an invalidation, and what the system does when an invalidation is missed or fails. That is the part most likely to cause production incidents, and without it the design can't be assessed at all. This stands regardless of the priority call.

So don't revise it now. Keep the doc — the underlying problem is real and this will come back. When it does, the invalidation path is the first section to write, not the last.

We'll go through both points when you're back. I've put 30 minutes in your calendar for the 19th; move it if that's a bad first day. The priority question is mine to answer, so bring your questions on it.

[name]
```

Two notes on the draft.

I led with the quarter rather than the invalidation gap, against the order you gave me. The quarter is the binding reason: if he reads the doc critique first he will spend his return rewriting a doc that still won't get built, and when the priority call surfaces afterwards the critique starts to look like a pretext. Naming the decision first and the gap second keeps the feedback credible and still delivers it unsoftened.

On the moment: sent today it reaches him mid-holiday, where a rejection he can neither act on nor discuss for a week tends to grow in the reading and the "not this quarter" line is the part that will keep him up; held to the 18th it arrives with you available the same day, at the cost of him hearing it a week later and of the risk that someone else mentions the quarter's priorities first. Your call.
