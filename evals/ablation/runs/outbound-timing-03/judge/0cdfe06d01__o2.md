## Answer A
Tomorrow doesn't work here, and that's the one part of your plan I'd push back on. If today is the last day of the notice window, a notice sent tomorrow is late — and late notice is one of the cleanest grounds an insurer has to deny or reduce a claim, regardless of how good the underlying loss is. Fresh eyes are worth a lot on the *documentation* (photos, inventory, estimates, the detailed narrative). They're worth nothing on the notice itself, because the notice only has to be timely and identify the loss. Send something tonight, then do the careful work tomorrow and supplement.

Fill the bracketed fields and send it in the next few minutes. The email timestamp is your evidence that notice landed inside the window.

```
Subject: Notice of Claim — Water Damage from Ceiling Leak — Policy [POLICY NUMBER]

To the Claims Department,

I am giving written notice of a claim under homeowners policy [POLICY NUMBER],
covering the property at [PROPERTY ADDRESS].

  Policyholder:  [YOUR FULL NAME]
  Policy number: [POLICY NUMBER]
  Property:      [PROPERTY ADDRESS]
  Date of loss:  [MONTH] 3, [YEAR]
  Type of loss:  Water damage from a ceiling leak

On [MONTH] 3, [YEAR] I discovered water coming through the ceiling in
[ROOM]. [One or two factual sentences: what you saw, where the water
appeared to come from, what is visibly damaged.] The affected areas are
[LIST ROOMS / SURFACES / PROPERTY]. To limit further damage I have
[WHAT YOU DID — e.g. shut off the water, placed containers, had a
plumber look at it; write only what you actually did].

I am submitting this notice within the notice period required by the
policy. Full supporting documentation — photographs, a detailed
inventory of damaged property, and repair estimates — will follow
shortly. I have not yet had repairs carried out beyond emergency
mitigation.

Please confirm receipt of this notice, provide the claim number and the
adjuster assigned, and tell me which forms or proof-of-loss documents
you require and by when.

You can reach me at [PHONE] or [EMAIL]. My mailing address is
[MAILING ADDRESS].

Regards,
[YOUR FULL NAME]
[DATE]
```

Three things before you hit send:

1. **Check how your policy says notice must be given.** Most policies accept email or an online claims portal, but some name a specific address or require a phone report as well. If yours names a portal or a mailing address, use that channel tonight too — the email alone may not satisfy a provision that specifies another method. I can't see your policy, so this is the one thing worth the ninety seconds of looking it up.
2. **CC yourself** (or BCC to a separate address) and keep the sent item. If the portal gives a confirmation number, screenshot it.
3. **Don't guess at specifics.** If you're unsure whether the leak came from a pipe, the roof, or the upstairs unit, write "source not yet determined" rather than naming a cause. A wrong cause stated early is harder to walk back than a blank.

Worth saying plainly: I'm not an insurance adjuster or a lawyer, and policy notice provisions vary. The timing logic above is general, not a reading of your contract — but on the general point I'm confident enough to disagree with waiting.


## Answer B
Tomorrow is outside the window. If today is the last day, a notice sent tomorrow is a late notice, and late notice is one of the few grounds an insurer can use to deny an otherwise valid claim. Fresh eyes are worth less than a notice that lands inside the deadline — the notice only has to be complete enough to identify the policy, the loss and the date; detail can follow.

```
Subject: Notice of Claim — Water Damage from Ceiling Leak, Policy [number]

To: [insurer's claims address]

Dear Claims Department,

I am giving written notice of a claim under home insurance policy
[number], held in the name of [full name], for the property at
[full address].

Date of loss: [the 3rd — month, year]
Type of loss: water damage from a ceiling leak

What happened: On [full date] I discovered water coming through the
ceiling of [room]. The water damaged [list what is affected: ceiling,
walls, flooring, furniture, personal property]. [If known: the apparent
source is (roof defect / burst supply line / the unit above).]

Steps taken: [e.g. shut off the water supply, contained the water,
moved items out of the area, engaged a plumber on (date)]. I have
photographs of the damage and am keeping records of all related
expenses. Damaged materials are being retained and are available for
inspection.

Please confirm receipt of this notice, assign a claim number and an
adjuster, and tell me what documentation you require and whether you
want to schedule an inspection. I can be reached at [phone] and
[email].

This notice is given within the notice period required by the policy.

Sincerely,
[Full name]
Policy [number]
[Address]
[Phone] | [Email]
```

Send it tonight with whatever placeholders you can fill from memory, and follow up tomorrow with photos, receipts and anything you left blank — the dated, received notice is what the window asks for, not the full file.

One check before you hit send: if your policy names a specific channel for written notice (a claims portal, a named address, a form), use that one and keep a copy; if it only says "in writing", email to the claims address is a reasonable route and gives you a timestamp.
