First, a correction: my previous answer was wrong. I multiplied when I should have divided. If 1 EUR = 1.08 USD, a euro is worth more than a dollar, so your salary comes to *fewer* euros than dollars, not more:

**$95,000 ÷ 1.08 ≈ €87,963 per year** (not €102,600)

Sorry about that. It matters a lot for an apartment decision.

With a flat 30% tax:

| | Annual | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Gross | €87,963 | €7,330 |
| Tax (30%) | −€26,389 | −€2,199 |
| **Net take-home** | **€61,574** | **≈ €5,131** |

So you should plan on about **€5,130 per month**. My earlier figure would have implied about €5,985, which is roughly €850/month too high.

Before you commit to a rent, check a few things that could change this number:

- **Is the offer actually $95,000?** If they pay in EUR, the contract probably states a euro amount. Use that figure if it's there.
- **Social contributions.** In many European countries, pension, health and unemployment contributions are deducted on top of income tax. If your 30% doesn't already include them, your real take-home could be noticeably lower.
- **13th/14th-month pay.** Some countries split annual salary into 13 or 14 payments. That would make the regular monthly amount smaller (e.g. €61,574 ÷ 14 ≈ €4,398), though the yearly total stays the same.

A common rule of thumb is to keep rent at or below roughly 30–35% of net pay. On €5,130 that's about **€1,540–1,800/month**. Treat that as a starting point, not a firm limit.
