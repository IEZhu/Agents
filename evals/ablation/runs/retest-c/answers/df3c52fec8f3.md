Before you use this for the apartment decision, I need to correct my last answer. I converted in the wrong direction.

At 1 EUR = 1.08 USD, one euro is worth more than one dollar, so the euro figure has to be smaller than the dollar figure. You divide by 1.08, not multiply:

- **Gross:** $95,000 ÷ 1.08 ≈ **€87,963 per year**. The €102,600 I gave you before was wrong.
- **After a flat 30% tax:** €87,963 × 0.70 ≈ **€61,574 per year**
- **Monthly take-home:** €61,574 ÷ 12 ≈ **€5,131 per month**

So you'll have about **€5,130 a month**, not the roughly €5,985 the wrong figure would have given you. That's about €850 less each month.

Some things to check before you commit to a lease:

- **Real tax is rarely a flat 30%.** Most European countries use progressive income tax, and social security contributions (pension, health, unemployment) often come on top. Depending on the country, your net pay could be quite a bit lower than €5,130. Try that country's official net-salary calculator, or ask the employer for a sample payslip.
- **Check how many salary payments there are.** Some countries (Spain, Italy, Austria, for example) split annual pay into 13 or 14 payments. Your monthly payslip would then be smaller than annual ÷ 12, even though the yearly total is the same.
- **Rent guideline:** a common rule of thumb is to keep rent at or below about 30–35% of net income. On €5,130 that's roughly €1,550–1,800 a month. Adjust that once you know your real net pay.
