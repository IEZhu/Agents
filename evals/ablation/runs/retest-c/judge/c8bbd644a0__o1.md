## Latest user message
Quick sanity check before a design review. Our /feed endpoint fans out to 5 backend services in parallel and waits for all of them before responding. Each backend has p99 latency of 100ms (measured independently). So the /feed p99 should also be about 100ms plus a bit of aggregation overhead, right? We're planning to grow the fan-out to 40 shards next quarter, and I want to put the expected latency numbers on a slide.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Rejects the premise that the endpoint p99 stays around 100ms
2. Computes that with 5 independent calls about 4.9% of requests (1 - 0.99^5) wait on at least one call slower than 100ms, and about 33% with 40 calls (1 - 0.99^40)
3. Notes the independence assumption (correlated slowness changes the numbers)
4. Suggests at least one concrete mitigation relevant to fan-out tail latency, such as hedged or backup requests, timeouts with partial results, or reducing the fan-out

## Answer A
**No. Please don't put that number on the slide.** When a request waits for *all* of its backends, its latency is the **maximum** of their latencies, not a typical one. The tail of a maximum grows quickly with fan-out. So your /feed p99 is already well above 100ms at 5 backends, and at 40 shards it gets a lot worse.

## The math (assuming backend latencies are independent)

A backend's p99 of 100ms means each call has a 1% chance of taking longer than 100ms. A /feed request stays under 100ms only if **every** backend does:

| Fan-out | P(all backends < 100ms) | Share of /feed requests over 100ms | Backend percentile that sets /feed p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.99 | 1% | p99 |
| 5 | 0.99^5 ≈ 0.951 | **~4.9%** | p99.8 |
| 40 | 0.99^40 ≈ 0.669 | **~33%** | p99.975 |

What this means:
- **Today (5 backends):** 100ms is about your /feed **p95**, not its p99. Your real p99 is roughly the backends' **p99.8** latency, plus aggregation overhead.
- **At 40 shards:** about **1 in 3** /feed requests will go over 100ms. That puts 100ms near your /feed **p67**. Your p99 becomes the shards' **p99.975** latency, which sits in the far tail: GC pauses, retransmits, cold caches, noisy neighbours. It can easily be several times the p99.

The last column comes from solving q^N = 0.99, which gives q = 0.99^(1/N).

## Why I can't give you a single number yet

The figures above tell you **which backend percentile** matters. The actual milliseconds depend on how heavy each backend's tail is beyond p99, and you haven't measured that yet. Two caveats also apply:
- **Correlation.** If the backends share a dependency (same DB, same hosts, same GC timing), slow calls cluster together. That changes the numbers in either direction. Independence is a reasonable baseline, not a guarantee.
- **The median rises too.** Even p50 of a max over 40 draws lands around each shard's ~p98 (0.5^(1/40) ≈ 0.983). Typical latency goes up as well, not only the tail.

## What to put on the slide instead

1. **Measure the right percentiles.** Pull p99.8 and p99.975 from your existing backend latency histograms. Make sure you have enough samples: p99.975 is 1 in 4,000 calls, so you need tens of thousands per window for a stable value.
2. **Or simulate it, which is better.** Resample real per-backend latencies from production traces, take the max across 5 or 40 draws per simulated request, and read the p50/p99 of that. Replaying real traces keeps the correlation the independence math ignores.
3. **Show the chart.** Plot /feed p99 against fan-out (1, 5, 10, 20, 40). It explains the problem to reviewers faster than any sentence.

## Mitigations to raise in the review

The 40-shard plan probably needs at least one of these:

| Technique | What it buys | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Hedged requests** (send a backup request after ~p95 delay) | Cuts the tail sharply for a few % extra load | Extra load; reads must be idempotent |
| **Per-shard deadline + partial results** | Caps latency; you return the feed without the slow shards | Result quality and completeness drop; product has to accept that |
| **Reduce fan-out** (bigger shards, a routing index, two-level aggregation) | Fixes the root cause | Re-sharding work |
| **Tighten backend tails** (GC tuning, queue limits, isolation) | Helps every percentile | Ongoing engineering effort |

Google's "The Tail at Scale" (Dean & Barroso, CACM 2013) is the standard reference for this effect and these mitigations. I'm recalling it from memory, so check the citation before putting it on a slide.


## Answer B
**No. A fan-out's p99 is set by the slowest of its N calls, not by a typical one.** When you wait for every backend, the request is slow if *any* backend is slow, so the fan-out p99 lands well above 100ms, and the gap grows quickly as N goes up. Please don't put "~100ms" on the slide.

### The math (assuming backend latencies are independent)

Each backend finishes under 100ms 99% of the time. For a request to finish under 100ms, all N calls must finish under 100ms:

- **P(request ≤ 100ms) = 0.99^N**

| Fan-out N | P(all ≤ 100ms) | Requests slower than a backend's p99 | Backend percentile that sets /feed p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 99.0% | 1% | p99 |
| 5 (today) | 95.1% | **~4.9%** | **p99.8** |
| 40 (next quarter) | 66.9% | **~33%** | **p99.975** |

How to read it:

- **At 5 backends**, about 1 in 20 requests already goes past 100ms. So 100ms is roughly your /feed **p95**, not its p99. The real /feed p99 is the backends' **p99.8** latency (from 0.99^(1/5) ≈ 0.998).
- **At 40 shards**, about 1 in 3 requests goes past 100ms. The /feed p99 becomes the backends' **p99.975**, i.e. a 1-in-4,000 event on each shard. Even the /feed **median** sits at about the backends' **p98.3** (from 0.5^(1/40)), so your *typical* request would be about as slow as today's *tail*.

### What the slide can honestly say

You can't turn these into milliseconds yet, because you haven't measured the backends that far into the tail. Latency distributions usually have long tails, so p99.975 could plausibly be 2–10× p99 (a guess; GC pauses, retries and queueing tend to dominate out there). Two ways to get real numbers:

1. **Measure /feed end-to-end** today. This is the only number that reflects the actual correlation between backends.
2. **Simulate 40 shards** with a Monte Carlo run over real per-shard latency logs: draw 40 samples, take the max, repeat about 1M times, and read off p50/p99. You need enough data to estimate p99.975, which means well over 4,000 samples per shard, and ideally 100k+.

### Caveats on the independence assumption

- **Positive correlation** (shared network, the same GC timing, a hot key hitting every shard) makes things *less* bad than the table, because slow calls cluster in the same requests.
- **Load under fan-out** usually makes things *worse*. Your "measured independently" p99s may not hold when every /feed request hits all 40 shards at once and multiplies backend QPS by 8×. Watch for queueing.

The table's direction is safe to present. The exact percentages are an idealized model.

### Options to raise in the review (from Dean & Barroso, "The Tail at Scale")

| Mitigation | What it buys | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Hedged requests** (send a duplicate after ~p95 and take the first reply) | Large cut to the tail for a few % of extra load | Backends must be idempotent; adds load |
| **Partial results + per-shard deadline** | Hard upper bound on /feed latency | Feed is sometimes incomplete; needs a product decision |
| **Reduce effective fan-out** (fewer, larger shards; replicas; two-level aggregation) | Lowers N directly | Rebalancing and capacity work |
| **Trim the backend tail** (GC tuning, isolating background work) | Raises every row of the table | Engineering time per service |

For 40 shards, I'd propose a per-shard deadline with partial results as the baseline, plus hedging, and get a real end-to-end measurement before committing to an SLA number.
