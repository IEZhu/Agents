## Latest user message
PostgreSQL 15, one primary, one streaming hot-standby replica used for reporting. Every night around 02:10 replay lag on the replica climbs to ~300 seconds, then recovers by ~02:40. Security gives me a 15-minute read-only session on both hosts tonight starting 02:05 and I won't get another one this month. What should I look at?

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. An early step separates where the lag is: compares write/flush lag against replay lag (e.g. write_lag, flush_lag, replay_lag in pg_stat_replication on the primary) to tell WAL shipping problems from replay problems
2. Steps branch on results (e.g. replay lag only leads to replica-side long-running queries and replay conflicts; shipping lag leads to network or WAL volume on the primary) instead of a flat list
3. Ties the fixed 02:10 timing to scheduled work on either host (cron jobs, backups or dumps, VACUUM or bulk loads) and includes checking what runs then
4. States what finding means the window has done its job (e.g. the lag type plus the job or query that coincides with it), so the time is not spent on open-ended exploration

## Answer A
**Bottom line:** you get one look, so use it to answer a single question: **is WAL arriving late, or is it arriving on time and replay is stalled?** If replay is stalled, find out whether it's *blocked* (a recovery conflict with a reporting query) or *slow* (a nightly write burst, with the single-threaded startup process limited by CPU or I/O). Have everything scripted before 02:05, sample repeatedly from about 02:05 to 02:19, and record your terminal on your own side. There's no time to improvise during the window.

The 300 s number is itself a clue. If `max_standby_streaming_delay` (or `max_standby_archive_delay`) on the replica is set to `5min`, the lag plateau very likely comes from recovery conflicts: replay waits for a reporting query, up to that limit, and then cancels the query. Check that setting first.

## Before tonight (no access to the hosts needed)

- **Privileges:** the login needs `pg_monitor` (or at least `pg_read_all_stats`). Without it, other users' queries show as `<insufficient privilege>` and the session is wasted. Confirm this today.
- **Scheduler inventory:** find out what starts between 01:55 and 02:10 on either host or against the primary. Look at ETL/batch jobs, `pg_dump`, `REINDEX`/`VACUUM FULL`/`CLUSTER` jobs, partition maintenance (`DROP`/`TRUNCATE`/`DETACH`), backups or VM/disk snapshots of the replica, and reporting jobs that begin at 02:00. Your job scheduler or config repo can usually answer this without touching the hosts.
- **Test the script on staging**, so a typo doesn't cost you three minutes.
- **Recording:** a read-only session probably means you can't write files on the hosts. Run `script`/terminal logging on your own workstation or jump host, or run `psql` remotely if the network allows it.

## 02:05–02:07: settings and baseline (once)

**Replica:**

```sql
-- Conflict/delay settings: the likely explanation for a flat ~300 s ceiling
SELECT name, setting, unit FROM pg_settings
WHERE name IN ('max_standby_streaming_delay','max_standby_archive_delay',
               'hot_standby_feedback','recovery_min_apply_delay',
               'recovery_prefetch','maintenance_io_concurrency',
               'wal_decode_buffer_size','shared_buffers',
               'log_recovery_conflict_waits','deadlock_timeout');

-- Cumulative conflict cancellations; take a second snapshot at the end to compute the delta
SELECT datname, confl_snapshot, confl_lock, confl_bufferpin,
       confl_tablespace, confl_deadlock
FROM pg_stat_database_conflicts;
```

**Primary:**
```sql
SELECT name, setting, unit FROM pg_settings
WHERE name IN ('wal_compression','full_page_writes','checkpoint_timeout',
               'max_wal_size','wal_level');

-- WAL volume counters (PG14+); diff against the final snapshot
SELECT now(), pg_current_wal_lsn(), * FROM pg_stat_wal;

-- If pg_stat_statements is installed: baseline of the top WAL producers (wal_bytes is PG13+)
SELECT queryid, calls, wal_bytes, wal_fpi, left(query,120)
FROM pg_stat_statements ORDER BY wal_bytes DESC LIMIT 15;
```

## 02:07–02:19: sampling loop (every 5–10 s, in `psql` with `\watch 5`)

**Primary: where does the lag sit?**
```sql
SELECT now(), application_name, state,
       write_lag, flush_lag, replay_lag,
       pg_wal_lsn_diff(pg_current_wal_lsn(), sent_lsn)   AS unsent_bytes,
       pg_wal_lsn_diff(sent_lsn, flush_lsn)              AS in_flight_bytes,
       pg_wal_lsn_diff(flush_lsn, replay_lsn)            AS unreplayed_bytes
FROM pg_stat_replication;
```
- If `write_lag`/`flush_lag` stay small while `replay_lag` climbs, the problem is replay on the replica. That's the most likely case.
- If `write_lag`/`flush_lag` climb too, the problem is the network or the replica's disk writes. Look at bandwidth and `iostat` on the replica, and at what else is saturating the link, such as a backup copy.

**Replica: what is the startup process doing?**
```sql
SELECT now(),
       now() - pg_last_xact_replay_timestamp()                            AS replay_age,
       pg_wal_lsn_diff(pg_last_wal_receive_lsn(), pg_last_wal_replay_lsn()) AS backlog_bytes,
       pg_get_wal_replay_pause_state()                                    AS pause_state;

SELECT pid, backend_type, wait_event_type, wait_event
FROM pg_stat_activity WHERE backend_type = 'startup';
```
How to read the startup process's wait event:
- **Lock, `BufferPin`, or a `RecoveryConflict*` event:** replay is blocked by a query. That's the conflict hypothesis.
- **`DataFileRead`/I/O events:** replay is I/O-bound.
- **NULL (running on CPU):** replay is CPU-bound. The fix then lives on the write side.

I'm recalling the exact event names from memory. Treat any non-NULL, non-I/O event as "waiting on something."

**Replica: which reporting queries are running, and for how long?**
```sql
SELECT pid, usename, application_name, state, backend_xmin,
       now() - xact_start AS xact_age, wait_event_type, wait_event,
       left(query, 150) AS query
FROM pg_stat_activity
WHERE backend_type = 'client backend' AND state <> 'idle'
ORDER BY xact_start;
```
A reporting transaction that starts around 02:00 and runs until about 02:10–02:40 is your prime suspect.

**Primary: what is writing?**
```sql
SELECT pid, usename, application_name, backend_type, state,
       now() - xact_start AS xact_age, wait_event_type, wait_event,
       left(query, 150) AS query
FROM pg_stat_activity
WHERE state <> 'idle' AND backend_type IN ('client backend','autovacuum worker')
ORDER BY xact_start;

-- Operations that generate heavy WAL, or AccessExclusiveLocks that are replayed on the standby
SELECT * FROM pg_stat_progress_vacuum;
SELECT * FROM pg_stat_progress_create_index;
SELECT * FROM pg_stat_progress_cluster;
SELECT l.pid, l.relation::regclass, l.mode, a.query
FROM pg_locks l JOIN pg_stat_activity a USING (pid)
WHERE l.mode = 'AccessExclusiveLock' AND l.locktype = 'relation';
```
`AccessExclusiveLock`s taken on the primary (`TRUNCATE`, `DROP`, `ALTER`, `VACUUM` truncating a table's tail) are replayed on the standby. A reporting query that touches that table blocks replay until it finishes or until `max_standby_streaming_delay` expires.

**OS, both hosts (a second terminal, running continuously):**
```bash
iostat -x 5                       # replica: %util / await on the data volume
top -H -p <startup_pid>           # replica: startup process near 100% CPU means apply-bound
vmstat 5
sar -n DEV 5                      # only if sysstat is installed; checks whether the network is saturated
crontab -l; systemctl list-timers # if you couldn't inventory schedules beforehand
```

## 02:17–02:20: final snapshots and logs

- Repeat `pg_stat_database_conflicts` (replica), `pg_stat_wal`, and the top-15 `pg_stat_statements` query (primary), and compute the deltas. The WAL volume divided by the elapsed time gives you the generation rate during the burst.
- Grep the replica's server log for last night:
  - `canceling statement due to conflict with recovery`
  - `recovery still waiting after` (only logged if `log_recovery_conflict_waits = on`; PG14+)
- Grep the primary's log for checkpoint lines around 02:00 (if `log_checkpoints` is on, which is the default since PG15) and for autovacuum or batch-job entries.
- If `recovery_prefetch` is enabled, check `SELECT * FROM pg_stat_recovery_prefetch;` (new in PG15) to see whether prefetching helps or is being skipped.

## How to read the results

| What you see | Likely cause | Fix (later, through change control) |
|---|---|---|
| Lag flattens near `max_standby_streaming_delay`; startup waits on Lock/BufferPin/conflict; a long reporting query is running; `confl_*` counters rise | Recovery conflict between the reporting query and replayed cleanup or locks | Move or split the 02:00 report. Consider `hot_standby_feedback = on` (trade-off: bloat on the primary while long queries run). Stop `TRUNCATE`/`DROP` during reporting hours. Or give reporting its own replica that tolerates delay. |
| Startup process at about 100% CPU, no conflict waits, a big jump in WAL volume or full-page images from a nightly job | A write burst that single-threaded replay can't keep up with | Throttle or batch the job, turn on `wal_compression`, avoid a forced checkpoint at job start (it causes a burst of full-page images), and prefer `pg_repack`/partition swaps over `VACUUM FULL`/`REINDEX` rewrites. |
| Startup process waiting on I/O, replica disk `%util` near 100% | The replica's storage is saturated, possibly by a backup or snapshot of the replica at the same time | Move the backup, get faster storage, raise `maintenance_io_concurrency` so `recovery_prefetch` is more effective |
| `write_lag`/`flush_lag` also high | Network or replica write path | Look at link saturation, and at a backup copying over the same interface |

Two caveats. First, this is all diagnosis. Nothing here changes settings, which fits a read-only session. Second, every fix in the table (especially `hot_standby_feedback` and any change to the delay settings) should be tried on staging first and measured against tonight's numbers before it goes to production.


## Answer B
**Short answer:** use the 15 minutes to separate three causes, and have every command scripted before 02:05. The three causes are:

1. **Replay blocked:** the startup process is waiting on a recovery conflict with a reporting query.
2. **Replay too slow:** a nightly write burst on the primary produces WAL faster than the single-threaded replay can apply it.
3. **Metric artefact:** the lag number is `now() - pg_last_xact_replay_timestamp()` while the primary is committing almost nothing.

The window covers the onset (02:10) and about 10 minutes of the climb. That's enough to tell the three apart if you're sampling from the first second.

A ~300 s ceiling followed by recovery is suspicious. `max_standby_streaming_delay = 5min` would produce exactly that: replay waits up to 300 s for a conflicting query, then cancels it and catches up. This is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis. Check it first.

**Primary: is WAL reaching the replica, and where is it stuck?**

**Primary: what starts at 02:10?**

```sql
WHERE state <> 'idle' AND pid <> pg_backend_pid()
```

**OS level on the replica (02:08 onward):**
- `watch -n5 'ps -o pid,pcpu,stat,wchan:20,args -C postgres | grep -E "startup|walreceiver"'`. The startup process title shows `recovering <WAL segment>`. If the segment name stops changing, replay is stalled. If it changes but slowly, replay is throughput-bound.
- `iostat -x 5` and `top -p <startup pid>`. Startup near 100% CPU means CPU-bound replay. High `%util` or await means IO-bound. Also check whether reporting queries or a backup are hammering the same disk.

## Previous nights' logs (while the samplers run)

On both hosts, grep the last few days' logs around 02:00–02:45 for:
- `conflict with recovery`
- `recovery still waiting` (this is only logged if `log_recovery_conflict_waits` is on; it defaults to off)
- `checkpoint starting` / `checkpoint complete`
- `automatic vacuum` / `automatic analyze`
- any long `duration:` lines

Checkpoint and autovacuum logging are on by default in PG15 as I recall (`log_checkpoints=on`, `log_autovacuum_min_duration=10min`). Confirm with the settings query above.

## How to read the result

| What you see | Likely cause | Direction of fix |
|---|---|---|
| `replay_lsn` frozen while receive LSN advances; startup waiting on `Lock` or a `RecoveryConflict*` wait event; a long reporting query is running; `confl_*` counters rise around 02:15; `max_standby_streaming_delay` ≈ 300 s | **Recovery conflict.** The trigger is usually an AccessExclusiveLock on the primary (DDL, TRUNCATE, partition maintenance, VACUUM truncating the table tail), or vacuum cleanup under a long snapshot. | Move either the primary job or the report. For snapshot conflicts, `hot_standby_feedback=on`, at the cost of bloat on the primary. For lock conflicts from vacuum truncation, the `vacuum_truncate=false` table option. Or a dedicated reporting replica. |
| Replay advancing but slower than primary WAL generation; `wal_bytes`/`wal_fpi` jump at 02:10; startup CPU- or IO-bound | **WAL burst exceeds single-threaded apply.** Full-page images after a checkpoint, a bulk load or a rewrite. | Throttle or split the job, enable `wal_compression`, space checkpoints further apart, keep `recovery_prefetch` on, reduce IO contention on the replica at that hour. |
| `write_lag`/`flush_lag` grow too, not just `replay_lag` | **Network, WAL receiver or replica disk-write bottleneck.** | Check `pg_stat_wal_receiver`, NIC throughput, disk-write latency on the replica. |
| `unapplied_bytes` ≈ 0 but "lag" still climbs | **Artefact.** Primary is idle, or one long transaction means no commits to replay. | Nothing to fix in the database; alert on LSN byte lag instead. |

Two caveats:
- The wait-event names are from memory, not checked against the PG15 docs. Whatever the exact name, the pattern that matters is `Lock` or `RecoveryConflict…` versus IO waits versus none.
- Everything above is a read. Any fix (settings, `hot_standby_feedback`, rescheduling) needs a separate change window: test on staging first, and have a current backup before any config or schema change on the primary.
