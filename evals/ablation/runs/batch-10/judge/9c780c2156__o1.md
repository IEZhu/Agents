## Latest user message
Our security team is tightening the PIN policy for our card app and I need to report how many 4-digit PINs (0000-9999, leading zeros allowed) will still be allowed. A PIN is rejected if ANY of these hold:

1. Two adjacent digits are equal (e.g. 1127, 4552).
2. Any single digit appears more than twice anywhere in the PIN.
3. It's a straight run up or down by 1 (0123, 1234 ... 6789, and 9876 ... 3210; no wrap-around, so 7890 is fine).
4. It has the form ABAB (e.g. 3737).
5. It has the form ABBA (e.g. 2552).
6. The first and last digits are the same (e.g. 4914).

How many PINs are allowed? The CISO wants the number plus a one-line justification for each rule showing how much it removes.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Final answer is 6466 allowed PINs.
2. States that rule 1 alone leaves 7290 PINs (10 * 9^3).
3. Recognises that rules 2 and 5 remove nothing additional because every PIN they catch already has two equal adjacent digits, with a brief reason for each.
4. Counts the additional removals correctly as 14 for rule 3, 90 for rule 4 (A != B) and 720 for rule 6 (first = last among PINs with no equal adjacent digits, 10*9*8), and notes these three sets do not overlap.

## Answer A
**6,466 PINs are allowed** out of 10,000, so the policy rejects 3,534 (35.34%).

Two of the six rules are redundant: rules 2 and 5 reject nothing that rule 1 has not already rejected.

## Removal by rule (applied in order, each counts only PINs not already rejected)

| Rule | Removes | Remaining | One-line justification |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Adjacent equal digits | 2,710 | 7,290 | PINs with no equal neighbours: 10 × 9 × 9 × 9 = 7,290 (each digit only has to differ from the one before it). |
| 2. A digit appears more than twice | 0 | 7,290 | A digit used 3+ times in 4 slots must sit in two neighbouring slots, because at most 2 of the 4 positions can be non-adjacent. Rule 1 already caught all of these. |
| 3. Straight run up/down | 14 | 7,276 | 7 ascending (0123…6789) + 7 descending (9876…3210). All have distinct digits, so rule 1 or 2 caught none of them. |
| 4. ABAB | 90 | 7,186 | 10 choices of A × 9 choices of B≠A. None are runs, and none have equal neighbours. |
| 5. ABBA | 0 | 7,186 | The "BB" in the middle is an adjacent repeat, so rule 1 already caught all of them. |
| 6. First digit = last digit | 720 | **6,466** | With no equal neighbours and d1 = d4: 10 (d1) × 9 (d2 ≠ d1) × 8 (d3 ≠ d2, d1) = 720. ABAB and runs never have d1 = d4, so none of these 720 were already counted. |

Total removed: 2,710 + 0 + 14 + 90 + 0 + 720 = 3,534, and 10,000 − 3,534 = **6,466**.

If the CISO wants each rule's **standalone** impact (as if it were the only rule), the numbers are different because the rules overlap:

| Rule | Standalone removals | Calculation |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 2,710 | 10,000 − 10·9³ |
| 2 | 370 | exactly 3 of one digit: C(4,3)·10·9 = 360, plus 10 PINs like 0000 |
| 3 | 14 | 7 up + 7 down |
| 4 | 90 | A ≠ B (100 if AAAA counts, but rule 1 removes those anyway) |
| 5 | 90 | A ≠ B |
| 6 | 1,000 | 10 × 10 × 10 × 1 |

These add up to more than 3,534 because many PINs break several rules at once. Use the first table for the report.

## Independent check

Together, rules 1 and 6 require d1≠d2, d2≠d3, d3≠d4 and d4≠d1, which means neighbours around a 4-cycle must differ. I counted these PINs by splitting on d3:

- d3 = d1: 10 × 9 × 1 × 9 (d4 ≠ d1) = 810
- d3 ≠ d1: 10 × 9 × 8 × 8 (d4 ≠ d3, d1) = 5,760
- Total = 6,570. This matches the formula for colouring a 4-cycle, (k−1)⁴ + (k−1) = 9⁴ + 9 = 6,570.

Next, subtract the 90 ABAB PINs (all are in the d3 = d1 group) and the 14 runs (all are in the d3 ≠ d1 group, and none are ABAB): 6,570 − 90 − 14 = **6,466**. ✓

**Recommendation:** Drop rules 2 and 5 from the written policy or mark them as covered by rule 1. They reject no additional PINs.


## Answer B
**6,466 PINs are allowed** (64.66% of the 10,000 possible).

How much a rule "removes" depends on the order you apply them, because the rules overlap. Below, each rule is applied in turn and counts only the PINs that the earlier rules have not already rejected. The last column shows how many PINs the rule would reject if it were the only rule, for comparison.

| # | Rule | Removed (in order) | Remaining | If it were the only rule |
|---|------|-------------------:|----------:|-------------------------:|
| – | All PINs 0000–9999 | – | 10,000 | – |
| 1 | Adjacent digits equal | 2,710 | 7,290 | 2,710 |
| 2 | Any digit used more than twice | 0 | 7,290 | 370 |
| 3 | Straight run up or down | 14 | 7,276 | 14 |
| 4 | ABAB | 90 | 7,186 | 90 (A≠B) |
| 5 | ABBA | 0 | 7,186 | 90 (A≠B) |
| 6 | First digit = last digit | 720 | **6,466** | 1,000 |

**One-line justifications:**

1. **Adjacent equal, removes 2,710:** PINs with no equal neighbours number 10 × 9 × 9 × 9 = 7,290 (any first digit, then each next digit differs from the one before it), and 10,000 − 7,290 = 2,710.
2. **Digit more than twice, removes 0:** in 4 positions, any 3 of them always include two neighbours, so a digit used 3 or more times always gives an equal adjacent pair, which Rule 1 already rejects. (On its own the rule rejects 10 × 4 × 9 + 10 = 370.)
3. **Straight runs, removes 14:** there are 7 ascending runs (0123 … 6789) and 7 descending runs (9876 … 3210). All have distinct digits, so none was already rejected.
4. **ABAB, removes 90:** 10 choices of A × 9 choices of B≠A = 90. None has equal neighbours or is a run. (AAAA is already rejected by Rule 1.)
5. **ABBA, removes 0:** the "BB" in the middle is an equal adjacent pair, so Rule 1 already rejects every one of these.
6. **First = last, removes 720:** among the PINs still left, those with d₁ = d₄ number 10 × 9 × 8 = 720. The first digit has 10 choices, d₂ ≠ d₁ has 9, and d₃ must differ from both d₂ and d₄ = d₁, which leaves 8. None of these is an ABAB or a run, so there is no double counting.

**Check by a second method (counting allowed PINs directly):** pick d₁ (10 ways) and d₂ ≠ d₁ (9 ways). Then:
- d₃ = d₁ (1 way), and d₄ ≠ d₃, d₁ (9 ways) → 9
- d₃ ∉ {d₁, d₂} (8 ways), and d₄ ∉ {d₃, d₁} (8 ways) → 64

That gives 90 × 73 = 6,570 PINs that pass Rules 1, 2, 5 and 6. Taking out the 90 ABAB PINs and the 14 runs leaves 6,570 − 90 − 14 = **6,466**, the same result.

**Points for the CISO:**
- **Rules 2 and 5 are redundant** given Rule 1. They cost nothing, but they add no protection either.
- **Rule 1 does most of the work,** followed by Rule 6. Together they remove 3,430 of the 3,534 rejected PINs.
- **Guessing chance for an attacker:** if the PIN is chosen uniformly from the allowed set, the space shrinks from 10,000 to 6,466, about 0.63 bits less entropy (log₂(10,000/6,466) ≈ 0.63). The policy's real value is in blocking the PINs people actually pick, not in raising the count.
