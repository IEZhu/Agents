### Why "we're fine" doesn't hold

A peak of exactly 5,000 against a 5,000 cap means the model only works if everything is perfect. Small deviations each push you over:

- **Throughput 1% below 40/min.** Workers deliver about 19,000 jobs over t=0–16, so a 1% shortfall is about 190 extra jobs. That gives a peak near 5,190 and roughly 190 dropped.
- **Boot takes ~10 s longer than 1 min.** Each scale-up wave loses about 200/min × 1/6 min ≈ 33 jobs. Three waves add about 100 jobs, all dropped.
- **Bursty arrivals.** Your load generator won't produce a perfectly smooth 1,500/min. Short-term spikes around t=15–16 go straight over the cap.
- **Cap semantics.** Check whether your trim (e.g. `LTRIM`) or producer guard drops at `len >= 5000` or `len > 5000`. With a peak of exactly 5,000, that off-by-one decides whether you lose messages at all.

So my expectation is that the real test will **probably drop some messages, likely in the tens to low hundreds**, around t=14–17. That's a judgment based on the sensitivities above, not a certainty. Your teammate's "blow past 5k" overstates it, but the risk is real.

### Cheap fixes for the review (any one gives you margin)

- **Start at 30 workers.** Then the peak is only about 2,300 (+300/min for 6 min, then +100/min) and nothing comes near the cap.
- **Shorten the autoscaler window** to 2–3 minutes, or scale on backlog *growth rate* rather than `backlog > 0`.
- **Raise the Redis list cap** to around 10k for the test, if memory allows (5k messages is usually tiny, but check your payload size).
- **Pre-warm** 5–10 workers just before the load ramps.
