The problem is time zones. Once every log entry is converted to UTC, Dana's PDF was created **43 minutes before J. Ortiz's comment existed**. Your DMS only includes comments that exist when the export runs, so her file could not have shown that comment.

**3. The "it's clearly a PDF" argument**

A PDF viewer toolbar shows that the image was taken *in a PDF viewer*. It doesn't show that the file came from the DMS export function. Many DMS web viewers render documents in a PDF-style viewer, often built on PDF.js, with a similar toolbar. Screenshots, phone photos of the screen and print-to-PDF all produce the same kind of image without any "export" event. Check whether the toolbar in the leaked image matches your DMS's own viewer or a desktop reader such as Acrobat or Edge. That comparison is a cheap and fairly strong test.

**4. What the evidence actually supports**

- Someone captured page 2 of v4 **between 13:55 and 14:20 UTC** with Ortiz's comment present.
- Among logged users, **Mark, Priya and Ortiz** had access during that window. **Dana's export didn't, and Tom's access came after the post.**
- The log doesn't identify the leaker. It only narrows the field to people with opportunity. Nothing here establishes intent or action for anyone.

**5. Assumptions that could change this (check before tomorrow)**

1. **Are the log times really local?** Confirm with the DMS admin or vendor that entries are stored or displayed in each user's local zone and that the conversion uses DST. If the log is actually UTC throughout, Dana's export at 14:12 would come after the comment and before the post, and the picture flips.
2. **Is the comment timestamp its creation time?** It could instead be the last-edit time. If Ortiz first added the comment earlier and edited it at 13:55, Dana's export might contain an earlier version. Compare the wording in the image with the comment's history.
3. **Is the log complete?** Ortiz's missing open event suggests it may not be. Look for other versions, print events, shared links and API access.
4. **The post time is only an upper bound.** The capture happened at or before 14:20 UTC, never after.
