**What's wrong with the current draft**

- **It implies intent without evidence.** Putting "previously advocated removing it" next to "disabled in PR #4127" tells readers he did it deliberately. The facts fit an accident just as well: a 30-file templating migration is exactly where a default or a missed key flips `true` to `false`. Have you asked Dan, or checked whether the new template renders `rate_limit.enabled` from a default? Settle that before anything goes out. If it was an accident, the draft has accused a senior engineer of sabotage in front of the whole company.
- **It blames the junior reviewer.** "Routed to a newly hired reviewer" makes the new hire part of the cause. They approved what the process let them approve.
- **It stops at a person, not a mechanism.** Even if the flag had been flipped on purpose, the real questions are why one approval was enough to disable a prod safety control, and why nothing caught it for 16 hours.
- **The action items won't prevent a repeat.** Revoking one person's rights fixes nothing if anyone else makes the same mistake. It also tells the org that postmortems are for punishing people, and after that people stop reporting things honestly.

**Suggested rewrite**

> **Root cause:** PR #4127, a ~30-file migration of gateway configs to new templating, changed `rate_limit.enabled` from `true` to `false` in `config/prod/gateway.yaml`. The change was not called out in the PR and was approved through our standard single-reviewer process. When partner batch traffic spiked Tuesday 09:12, no gateway throttling was in place, the DB connection pool was exhausted, and the public API was unavailable for ~50 minutes.
>
> **Contributing factors:**
> - Safety-critical prod config changes are not distinguished from routine changes in review; one approval suffices regardless of reviewer familiarity with the gateway.
> - Large mechanical refactors mixed with semantic config changes, making the diff hard to review.
> - No automated check or alert when the gateway rate limiter is disabled.
> - No backpressure below the gateway: a single traffic spike could exhaust the DB pool.
>
> **Action items:**
> 1. CODEOWNERS on `config/prod/gateway*` requiring gateway-owner approval.
> 2. CI check that fails (or requires explicit override) when safety flags like `rate_limit.enabled` change in prod configs.
> 3. Guideline: config-format migrations must be behavior-preserving; semantic changes go in separate PRs. Add a rendered-config diff to CI for templating changes.
> 4. Alert on rate limiter disabled or zero throttle events under high load.
> 5. Per-partner quotas and DB pool saturation protection (connection limits, load shedding) so a single client can't take down the API.

**About Dan specifically**

It doesn't belong in an org-wide root-cause section. The same goes for the reviewer: talk to them about reviewing prod config, don't name them.
