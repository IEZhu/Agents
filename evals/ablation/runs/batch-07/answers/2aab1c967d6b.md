I'd rewrite this section, not just tighten it. As drafted, it pins the outage on Dan's motives, and your timeline doesn't support that. Sent to the CTO and the whole org, it will read as a public accusation. That could backfire on you and on the postmortem process.

**What's wrong with the current draft**

- **It implies intent without evidence.** Setting "the author had previously advocated removing it" next to the flag flip suggests sabotage or a backdoor win. It's just as plausible that the templating migration changed a default, or that a mapping bug set `enabled: false`, and Dan never noticed. That's common in 30-file config refactors. Until someone has checked the diff history and asked Dan, the architecture-review argument doesn't belong in the root cause. It's a post hoc inference.
- **"Routed to a newly hired reviewer" suggests he chose her to avoid scrutiny.** Did he pick her, or did CODEOWNERS or a round-robin assign her? Check before you imply anything.
- **It names a person as the root cause.** The actual failure is that one flag change in prod could disable the only thing protecting the DB, and nothing caught it: not review, CI, deploy checks, or alerting, for about 16.5 hours. Swap in any engineer and the same outage happens.
- **"Revoke merge rights" punishes one person and leaves the gaps open.** It also teaches the org to hide mistakes in future postmortems.

**Suggested rewrite**

> **Root cause:** PR #4127 (gateway config templating migration, ~30 files) set `rate_limit.enabled: false` in `config/prod/gateway.yaml`. The change was not flagged in review, CI, or deploy, and no alert fired while the limiter was off. When partner batch traffic spiked Tuesday at 09:12, requests reached the database unthrottled and exhausted the connection pool, causing ~50 min of timeouts and 503s.
>
> **Contributing factors:**
> - A safety-critical setting was changed inside a large refactor, and nothing required it to be reviewed separately.
> - Gateway config changes need only one approval, with no requirement for domain ownership.
> - No policy/CI check guards critical prod flags (rate limiting, auth, etc.).
> - No monitoring or alerting on rate-limiter state.
> - The DB connection pool has no backpressure or load shedding, so a single upstream control was the only line of defense.
>
> **Action items:**
> 1. Add CODEOWNERS for `config/prod/gateway*` requiring approval from the gateway/platform owners.
> 2. Add a CI policy check that fails when critical flags are disabled in prod without an explicit override label.
> 3. Alert on rate-limiter disabled or bypassed and on per-client request-rate anomalies.
> 4. Add backpressure at the service/DB layer (pool wait timeouts, load shedding) so one missing control can't take down the API.
> 5. Guideline: split behavior-changing config edits out of mechanical refactors.
> 6. Confirm how the flag flipped (intentional change vs. templating default/bug) and record the finding here.

**Separately, not in the doc:** if you have real reason to think the change was deliberate, talk to Dan and his manager privately. The architecture-review disagreement is a performance or trust question, and an org-wide postmortem is the wrong place for it. The per-service vs. gateway rate-limiting debate can also be reopened on its merits. This incident is evidence for keeping a gateway-level backstop, whatever else gets built.
