**My recommendation is Laptop Y.** Of your three sources, the lab review is the one to weight most heavily. On its numbers, X costs $550 more (about 41%) for roughly 12% more sustained performance and about 2 extra hours of battery. For your workload that's a real but small gain, and it's poor value for the money.

One caveat: I haven't read that review or the Reddit thread myself, so everything below assumes the figures you quoted are accurate.

## How much to trust each source

| Source | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Lab review** (30-min sustained load, measured battery test) | **High** | It tested your actual problem: whether the machine holds performance under long load. It used a controlled method and measured instead of guessing. |
| **Reddit thread** (~40 upvotes) | **Low, but don't ignore it** | Upvotes show that people agree, not that anyone measured anything. It may still be pointing at something real (see below). |
| **Manufacturer "2x faster"** | **Very low** | This is marketing. Claims like this usually compare against an older generation or measure one specific workload, such as the NPU, GPU or a single benchmark. The same chassis with ~12% more sustained multi-core is typical for a generational step. 2x is not. |

**Why the Reddit and lab results can both be true.** "Throttles like crazy" reports usually come from one of these:
- **An early BIOS or firmware version.** Previous-gen models often get thermal and power fixes months after launch, so check the thread's dates against the date of the lab review.
- **The power profile.** Running in "quiet" or "balanced" mode instead of "performance" can cut sustained clocks a lot.
- **A different configuration**, for example a different CPU SKU in the same chassis.
- **Individual bad units**, such as poor thermal paste or a blocked intake. Some people also use it on a bed or on their lap.

A lab test that ran 30 minutes of sustained load and found only a 12% gap directly contradicts "basically unusable for compiling." If Y really throttled that badly, a test built to find exactly that would have shown it.

## What the difference means for you

- **C++ builds:** if X does your build in 35 min, Y would take about **40 min** (12% lower throughput works out to roughly 13–14% more time). Real builds are partly limited by I/O and linking, so the gap may be smaller. That's noticeable, but it doesn't change how you work.
- **Python + Docker:** there's practically no difference. This work is rarely limited by sustained multi-core speed.
- **Battery (~11h vs ~13h in web browsing):** this is the one advantage of X that matters for you, since you travel 2 weeks a month. Keep in mind that video calls drain the battery much faster than browsing, so on a call-heavy day both will probably land well below those figures. The gap will likely shrink in absolute hours too. Both should last a normal working day. Neither will get through a long-haul flight plus a full day of calls without charging.
- **Weight (1.39 vs 1.41 kg):** 20 g, which makes no practical difference.

## When X is worth it

- You often work long stretches without a charger, where 2 extra hours decides whether you finish the day.
- You compile several times a day, and 5 minutes per build adds up to real time for you.
- X offers something that isn't on your list. For example, a newer webcam or mics would matter for all your calls. Worth checking.

If none of these apply, the $550 is better spent elsewhere. A good 100W GaN charger or a USB-C power bank that can charge the laptop would easily make up the battery difference.

## Before you buy Y

1. **Check that the review tested the exact configuration** you're buying (CPU model, 32GB/1TB).
2. **Update the BIOS/firmware on day one** and set the power profile to "performance" while plugged in for builds.
3. **Run one full build during the return window**, while watching clock speeds and temperatures (e.g. with `s-tui`/`htop` on Linux or HWiNFO on Windows). If it really collapses under load, return it. That's the cheapest way to settle the Reddit question for your own unit.
4. **Make sure the sale price includes the full standard warranty.** Previous-gen stock sometimes sells with shorter or reseller warranties.

Overall: buy Y, test it under your real build load while you can still return it, and put the savings toward charging gear for travel.
