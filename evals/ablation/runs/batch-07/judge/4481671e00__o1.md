## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. States what she is being asked to approve and the bottom line early, in plain business language.
2. Money figures are carried over faithfully: about $14.2k/month now versus about $11.8k/month estimated after, i.e. roughly $2.4k/month (about $28.8k/year) lower, plus the one-time cost of about 6 engineer-weeks; it also mentions the ~0.5 FTE of ongoing maintenance effort in the current setup.
3. Translates the risks into business terms (e.g. some dashboards must be rebuilt, two add-ons need replacing, searches on logs older than 30 days may be somewhat slower, and staying put means running software without security updates) rather than leaving jargon like shards, UltraWarm, ingest pipelines or p99 unexplained.
4. Stays brief enough to read in a couple of minutes and does not invent numbers, dates or risks that are not in the engineering text.

## Answer A
Here's a version written for a finance reader. Below it are a few gaps you should close with engineering before it goes to her, because she will probably ask about them.

---

**Subject: Approval requested by Friday: move our search platform to a managed AWS service**

**Decision needed**
Approve moving our search and logging platform from servers we run ourselves to Amazon's managed equivalent (Amazon OpenSearch Service).

**Why now**
Our current search software is stuck on an old version because of a vendor licensing change, and it no longer gets security fixes. The only way to keep patching it ourselves is a major upgrade, and that upgrade needs a license review that could add cost. Moving to the managed service removes that exposure, and AWS takes over patching and upgrades.

**Cost impact**

| | Today | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly infrastructure cost | ~$14.2k | ~$11.8k |
| Annualized | ~$170k | ~$142k |
| **Estimated savings** | | **~$2.4k/month (~$29k/year)** |

- **Staff time:** about half of one SRE's time currently goes to maintaining this system. Most of that time is freed for other work. It is a capacity gain and does not reduce headcount.
- **One-time cost:** about 6 engineer-weeks of migration work, plus roughly 2 weeks of running the old and new systems side by side, which briefly doubles that month's infrastructure bill.

**Risks and how we manage them**
- **Slower searches on old data:** searches on logs older than 30 days may be 10–15% slower. We accept this because it is how we get the storage savings. Recent data is unaffected.
- **Rework:** some internal dashboards need to be rebuilt, and two custom add-ons need to be replaced with built-in features. Both are included in the 6-week estimate.
- **Cutover:** we move over during a scheduled maintenance window, after two weeks of running both systems in parallel, so we can fall back if something goes wrong.

**Recommendation**
Approve. The move cuts running costs by about 17%, closes a security gap we can't fix cheaply otherwise, and frees up SRE time. The one-time cost is modest.

---

**Check these with engineering before you send it:**

1. **Is the SRE time already in the $14.2k?** The original wording is ambiguous. If $14.2k covers infrastructure only, the real savings are larger than shown, because half an SRE's time is being freed. If $14.2k already includes the SRE time, then $11.8k may be an infrastructure-only figure and the two numbers aren't comparable. Get both figures on the same basis.
2. **Put a dollar figure on the one-time cost.** A finance reader will want the migration cost in dollars and a payback period. As an illustration only: if a loaded engineer-week costs about $4k (my assumption, so use your internal rate), the one-time cost comes to about $24k of labor plus about $12k for the overlap month, so roughly $35k in total. That pays back in about 14–15 months from the infrastructure savings alone, and sooner if you count the SRE time.
3. **Confirm the $11.8k estimate.** Ask whether it uses on-demand or reserved pricing, and whether it covers data transfer and snapshot storage. Reserved instances could lower it further, but they come with a 1- or 3-year commitment that she should know about.
4. **Confirm the security claim.** "No more security patches" reflects engineering's description of our situation. Check that the memo states it accurately, because this is the strongest reason for doing the move now.


## Answer B
Here's a version for her. Before it goes out, check the three open items at the bottom with engineering. As written, the numbers leave those gaps, and a finance reviewer is likely to spot them.

---

**Subject: Approval request: move our search cluster to a managed AWS service**

**The ask**
Approve moving our search infrastructure from servers we run ourselves to Amazon's managed service (Amazon OpenSearch Service). Engineering is asking for sign-off by Friday.

**Why now**
Our current search software (Elasticsearch 7.10) is stuck on an old version because of a vendor licensing change, and it no longer receives security patches. Staying on it means a growing security risk. Upgrading instead would require a new license, which would need legal and cost review and hasn't been priced.

**Financial impact**

| | Today | After migration |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly infrastructure cost | ~$14.2k | ~$11.8k |
| Ongoing engineering upkeep | ~0.5 FTE (patching, upgrades) | Mostly handled by AWS |

- **Run-rate savings:** ~$2.4k/month, about **$29k/year** in infrastructure alone.
- **Freed capacity:** about half of one engineer's time moves from maintenance to other work.
- **One-time cost:** about 6 engineer-weeks of effort. We also pay for both systems during a 2-week overlap period.

**Risks (all rated manageable by engineering)**
- Some internal dashboards need to be rebuilt in the new tool.
- Two custom components aren't supported and will be replaced with standard features.
- Searches on data older than 30 days may be 10–15% slower. That data moves to cheaper storage, and this is where much of the savings comes from.
- The final switchover happens during a scheduled maintenance window.

**Recommendation**
Approve. The move lowers monthly cost, removes an unpatched security exposure, and avoids an open-ended license negotiation.

---

**Check these with engineering before sending:**

1. **Is the 0.5 FTE included in the $14.2k?** The original wording is ambiguous. If it isn't included, the real savings are much larger than $29k/year, and the memo should say so.
2. **What does the one-time cost come to in dollars?** Get a loaded dollar figure for the 6 engineer-weeks plus the 2-week overlap. With that she can see a payback period rather than just "effort."
3. **Is $11.8k a firm quote or an estimate?** Confirm whether it's based on AWS pricing for our actual data volumes, and whether it assumes reserved-instance discounts.
