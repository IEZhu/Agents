## Answer A
**Yes, B won, and it wasn't close.** You fixed the duration in advance, nobody peeked and the split is clean, so a standard two-proportion test applies directly.

| | Visitors | Purchases | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 10,000 | 500 | 5.00% |
| Variant B | 10,000 | 610 | 6.10% |

- **Absolute lift:** +1.1 percentage points
- **Relative lift:** +22%
- **z ≈ 3.40, two-sided p ≈ 0.0007.** That is well below 0.05. A 1.1pp gap this large would be very unlikely if the two versions actually converted at the same rate.
- **95% CI on the difference:** about +0.5pp to +1.7pp. In relative terms that's roughly +9% to +35%.

The whole interval sits above zero, so B is better. The honest way to report it is "about +22%, plausibly anywhere from +9% to +35%". Don't present 22% as a precise number.

**Check these before you post:**
1. **Was purchase conversion the primary metric you chose before the test?** If you looked at several metrics and are reporting the one that moved, the p-value overstates how sure you can be.
2. **Revenue per visitor / AOV.** This test only shows that more people bought. If B has a lower average order value (for example, it pushes a cheaper option), the revenue result could be smaller or even negative. Check this if you have the data.
3. **Counting unit.** Make sure "visitors" means unique users assigned to a variant, not sessions. If one user can count more than once, the test treats the data as more independent than it really is, and the confidence interval ends up too narrow.
4. **Long-term effect.** Two weeks covers weekly cycles, which is good. Still, part of the lift could be novelty. Keep an eye on conversion for a few weeks after you ship.

Your setup also holds up. The 50/50 split landed exactly at 10,000 each, so the allocation looks fine. Two full weeks covers weekday and weekend cycles, and since nobody peeked, the p-value means what it says.

**Before you call it a clean win, check three things:**

1. **Was conversion the pre-registered primary metric?** If you were also watching several other metrics, this one still clears the bar easily, but say which metric was primary.
2. **Revenue per visitor and AOV.** A checkout button change can raise conversion while shrinking the average order, for example if it pulls in more small impulse buys. If revenue per visitor also went up, the result is stronger.
3. **Expect the lift to shrink in production.** The confidence interval is wide, and winning variants usually shrink after rollout (winner's curse, novelty effects). Plan around something nearer the low end of the interval than the +22% headline.
