## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Keeps the refusal and the 30-day reason clear.
2. Friendly, empathetic tone in natural English.
3. Offers a constructive next step without promising a refund.

## Answer A
Here's a friendlier version that still gives the same answer:

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for reaching out, and I'm sorry to hear the purchase didn't work out for you.
>
> Unfortunately, we aren't able to offer a refund in this case. Our refund window is 30 days from the date of purchase, and this order falls outside that period. You can find the full details in our terms and conditions [link].
>
> I know this isn't the answer you were hoping for. If there's anything else I can help with, such as troubleshooting an issue with the product, please let me know.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your name]

A few notes on the changes:

- **The "no" is still clear.** "We aren't able to offer a refund" leaves no room for the customer to read it as "maybe." Avoid softer wording like "we may not be able to," because it invites follow-up requests.
- **The reason is there, without blame.** It explains the 30-day window instead of telling the customer to "read the terms." It still points them to the terms, with a link.
- **Only offer help you can actually give.** Keep the closing offer if you have something to provide, such as support, a replacement, or store credit. If you don't, cut it down to "If you have any other questions, I'm happy to help." Don't hint at options that don't exist.


## Answer B
Here's a friendlier version that still gives the same answer:

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for getting in touch, and I'm sorry this isn't the answer you were hoping for. Our refund policy covers returns within 30 days of purchase. Your order is past that window, so unfortunately we can't offer a refund this time.
>
> You can find the full details in our terms here: [link]. If there's anything else we can help with, like a question about your order or getting the most out of your purchase, just reply to this email and we'll be glad to help.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your name]

A few notes on the changes:

- It still says no clearly. "Unfortunately we can't offer a refund" leaves nothing open to negotiation, so the customer won't take it as a maybe.
- I changed "Read the terms" to a link with a short pointer. The original sounds like you're telling them off, and the link gives them the same information without that tone.
- The offer of other help ends the email on a positive note without promising anything. If you have a real option to offer, such as store credit, an exchange or a repair, mention it there by name. If you don't, keep that line general so it doesn't suggest an exception you can't make.
