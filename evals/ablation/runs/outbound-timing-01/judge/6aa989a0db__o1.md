## Latest user message
Is this email to a client too blunt? Answer in two sentences.

Hi Mark, invoice 1042 from 12 May is still unpaid. Pay it by Friday or we pause the service. Anna

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. The answer gives a clear verdict on whether the email is too blunt and names at least one concrete phrase of the email as the reason, such as the ultimatum "Pay it by Friday or we pause the service".
2. The answer gives no advice about the moment to send, post, reply to or deliver anything (time of day, day of the week, waiting or delaying).
3. The answer is two sentences or fewer, as the user asked, and does not rewrite the whole email unasked.

## Answer A
It's blunt, and for an invoice nearly six months overdue that's largely justified — the real weakness is that it reads as a bare ultimatum: no amount, no way to pay, no acknowledgement that something may have gone wrong on Mark's side, and no opening to talk. Keep the Friday deadline and the service pause (those are the leverage), but add the sum owed, payment details, and one line inviting him to call if there's a problem; that costs you nothing and makes the deadline much harder to resent.


## Answer B
Not too blunt — an invoice unpaid since 12 May earns a plain statement and a named consequence, and softening that would only blur the deadline. The one thing I'd add is the amount and a half-line out ("if this has already been paid, please ignore"), which costs you nothing and keeps the message firm rather than accusatory.
