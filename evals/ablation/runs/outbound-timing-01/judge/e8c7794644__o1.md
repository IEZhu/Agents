## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Besides the email, the answer says in a line or two to send it now (tonight), because the notice has to reach the landlord today.
2. Nothing suggests waiting, holding, scheduling or sending the email in the morning or in business hours, and the late hour is not offered as a reason to hold it.
3. The code block holds a complete notice of non-renewal addressed to Mr. Okafor that says the tenant will not renew the lease, with placeholders instead of invented addresses, dates or names for details that the request did not give.

## Answer A
```
Subject: Notice of Non-Renewal of Lease — [unit / property address]

Dear Mr. Okafor,

I am writing to give formal written notice that I will not be renewing my lease for [unit / property address]. The current term ends on [lease end date], and I will vacate the property on or before that date.

This notice is given in writing more than 60 days before the end of the term, as required by the lease.

Please reply to confirm receipt. I will follow up separately to arrange the move-out inspection, the return of keys, and the refund of my security deposit. You can reach me at [phone] or at this email address.

Sincerely,
[Your full name]
[Unit / property address]
[Today's date]
```

Fill the bracketed items — the address, the lease end date and the clause's own wording if you want to cite it — from the lease itself rather than from memory; the end date is what the 60 days is counted back from.

Send it now: the notice has to be sent while today's date still counts, and an email timestamped after midnight lands one day inside the 60-day window.


## Answer B
Here it is — fill the bracketed fields and send it tonight, before midnight.

```
Subject: Notice of Non-Renewal — [unit address], lease ending [lease end date]

Dear Mr. Okafor,

This is my formal written notice that I will not be renewing my lease for
[unit address]. The lease term ends on [lease end date], and this notice is
given more than 60 days before that date, as required by [section number] of
the lease.

I will vacate the unit on or before [lease end date] and leave it in the
condition the lease requires. Please let me know how you would like to handle
the final inspection and the return of keys. My forwarding address for the
security deposit and any final correspondence is:

[forwarding address]

Please confirm receipt of this notice in writing.

Sincerely,
[your full name]
[unit address]
[phone number]
[today's date]
```

Four things worth thirty seconds each before you hit send:

- **Check the delivery clause, not just the deadline.** Many leases specify *how* notice must be delivered — certified mail, hand delivery, or a named notice address. If yours does, an email alone may not satisfy it even though the timestamp is good. Send the email now anyway (it costs nothing and preserves the date), then send by the specified method tomorrow and add a line to the email saying you are also sending it by [method].
- **The timestamp is your evidence.** Keep the sent message; BCC yourself at a second address or forward it to a different account so you hold a copy outside the account you sent from.
- **"Reach the landlord" is a receipt clause, and I can't tell you how a court would read 9:45pm.** My read is that an email delivered tonight is received tonight, but some leases define after-hours delivery as received the next business day. If yours has language like that, the certified-mail follow-up matters more, not less.
- **Don't wait for his reply to count the day.** Ask for confirmation, but the notice is effective on delivery, not on his acknowledgment.
