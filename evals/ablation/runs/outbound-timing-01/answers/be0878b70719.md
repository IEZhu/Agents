Four things worth thirty seconds each before you hit send:

- **Check the delivery clause, not just the deadline.** Many leases specify *how* notice must be delivered — certified mail, hand delivery, or a named notice address. If yours does, an email alone may not satisfy it even though the timestamp is good. Send the email now anyway (it costs nothing and preserves the date), then send by the specified method tomorrow and add a line to the email saying you are also sending it by [method].
- **The timestamp is your evidence.** Keep the sent message; BCC yourself at a second address or forward it to a different account so you hold a copy outside the account you sent from.
- **"Reach the landlord" is a receipt clause, and I can't tell you how a court would read 9:45pm.** My read is that an email delivered tonight is received tonight, but some leases define after-hours delivery as received the next business day. If yours has language like that, the certified-mail follow-up matters more, not less.
- **Don't wait for his reply to count the day.** Ask for confirmation, but the notice is effective on delivery, not on his acknowledgment.
