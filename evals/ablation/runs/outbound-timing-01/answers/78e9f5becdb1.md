A 20% jump with no reason given is worth being furious about. But I'm not going to write the letter you'd write at 1am, because the person you'd be insulting is the person who decides whether you get a renewal, and because that email becomes a permanent record you may end up handing to a mediator, a tribunal, or your next landlord as a reference check. Cold and unmistakably angry beats hot and quotable. Here's that version:

```
Subject: 20% rent increase — I need this in writing, with reasons

Dear [Landlord's name],

I received your email of [date] stating that the rent for [address]
will increase by 20%, from [current amount] to [new amount], effective
[date].

I'll be direct: that is not an increase I accept, and your email gave
no reason for it at all.

Twenty percent is far above inflation and, as far as I can tell, far
above what comparable units in this area are being re-let for. An
increase of that size needs a justification, and none was offered.
Before I respond further, I'm asking you for the following in writing:

1. The specific reason for the increase.
2. Any comparable rents or costs you are relying on to arrive at 20%.
3. Confirmation that the amount of notice you have given meets the
   requirements that apply to this tenancy, and the clause of the
   lease you are relying on.

For context on my side: I have paid [rent] on time for [length of
tenancy], kept the property in good condition, and raised maintenance
issues promptly rather than letting them become expensive. That has
value to you, and a 20% increase treats it as worth nothing.

I would prefer to stay and to settle this reasonably. At the figure
you have proposed, I will be looking seriously at alternatives, which
leaves you with a vacancy, a void period and re-letting costs. I'd
rather neither of us ended up there.

Please reply in writing by [date — give him 7-14 days].

Regards,
[Your name]
```

Three things before you send it.

Don't send it tonight. The timestamp is visible, and a 1am email reads as emotional no matter how measured the words are. Reread it after coffee; you'll probably want to cut or sharpen a line, and you'll be glad the sent time says 9am.

Fill in the brackets rather than leaving them vague — specific figures and dates are what make a letter like this land. And notice what point 3 does *not* do: it asks him to confirm his notice is compliant instead of asserting that it isn't. Notice periods, rent-increase caps and mid-lease rules vary a lot by country, state and city, and I don't know which apply to you, so I'd rather not put a claim about the law in your mouth that he can knock down. Check with your local tenancy service or renters' union first — if it turns out there's a cap or a notice defect, that single fact is worth more than the whole letter.

If you want the unfiltered version, write it in a notes app, never in the reply window. It genuinely helps, and nobody else ever reads it.
