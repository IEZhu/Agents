Two things before the text itself.

Don't send this at 5:40 on a Friday when you're about to go dark. Tom reads "two outages" plus "change how you work," then sits with it for 60 hours with nobody to ask what it means. That reliably turns a 20-minute conversation into a weekend of worst-case interpretation, and you spend Monday repairing that instead of fixing the actual problem. Write it now, send it Monday morning.

And check your pipeline before you put this on Tom. If a branch can merge with tests unrun, "test before you merge" is a request he can forget and so can the next person. Two outages out of one release looks at least as much like a missing merge gate as a careless engineer. If there's no gate, the gate is the fix and this message becomes a heads-up rather than the intervention.

Here's the message, for Monday:

```
Tom — can you grab 30 minutes with me Monday morning? I want to go through
last week's release.

Two outages came out of it, and [the on-call team was paged twice / we were
degraded for X hours]. Both looked like things that would have shown up in a
test run on your branch.

What I want going forward is tests run and green before you merge, not after.
Before I treat that as settled, I want to hear from you whether anything is
making it hard — gaps in coverage, a pipeline that's too slow to wait for,
pressure to get things in by a date. If it's one of those, that's mine to fix,
not yours.

This isn't a formal anything. I want the problem closed and the release path
tightened.
```

Two notes on it. The impact line is bracketed because I don't know the real numbers, and vague impact ("it caused problems") is what makes this kind of message land as a mood rather than a fact — put the actual paging or downtime in. And keep the question in: if it turns out nobody ever told him the expectation, or the pipeline makes it impractical, you need to know that before Monday's conversation becomes a performance conversation.

If you genuinely want something out tonight, send only the invite, with none of the substance:

```
Tom — can we take 30 minutes Monday morning to go through last week's
release? Nothing dramatic, I just want to get ahead of the outages before
they become a pattern. Have a good weekend.
```

That costs you nothing and keeps the hard part live, which is where it belongs anyway.
