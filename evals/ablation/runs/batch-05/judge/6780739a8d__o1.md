## Answer A
**Short answer: no, this plan won't work as it stands.** Under Russian law, selling a share in a flat to an outsider has to be done through a Russian notary. A simple written contract will be rejected by Rosreestr, and even if it somehow got registered, it could be declared void. There are also three other problems: your mother's and sister's pre-emption right, the fact that your cousin would be signing both as your representative and as the buyer, and the formalities for a German power of attorney. Taxes look favourable for you on both sides, but you should confirm that.

Jurisdiction: Russian Federation (the flat is in Kazan, Republic of Tatarstan). There are also German tax points because you live in Germany.

---

## 1. Notarial form is mandatory. This is the main problem.

- **Art. 42(1) of Federal Law No. 218-FZ "On State Registration of Real Estate"** requires notarisation for a transaction that sells a share in common shared ownership (*долевая собственность*). There are a few narrow exceptions, for example when all co-owners sell the whole flat together in one deal. A sale of your 1/3 to your cousin alone doesn't fall under any of them.
- If the notarial form is missing, the deal is **void** (Art. 163(3) of the Civil Code, *ГК РФ*), and Rosreestr will refuse or suspend registration.
- Where the money goes: the state notarial fee is a percentage of the price with a cap (about 0.5%, max 20,000 RUB for non-close relatives, per Art. 333.24 of the Tax Code, *НК РФ*; recalled, not verified). On top of that the notary charges regional fees for "legal and technical services", usually around 10,000–25,000 RUB in Tatarstan (my estimate, check with the notary). On a 2.1M RUB deal that's a small price to pay for a valid transaction. As a bonus, the notary normally files the registration with Rosreestr electronically, so nobody has to go to the MFC.

## 2. Your mother and sister have a pre-emption right

- **Art. 250 ГК РФ**: when a share is sold to a third party, the other co-owners can buy it first **at the same price and on the same terms**. A cousin counts as a third party here.
- You have to notify them in writing of the price and terms. They then have **one month** (for real estate) to accept. Alternatively, they can sign **written waivers** earlier, and in practice notaries want these notarised.
- The notary won't certify the sale without proof of notice or waivers. If the right is skipped, a co-owner can go to court within **3 months** and have the buyer's rights transferred to themselves (Art. 250(3)).
- In your family this is probably just a formality, but it has to be done. The simplest route is for your mother and sister each to sign a notarised waiver at any notary in Kazan.

## 3. The power of attorney

**(a) Don't make your cousin your representative.** Under **Art. 182(3) ГК РФ**, a representative who concludes a deal on the principal's behalf with *himself* creates a contestable transaction, and there is a presumption that it harms the principal. Russian notaries routinely refuse to certify such deals. Give the POA to someone else, for example your sister, your mother or a trusted friend.

**(b) Form of the POA.** A POA for a transaction that must be notarised must itself be notarised (Art. 185.1(1) ГК РФ). You have two options:
- **Russian embassy or consulate in Germany.** A POA done there has the same force as one from a Russian notary. It's written in Russian and needs no apostille or translation. This is usually the smoothest option. Russia's consular network in Germany was cut back in 2023, so check the appointment situation.
- **German notary.** This works in principle, but you'd need an **apostille** (Hague Convention of 1961) plus a **certified Russian translation** made in Russia. Check that the apostille route between Germany and Russia is still operating normally. Also, Kazan notaries can be picky about German-style wording. If you go this way, get the exact POA text in Russian from the Kazan notary in advance and have it executed bilingually.

The POA should expressly cover: selling your 1/3 share at a specific address (with the cadastral number) to a named buyer at a named price, signing the contract and the transfer deed, receiving the money, and filing with Rosreestr.

## 4. Tax

- **Russia.** The share was acquired by inheritance, and inherited property is exempt once held for at least **3 years** (Art. 217.1(3) НК РФ). The holding period runs from the date the inheritance opened, i.e. your father's death in 2019. So the sale should be exempt from personal income tax (*НДФЛ*) and you wouldn't need to file a declaration. As I recall, since 2019 this exemption also covers non-residents, but please confirm, because it decides between 0% and 30% tax.
- **Germany.** Private sales of real estate are taxable only within 10 years (§ 23 EStG). For inherited property, your father's acquisition date generally counts for you. If he bought the flat more than 10 years before the sale, you're most likely outside the taxable period. The Russia–Germany double tax treaty also allocates taxing rights on real estate to the country where it's located. Confirm with a German Steuerberater, especially if your father acquired the flat recently.

## 5. Other points to check

- **Your citizenship.** If you're a Russian citizen, the "unfriendly states" restrictions on real estate deals (Presidential Decree No. 81 of 01.03.2022) generally don't apply to you. If you hold only German citizenship, the deal may need special permission, and that would change everything. Tell me if this applies.
- **Payment and currency control.** Get paid by bank transfer, or better, through a letter of credit or the notary's deposit account, not in cash. As someone living abroad you're likely a currency non-resident. Transfers from Russia to foreign accounts are currently subject to limits and bank-specific rules, so check with your Russian bank before the deal how you'll actually get the money to Germany.
- **Title.** Make sure your 1/3 is actually registered in your name in the EGRN (the unified state register). An extract takes a few minutes to order.
- **Spouse consent.** Inherited property is your personal property (Art. 36 of the Family Code, *СК РФ*), so no spouse consent is needed on your side.

## 6. Suggested order of steps

1. Choose a notary in Kazan and agree the draft contract and POA text.
2. Your mother and sister sign notarised waivers of their pre-emption right, or you send them formal notice and wait one month.
3. You sign the POA at the Russian consulate in Germany (preferred), or at a German notary with apostille plus translation, naming someone other than your cousin.
4. The notary certifies the sale contract, and your cousin signs as buyer.
5. The notary files for registration with Rosreestr.
6. Payment goes through a letter of credit or the notary's deposit, released after registration.

## Answer B
**Short answer (Russian law):** No, the plan won't work as it stands. A sale of a *share* in a flat must be notarised in Russia, so a contract you sign yourselves will be void and Rosreestr will refuse to register it. There are three more gaps: your mother and sister have a pre-emptive right to buy, you shouldn't give the power of attorney to the buyer himself, and the German PoA needs extra formalities. Most of this is quick to fix, and the notary is a small cost next to 2.1 million rubles.

---

## 1. A simple written contract is not enough

- **Federal Law No. 218-FZ "On State Registration of Real Estate", Art. 42(1):** transactions that alienate a share in common ownership of real estate must be notarised. The main exception is when *all* co-owners sell the whole flat in a single deal, which isn't your case.
- **Civil Code (ГК РФ) Art. 163(3):** if a transaction that requires notarisation is not notarised, it is **void** (ничтожна).
- In practice the MFC will accept the papers, and then Rosreestr will suspend and refuse registration. You would lose weeks and the state duty.

**Fix:** sign the contract (договор купли-продажи доли) with a notary in Kazan. The notary also files it with Rosreestr electronically, usually on the same day, so the cousin doesn't need to go to the MFC. Registration after a notary's filing is usually very quick, often 1–3 working days (recalled, not verified; confirm with the notary).

## 2. Your mother and sister have a pre-emptive right (преимущественное право покупки)

- **ГК РФ Art. 250:** the other co-owners have the right to buy your share at the same price and on the same terms before any outsider. Your cousin is an outsider for this purpose.
- You must notify them in writing of the price and terms. You can sell to the cousin only if they refuse, or if they don't buy within **one month** (the period for real estate).
- If you skip this, either of them can sue within **3 months** to have the buyer's rights and duties transferred to her.
- The notary will not certify the deal without proof that this step was done.

**Fix:** the fastest route is for your mother and sister to sign notarised refusals (отказ от преимущественного права покупки). Then there is no one-month wait. The other route is to send them formal notice through a notary and wait the month.

## 3. The power of attorney

**Don't make your cousin your attorney.** Under ГК РФ Art. 182(3), a representative may not make a transaction on the principal's behalf with himself. Such a deal can be challenged. Give the PoA to a neutral trusted person, for example your mother or sister, or someone else you trust.

**Form:** a PoA for a transaction that must be notarised must itself be notarised (ГК РФ Art. 185.1(1)). A German notarial PoA can be used, but:
- it needs an **apostille** (Germany and Russia are both parties to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention);
- in Russia it needs a translation into Russian with the translator's signature certified by a Russian notary;
- Kazan notaries don't all treat foreign PoAs the same way. Some are cautious because a foreign PoA can't be checked in the Russian notarial registry.

**Often the simpler option:** get the PoA at a **Russian consulate in Germany**. Consular officials perform notarial acts, and their PoAs are accepted in Russia like any domestic one, with no apostille or translation. Signing in person on a trip to Kazan also works.

Whichever route you choose, **send the draft PoA wording to the Kazan notary first** and get their approval. It should give the right to sell your 1/3 share at a named address and cadastral number, sign the contract, receive the money or confirm receipt, and deal with Rosreestr.

## 4. Taxes

**Russia.** You live in Germany, so you are probably a Russian tax **non-resident** (fewer than 183 days in Russia in 12 months; НК РФ Art. 207). The general rate for non-residents is 30% (Art. 224(3)). However:
- inherited property is exempt after a minimum holding period of **3 years** (НК РФ Art. 217.1(3)). For inheritance, ownership runs from the date of death (ГК РФ Art. 1152(4)). Your father died in 2019, so that period has long passed;
- my understanding is that since the 2019 amendments this exemption also covers non-residents. I recalled this and have not verified it for the current year, so confirm it before you rely on it. If it applies, no Russian tax is due on the 2.1 million.

**Germany.** As a German tax resident you are taxed on worldwide income. A private sale of real estate is taxable under **§ 23 EStG** only if it happens within **10 years of acquisition**. An heir takes over the deceased's acquisition date. So if your father bought the flat more than 10 years ago, the sale should not be taxable in Germany. If he bought it more recently, a German Steuerberater needs to look at it. Keep in mind that Russia suspended key articles of its tax treaties with "unfriendly" states, including Germany, in 2023. I recall this but haven't verified the current status.

## 5. Other things to check

- **Your share is registered in your name in the EGRN (ЕГРН).** Having the inheritance certificate is not enough. Get a fresh EGRN extract.
- **Citizenship.** If you are a Russian citizen, the 2022 counter-sanctions permission regime (Presidential Decree No. 81) generally does not apply to you. If you hold only German or another "unfriendly-state" citizenship, it may, and it could require special accounts or permission. Tell me which applies.
- **Payment.** Choose a traceable method: bank transfer or a notary deposit (депозит нотариуса), which many notaries offer. Put the price and the payment mechanism in the contract. Don't rely on "cash, paid in full" if you won't be there.
- **Marital consent.** Inherited property is your personal property (Family Code Art. 36), so you don't need your spouse's consent.

## 6. Rough steps and costs

1. Choose a notary in Kazan and ask for the document list and a fee quote.
2. Your mother and sister sign notarised refusals of their pre-emptive right.
3. You sign the PoA (consulate, or German notary + apostille + translation) in favour of a trusted person, not the cousin.
4. The contract is signed at the Kazan notary, who files it with Rosreestr electronically.
5. Payment follows the contract terms, and registration is completed.

**Costs** (all recalled, not verified; confirm with the notary):
- Notarial tariff for the contract: 0.5% of the price, with a minimum of 300 rubles and a maximum of 20,000 rubles. On 2.1 million that is about 10,500 rubles.
- Regional technical-services fees (УПТХ): usually several thousand to about 15,000 rubles.
- Rosreestr state duty for an individual: about 2,000 rubles.
- Small fees for the refusals and the translation.

In total, expect a few tens of thousands of rubles, not a large sum.

**Main risks if you go ahead with the original plan:** the contract is void and registration is refused, your mother or sister has a claim under Art. 250, the deal could be challenged because of self-dealing through the PoA, and the notary could reject the foreign PoA.
