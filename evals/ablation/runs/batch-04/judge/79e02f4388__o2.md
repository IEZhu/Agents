## Answer A
**Bottom line (Colombian law):** The move itself is mostly paperwork: Cámara de Comercio, RUT, municipal ICA, payroll affiliations and the lease. The bigger risk is the freelance designer arrangement. Paying her COP 5M gross every month against a *cuenta de cobro*, with no withholding analysis, no electronic *documento soporte* and (probably) no check of her social security payments, can make those expenses non-deductible. It can also leave the SAS liable for tax it should have withheld, and it carries some labor-reclassification risk. Fix that before year-end close, and get a contador back before January if you can.

A note on sourcing: the article numbers and figures below are from memory and I haven't checked them against current texts here. Treat anything marked *(verify)* as needing confirmation with DIAN or a contador público.

## Part 1: Clean up before you move

### 1. The freelance designer (highest priority)

**a) Documentation for the deduction.** Since the move to electronic invoicing, a *cuenta de cobro* on its own does not support a deduction. When you buy from a person who isn't required to issue invoices, the SAS has to generate a **documento soporte en adquisiciones a no obligados a facturar** and transmit it electronically to DIAN (art. 771-2 ET; Resolución DIAN 000167 de 2021 *(verify)*). If you haven't issued these for March through December, you've built up about COP 50M of expenses that DIAN could reject in your 2025 *declaración de renta*.
- Action: issue the missing documentos soporte now, through your invoicing provider (most offer this module). Ask a contador how DIAN currently treats late issuance.
- Also check whether she is actually required to invoice (it depends on her total income and whether she's IVA-responsible). If she is, you need her electronic invoice, not a documento soporte.

**b) Retención en la fuente.** The SAS is a withholding agent. For fees paid to an individual:
- If she certifies that she hasn't hired two or more workers for her activity, the **art. 383 ET table** applies (as amended by Ley 2277 de 2022 *(verify)*). You first subtract her mandatory contributions and the 25% exempt income. On COP 5M a month the result may land in or near the 0% bracket, so the retention due may have been zero or small.
- Without that certification, the general rate for *honorarios* applies: 10% or 11% depending on whether she files a tax return *(recalled, verify)*. That would be about COP 500k a month you should have withheld.
- The withholding agent is liable for amounts it failed to withhold (art. 370 ET), on top of penalties, and the deduction can be challenged.
- Action: get her written certification and redo the calculation for each month. If retention was due, a contador needs to correct the relevant **Formulario 350** returns, which carry interest and penalties. The earlier you fix this, the lower the penalty tends to be.

**c) Her social security.** To deduct payments to an independent contractor, the contracting party must check that she paid health and pension contributions on the right base (art. 108 ET, parágrafo 2). The base is generally 40% of the monthly contract value *(verify the current legal basis)*. For contracts longer than one month she should also have ARL coverage, and the contratante has duties around affiliating her (Decreto 723 de 2013 *(verify)*).
- Action: collect her PILA receipts for every month from March onward and keep them with each payment.

**d) Contrato realidad risk.** A fixed monthly amount for 10 months with no written contract looks like employment if she also works set hours, takes instructions, uses your tools or works only for you. CST art. 24 presumes that any personal work relationship is an employment contract. If it were reclassified, you'd owe *prestaciones*, back social security (the UGPP audits exactly this) and possibly the *indemnización moratoria* under art. 65 CST.
- Action: either sign a proper *contrato de prestación de servicios* with deliverables, her own schedule and her own tools, and actually run it that way, or put her on payroll. Don't paper over a relationship that is really employment.

**e) Exógena.** Her payments must appear in your *información exógena* for tax year 2025, filed in 2026. That report is easier to get right once (a) through (c) are fixed.

### 2. The missing accountant

This affects more than convenience:
- Annual financial statements must be certified by the legal representative **and** the contador público who prepared them (Ley 222 de 1995, art. 37).
- Some tax returns need a contador's signature above certain thresholds (arts. 596 and 606 ET).
- Monthly or periodic filings keep coming due: retención (350), IVA, nómina electrónica and exógena.

**IVA on SaaS:** check with the contador whether your product qualifies for the IVA exclusion for cloud computing services (art. 476 ET *(recalled, verify the current numeral and conditions)*) or whether you should be charging 19%. Getting this wrong adds up quickly.

### 3. The office rent

- Check that you've withheld retención on rent payments to the landlord (3.5% on property leases *(verify)*). The documentation problem is the same as with the designer.

## Part 2: The relocation checklist

### Corporate and registry
- [ ] **Reforma estatutaria** changing the *domicilio principal* from Medellín to Bogotá: a shareholders' decision recorded in an *acta* and registered with the Cámara de Comercio. You usually file in Medellín, and the file is transferred to the Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá *(verify the current procedure with both chambers)*.
- [ ] Cancel or move any **establecimiento de comercio** registered for the El Poblado office, and register a new one in Bogotá if needed.
- [ ] Watch the calendar: matrícula renewal is due in the **first three months of the year** (C.Co. art. 33), and the ordinary shareholders' meeting approving the 2025 financial statements is due by **end of March** (C.Co. art. 422). You could approve the domicile change at the same meeting, or hold it earlier so it's done before the move.
- [ ] The **NIT stays the same**. Only the address and domicile change.

### Tax (national)
- [ ] **Update the RUT** with the new address, probably within one month of the change *(verify the deadline)*. Late updates carry penalties (art. 658-3 ET). This may also move you to a different DIAN seccional.
- [ ] Update your address in your electronic invoicing and nómina electrónica software. Check that invoice numbering authorizations still work.

### Tax (municipal ICA): the part people forget
- [ ] **Medellín:** file for cancellation (*novedad de cese*) with the Secretaría de Hacienda. Then file the final ICA return covering 2025 plus any 2026 activity before the move, and settle any *anticipo* already paid. Also check whether you were designated an ICA withholding agent, and cancel that too.
- [ ] **Bogotá:** register in the **RIT** with the Secretaría Distrital de Hacienda before you start operating. Confirm your filing frequency (bimonthly or annual, depending on the tax amount) and the activity code and rate for software/SaaS.
- [ ] If you keep clients in Antioquia, ask where the income from digital services is taxed for ICA purposes. Territorial rules for services aren't always obvious.

### Employees (3)
- [ ] **Confirm who is actually moving.** Moving the place of work to another city is a significant change to the contract, so get each employee's written agreement. If you impose it, you risk it being treated as a dismissal without just cause, with compensation under art. 64 CST. Options for anyone who stays: remote work (*trabajo remoto*, Ley 2121 de 2021; or *teletrabajo*, Ley 1221 de 2008) or a negotiated exit.
- [ ] If you move someone at your initiative, you may have to cover their relocation expenses and, when the contract ends, their return travel (CST art. 57, num. 8).
- [ ] **Caja de compensación:** switch from the Antioquia caja to one in Bogotá (Compensar, Colsubsidio, Cafam, etc.) for employees working in Bogotá. Anyone staying in Antioquia remotely may keep theirs *(verify)*.
- [ ] **ARL:** register the new workplace and update risk classification. **EPS/AFP** are national, but employees update their own municipality.
- [ ] **PILA:** update department and municipality codes starting with the first payroll after the move.
- [ ] **SG-SST:** update the hazard matrix, emergency plan and workplace inspection for the new office.
- [ ] Deadlines that fall around the move: **intereses sobre cesantías** paid to employees by **31 January**, **cesantías** deposited into the funds by **14 February** (Ley 50 de 1990, art. 99), the new minimum wage and *auxilio de transporte* from 1 January, and any *dotación* if salaries are 2 SMMLV or less.

### El Poblado lease
- [ ] Read the termination clause: notice period, *cláusula penal*, early-exit penalty. Give written notice now if you haven't, because notice periods often run for months.
- [ ] Arrange the handover (*acta de entrega* with an inventory), recovery of the deposit, and final utility and admin bills.
- [ ] New Bogotá office: before signing, check the **uso de suelo** for office use, and the requirements under Ley 1801 de 2016 art. 87 if you'll be open to the public.

### Commercial and admin
- [ ] Update your address in the terms of service, privacy policy (Ley 1581 de 2012), client contracts, invoices, website and bank records.
- [ ] Tell the banks, payment processors and any SaaS vendors that invoice you.

## Answer B
**Short answer (Colombia):** Fix the freelancer setup before you deal with the move. Paying COP 5,000,000 a month to an individual against a cuenta de cobro, with no withholding, no electronic support document and apparently no check of her social security payments, is the biggest exposure you've described. It can make seven months of expenses non-deductible, make the SAS liable for retención that was never withheld, and add labor-reclassification risk. You also need a contador público again, soon.

## Part 1 — Clean up before the move (do this now, September–October)

### A. The freelance designer (highest priority)

1. **Retención en la fuente on her payments**
   - Payments to individuals for honorarios or services are generally subject to withholding. The SAS is the withholding agent (agente de retención).
   - As I recall, Art. 383 E.T. (parágrafo 2) says that for an individual earning honorarios who hasn't hired 2 or more workers, the **progressive employment-income table** applies instead of a flat rate. Under that table you first subtract her mandatory contributions and the 25% renta exenta. The other route is the flat honorarios rate of roughly 10–11% (recalled, not verified).
   - At COP 5M/month, the table method could give a low or even **zero** withholding. **You still have to run the calculation for every payment and keep the working papers.** Ask her in writing whether she has hired 2 or more workers, because the answer decides which method applies.
   - If withholding was due and you didn't apply it, **the SAS answers for it** (Art. 370 E.T.). Fixing it means amending the Form 350 withholding returns for the affected months and paying the tax plus interest and a correction penalty. Your contador should do this calculation first.

2. **Documento soporte electrónico**
   - A cuenta de cobro is not enough to deduct a purchase from someone who isn't required to invoice. DIAN requires the buyer to issue an **electronic support document** (documento soporte en adquisiciones a sujetos no obligados a facturar), under DIAN Resolución 000167 de 2021 and Art. 771-2 E.T. (recalled, verify current version).
   - Without it, DIAN can reject the deduction for **COP 35M+** of expenses since March.
   - Issue the documents for the past months now. Check with your contador or software provider about any penalty for issuing them late.
   - Also look at her RUT. If she is registered as **responsable de IVA**, she must issue *factura electrónica* herself, and a cuenta de cobro is wrong for that reason too.

3. **Her social security contributions (salud, pensión, ARL)**
   - As I understand the current rule, Art. 108 E.T. makes payments to independent contractors deductible only if the contracting party **checks that the contractor has paid her contributions**. Those contributions are calculated on 40% of the monthly contract value, per Art. 244 Ley 1955 de 2019 (recalled).
   - **In practice:** ask for her paid PILA planilla for the month before you release each payment, and keep it on file. Ask for the planillas going back to March now.
   - For ARL, Decreto 723 de 2013 (recalled) requires contractors on contracts longer than one month to be affiliated. The contracting company usually handles the affiliation. Confirm this with the ARL you use for your employees.

4. **Contrato realidad (misclassification) risk**
   - She has worked continuously for seven months for a fixed monthly amount. If she also works set hours, takes day-to-day orders or works like staff, a court or the **UGPP** could treat her as an employee (Arts. 23–24 CST, where the presumption of an employment contract applies). You would then owe back prestaciones sociales, vacations and contributions, plus penalties.
   - **Fix:** put a written *contrato de prestación de servicios* in place with defined deliverables, her own schedule and tools, and no subordination. Make sure the day-to-day reality matches the contract. If she really works as staff, it may be cheaper to hire her.

5. **Exógena:** payments to her have to be reported in your annual información exógena filing. Make sure her NIT/cédula details are correct.

### B. Filings that may have been missed since June

With no accountant since June, check each of these for July, August and September:
- **Form 350** (monthly withholding return), for payroll withholding and any other withholding.
- **IVA returns** (Form 300), bimonthly or four-monthly depending on your income.
- **Nómina electrónica**, the monthly electronic payroll support transmitted to DIAN.
- **PILA** for the 3 employees. It's probably fine if the payroll operator runs it, but confirm.
- **ICA Medellín**, including the filing frequency and any ICA withholding (retención de ICA) you're required to apply.
- **Accounting books kept up to date.** Your financial statements must be certified by the legal representative **and a contador público** (Art. 37 Ley 222 de 1995), so you can't run this for long without one.

### C. IVA on your SaaS

Confirm how IVA applies to your subscriptions. Certain **cloud computing services are excluded from IVA** under Art. 476 E.T. (recalled), but only if specific conditions are met. Sales to foreign customers may also get different treatment. If you've been charging 19% when the service qualifies for the exclusion, or not charging it when it doesn't, fix that before you change jurisdiction.

## Part 2 — Relocation checklist

### Corporate / registry
- [ ] **Check your bylaws (estatutos).** If the domicilio social is Medellín, changing it is a **bylaws amendment** (reforma estatutaria) and needs a shareholders' meeting decision with the quorum and majority the bylaws require. Record it in the minutes (acta).
- [ ] Register the amendment with the **Cámara de Comercio de Medellín para Antioquia**. The file is then transferred to the **Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá**. Budget for registration fees and the departmental registration tax (impuesto de registro).
- [ ] Update the registered **commercial and notification addresses** (dirección comercial and dirección de notificación judicial).
- [ ] Remember that the **2027 matrícula mercantil renewal** is due by **31 March** (Art. 33 C.Co.), and it will be in Bogotá.
- [ ] Alternative to consider: if the change of domicile is complicated, you could keep the Medellín domicile and just open operations in Bogotá. For a 3-person company, a clean move is usually simpler.

### DIAN / national tax
- [ ] **Update the RUT** with the new address and the corresponding dirección seccional. Do it promptly, because late updates carry a per-day penalty under Art. 658-3 E.T. (recalled).
- [ ] Update the address on your **electronic invoices**, nómina electrónica and documento soporte templates with your provider.
- [ ] Make sure the change is reflected in your 2026 income tax return, filed in 2027.

### Municipal tax (ICA)
- [ ] **Medellín:** report the cese de actividades / cancellation with the Secretaría de Hacienda de Medellín, then file the final ICA return and pay what you owe.
- [ ] **Bogotá:** register in the **RIT** (Registro de Información Tributaria) with the Secretaría Distrital de Hacienda **before** you start operating there. Check the ICA rate and filing frequency for your CIIU code. Rates vary by city, so don't reuse the Medellín numbers.
- [ ] Split your ICA income correctly between Medellín and Bogotá for the transition period.

### Employees
- [ ] **Review each employment contract.** If the place of work is fixed as Medellín, moving it is a material change that needs the employee's **written consent**. You may also have to pay relocation costs; look at Art. 57 CST.
- [ ] For anyone who won't move: agree to **remote work** (trabajo remoto, Ley 2121 de 2021, or teletrabajo, Ley 1221 de 2008) with a written addendum. The other route is a negotiated exit. Ending a contract without just cause triggers the indemnización under Art. 64 CST.
- [ ] **Caja de compensación:** these are regional, so you'll probably need to move from the Antioquia caja to a Bogotá one for employees working there. Confirm the rules for remote staff who stay in Antioquia.
- [ ] **ARL:** register the new work site and update the risk classification.
- [ ] **SG-SST:** update your occupational health and safety system for the new premises.
- [ ] EPS and pension funds are national, so they shouldn't change. Just update the employer address.

### Office lease
- [ ] **El Poblado lease:** read the termination clause, the notice period and any early-exit penalty now. Commercial leases fall under Arts. 518–524 C.Co., and the contract terms control how you as tenant can leave. Give notice in writing, arrange the move-out inspection and set a date for getting the deposit back.
- [ ] **Bogotá premises:** before you sign, check that **zoning (uso de suelo)** allows your activity at that address.

### Commercial / data / operations
- [ ] Update the legal address in your **SaaS terms of service, privacy policy and DPA**. Under **Ley 1581 de 2012**, your privacy policy has to identify the data controller and its contact details correctly.
- [ ] Notify your bank, payment processors, customers (the address appears on invoices) and suppliers, and update your website.
- [ ] Update notification addresses in customer contracts.

### Professional support
- [ ] **Hire a contador público before January.** Beyond the certification rule, amending the withholding returns and closing out ICA in Medellín while registering in Bogotá is exactly the work a contador should do.
- [ ] Check whether you've crossed the thresholds that require a **revisor fiscal** (Ley 43 de 1990, Art. 13 par. 2, based on assets and income expressed in SMMLV). A small SaaS company probably hasn't, but confirm.

1. **Now:** hire a contador. Get the designer's planillas and RUT, sign a written contract with her, issue the missing documentos soporte, and calculate and correct withholding. Check for any missed filings since June.
2. **October–November:** give notice on the lease, agree changes with employees, hold the shareholders' meeting and register the change of domicile.
3. **December:** close ICA in Medellín, register in the Bogotá RIT, update the RUT, and update invoicing and payroll software.
4. **January:** move and update the caja, ARL and SG-SST. Then prepare for the matrícula renewal by 31 March.
