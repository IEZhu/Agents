## Things to watch

- **You left out two items: GHS and income tax on the rents.** GHS is the one that costs you money here. The company withholds the 2.65% on dividends along with the SDC. You pay GHS on the rents yourself, and there is no cap in your case because the total goes over it anyway. Rental income is subject to personal income tax, not only SDC. It comes to zero for you only because you have no other taxable income. If you pay yourself a salary or director's fees, the rent is added on top and falls into the 20% and higher bands.
- **Timing of the dividend.** You can't formally distribute 2026 profit until it exists. In practice that means paying an interim dividend during 2026, if your articles and Cap. 113 allow it and the company has enough distributable reserves. The other route is a final dividend in 2027 once the accounts are approved. Either way, a dividend paid after 1 January 2026 should get the 5% rate. I'm not certain whether any transitional rules apply to dividends paid out of older retained profits, so check that if you also plan to pay out reserves from 2025 or earlier.
- **Old undistributed profits.** Profits from 2024 and 2025 are still within the deemed dividend distribution rules. Paying them out as actual dividends now at 5% may be worth doing, but ask an adviser to confirm.
- **Cash flow.** The company's CIT is paid through provisional tax instalments, due 31 July and 31 December. Budget for €30,000 rather than €25,000. Your 2026 provisional estimate should use the 15% rate.
- **Salary vs dividend.** At 5% SDC plus 2.65% GHS on dividends, a salary is unlikely to beat a dividend for you. The exception is a small salary kept within your unused personal tax-free band.
