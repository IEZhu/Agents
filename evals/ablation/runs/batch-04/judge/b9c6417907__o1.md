## Answer A
**Bottom line (Georgia, Tax Code of Georgia 2010):**

1. **Your bookkeeper is wrong about the 90,000 GEL.** Since 1 January 2017 Georgian companies use the "Estonian model" of profit tax. Profit you keep in the company is not taxed. Tax is due only when profit is distributed, or treated as distributed. Keeping 600,000 GEL and using it to buy a warehouse for the business does not trigger profit tax.

## 1. The 90,000 GEL claim

**Rule.** Chapter XIV of the Tax Code (profit tax, roughly Articles 97–98) taxes these items, not annual accounting profit:
- distributed profit (dividends);
- expenses unrelated to economic activity;
- free supply of goods or services, or transfer of funds for free;
- representation (entertainment) expenses above the statutory limit.

The rate is 15% of the grossed-up amount (recalled, not verified).

**Your facts.** The company earned 600,000 GEL, paid no dividends and bought a warehouse for its wholesale business.
- Retaining profit is not a taxable event.
- Buying a business asset is ordinary capital expenditure. It is not a distribution or a "non-business expense".

**Conclusion.** You owe no profit tax for 2025 on the retained 600,000 GEL, assuming nothing in 2025 counts as a deemed distribution. Ask the bookkeeper which article they rely on. They are probably applying the pre-2017 annual-profit model or a foreign (e.g., Russian or Ukrainian) approach. It is also possible they found something specific in your books that is taxable. Check for these:
- **Deemed distributions:** personal expenses paid by the company, a car or apartment used privately, or loans from the company to you that are not on arm's-length terms. Some of these can be requalified as distributions.
- **Warehouse ownership:** the warehouse must be registered to the LLC (NAPR). If it were bought in your personal name with company money, that is a distribution to you and is taxable.
- **Filing:** Georgian profit tax is declared **monthly**, by the 15th of the following month. Months with no taxable object are simply zero. There is no year-end 15% "catch-up" on retained earnings.

## 3. Moving to the 1% "small business" regime

The friend's idea runs into three structural problems:

1. **Wrong legal form.** Small business status (roughly Articles 88–90) is granted only to an **individual entrepreneur** (ინდივიდუალური მეწარმე), not to an LLC (შპს). The LLC itself can never pay 1% turnover tax.
2. **Turnover cap.** The 1% rate applies to gross turnover up to **500,000 GEL per calendar year** (recalled, verify). The cap counts revenue, not profit. A building-materials wholesaler with 600,000 GEL of profit very likely has turnover in the millions. Above the cap, status is lost and a higher rate applies to the excess, roughly 3% as I recall.
3. **Excluded activities and hidden costs:**
   - Some activities are excluded from small business status (for example licence-based activities and certain consulting). Check wholesale trade against the current government resolution that lists them.
   - Status does not remove **VAT**. An IE whose taxable turnover exceeds 100,000 GEL in 12 months must register for VAT, so you would stay VAT registered.
   - Moving stock, the warehouse or customers from the LLC to yourself as an IE is a transfer out of the company. It would likely be treated as a distribution or taxable supply, triggering the same 15% gross-up plus VAT.

**Conclusion.** Unless the business is shrunk to well under 500,000 GEL of annual *turnover*, the 1% regime is not available, and restructuring to reach it would be costly. At your scale, the Estonian-model LLC is already favourable: 0% on reinvested profit and about 19.25% only on what you take out.

## 1. The 90,000 GEL claim

**Rule.** Since 1 January 2017, Georgian commercial companies (except certain financial institutions such as banks, insurers and microfinance organisations, which have their own rules) pay profit tax on **distributed profit**, not on annual accounting profit. This is set out in the Tax Code of Georgia, Chapter on profit tax, principally Art. 98¹ (object of taxation) and Art. 99 (15% rate). *(Article numbers recalled, not verified here. Check them against the current consolidated text on matsne.gov.ge.)*

**Your facts.** You are a wholesale trader, so the 2017 model applies to you and the bank/insurer exceptions do not. You made no distribution. Buying a warehouse used in the business is reinvestment, which is exactly what the model leaves untaxed.

**Conclusion.** No profit tax is due on the retained 600,000 GEL. The bookkeeper seems to be applying the pre-2017 annual profit tax, or treating accounting profit as the tax base. Ask them which article they are relying on.

**Where the 15% can still bite before any dividend.** The taxable object also covers these items:
- **Deemed (hidden) distributions.** Examples are non-arm's-length deals with you as a related party, such as buying from you or selling to you at off-market prices, or paying for your personal expenses.
- **Expenses not connected to economic activity.** For example, if you use the warehouse or company assets privately.
- **Free supplies** of goods, services or money.
- **Representation expenses above the statutory limit.**
- **Loans from the company to you personally.** As I recall, loans to individuals are treated as taxable. Confirm this before you take any "loan" from the company instead of a dividend.

These are declared and paid monthly, by the 15th of the following month. If the bookkeeper found real items like these in 2025, some tax may be due, but only on those items, not on the whole 600k.

## 3. Moving to the 1% small business regime

It does not work, for three reasons:
1. **Wrong legal form.** Small business status (1% of turnover, 3% once you exceed the threshold) is granted to **individual entrepreneurs** (ინდივიდუალური მეწარმე), meaning natural persons. An LLC (შპს) cannot hold it.
2. **Turnover cap.** The status covers annual income up to **500,000 GEL**. A wholesale business with around 600,000 GEL of *profit* almost certainly has turnover several times that. Once you exceed the cap, the rate goes up to 3% on all income from the month of exceedance to year-end, and the status is lost the following year. The regime is designed for businesses smaller than yours.
3. **Moving the business into an individual entrepreneur would hurt you.** You would lose limited liability. Moving the stock, warehouse and goodwill out of the LLC could itself be a taxable distribution or supply (profit tax and VAT). Splitting the business across several individual entrepreneurs to stay under the cap is the kind of artificial arrangement the Revenue Service challenges.

Your current setup is already efficient: 0% on reinvested profit and about 19% when you take money out.
