# Sleep Tips from One Tired Parent to Another 💤

*Real-life tips from a mom in our group, plus a few safe-sleep basics to go with them.*

Newborn sleep is hard, and you are doing a great job. None of these tips is a magic fix. Take what works for your family and skip the rest.

## What finally worked for us

**🛁 A cozy, predictable bedtime routine**
Bath, feed, a song, then lights out around 7:30. Doing the same steps in the same order each night helps your baby learn that sleep is coming.

**🔊 Gentle white noise**
Put the machine across the room, not next to the crib, and keep the volume low. Think soft shower, not vacuum cleaner.

**🌡️ A comfortable room**
Aim for about 68–72°F. Dress baby in no more than one layer more than you'd be comfortable in. Watch for overheating: a sweaty neck or flushed cheeks mean baby is too warm.

**😴 Drowsy but awake**
Lay baby down when they're sleepy but not fully asleep. It helps them learn to drift off on their own. Some nights it won't work, and that's okay.

**🤗 Help with the startle reflex**
If your baby keeps startling awake, try a snug (non-weighted) swaddle with room for the hips to move. Once baby starts showing signs of rolling, switch to an arms-out sleep sack. Please skip weighted sleep sacks and blankets, because pediatricians advise against them.

**🎠 Asleep in the swing? Time for a transfer**
Swings are great for calming a fussy baby. If your little one drifts off in one, though, move them to their crib or bassinet as soon as you can. Swings, car seats and bouncers aren't safe for sleep.

## The safe-sleep ABCs

- **A**lone: baby sleeps in their own space, with no pillows, bumpers, blankets or toys.
- **B**ack: put baby on their back for every sleep, naps included.
- **C**rib: use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet in a crib, bassinet or play yard.

Room-sharing (without bed-sharing) is recommended for at least the first 6 months.

## Be kind to yourself 💛

Every baby is different, and every phase passes. If you're worried about your baby's sleep, feeding or breathing, call your pediatrician. And if you're running on empty, tell someone in this group. We've been there.

*This handout shares tips from parents, not medical advice. Check with your pediatrician about what's right for your baby.*
