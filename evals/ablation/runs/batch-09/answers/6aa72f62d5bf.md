**Two tips to change first**

1. **Weighted sleep sack.** The American Academy of Pediatrics advises against weighted sleep sacks, swaddles and blankets for infants. It says there's no evidence they're safe, and extra weight on a baby's chest may affect breathing or make it harder for the baby to wake. I haven't checked the current wording today, so look it up on aap.org or healthychildren.org before you print. A regular (unweighted) wearable blanket still gives the cozy feeling. Also stop swaddling once a baby shows signs of rolling.
2. **Letting him finish the night in the swing.** Safe-sleep guidance is that babies who fall asleep in a swing, car seat, bouncer or carrier should be moved to a firm, flat sleep surface as soon as it's practical. When a baby is sitting up, the head can drop forward and restrict the airway. Being strapped in doesn't prevent that and adds a strangulation risk. Moving him is the key part of this tip, so I reversed it instead of dropping it.

# Sleep Tips from One Parent to Another 🌙

*One of our moms shared what finally helped her family get more rest. Every baby is different, so take what works for you and skip what doesn't. You're doing great.*

### Build a bedtime routine
Do the same few steps in the same order every night. Try **bath → feed → song → lights out** at about the same time, around 7:30. Babies like knowing what comes next, and after a while the routine itself starts to tell them it's time to sleep.

### Add some white noise
A white noise machine can cover household sounds. Put it **across the room from the crib** and keep the volume low, about as loud as a quiet shower.

### Keep the room comfortable
Aim for about **68–72°F**. Dress your baby in **no more than one layer more than you'd wear**. Overheating is a sleep risk, so if you're unsure, use fewer layers. Check the back of the neck: it should feel warm, not sweaty.

### Try a wearable blanket
A regular **(non-weighted)** sleep sack keeps babies snug without loose blankets in the crib. Stop swaddling once your baby starts trying to roll.

### Put baby down drowsy but awake
If your baby falls asleep in the crib, they learn to settle there, and that helps when they wake at 2 a.m.

### Dozed off in the swing? Move them.
It happens to all of us. When your baby falls asleep in a swing, car seat or carrier, **move them to their crib or bassinet** as soon as you can.

**The ABCs of safe sleep, for every nap and every night**
- **A**lone: no pillows, bumpers, blankets or stuffed animals in the crib
- **B**ack: always put baby down on their back
- **C**rib: use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet. Room-sharing (not bed-sharing) is recommended.

*Questions about your baby's sleep? Your pediatrician is always a great person to ask.*
