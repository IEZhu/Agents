## Answer A
Here's a tighter version. Three of the facts in your draft are wrong, though, so I fixed them rather than just trimming. The changes are listed below the slide text.

---

**GDPR basics**

GDPR has applied since 25 May 2018. It covers us because we [offer goods/services to, or monitor, people in the EU — confirm wording with Legal].

- **Access requests:** Reply without undue delay, and within **one month**. For complex or numerous requests we can extend by up to **two more months**, but we must tell the person within the first month.
- **Data breaches:** Report to the supervisory authority within **72 hours** of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to put people at risk. Tell Legal immediately. Don't decide on your own.
- **Fines:** Processing without a lawful basis can cost up to **€20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher**.

**When in doubt, ask Legal before collecting new data.**

*(~115 words)*

---

**What I changed and why** (from my recollection of the GDPR text. I didn't check the sources this time, so please have Legal confirm before Monday):

1. **Access request deadline (Art. 12(3)).** The deadline is "one month", not "30 days". The extension is up to *two further months*, not one.
2. **Fines (Art. 83).** Having no lawful basis breaks Art. 6, which sits in the **higher tier (Art. 83(5)): €20M / 4%**. The €10M / 2% tier covers other breaches, such as certain controller/processor obligations. Both tiers are "whichever is **higher**", not lower. This error mattered most, because the draft understated the risk.
3. **Why GDPR applies (Art. 3).** It isn't triggered just by holding "data of EU residents". It applies if we're established in the EU, or if we offer goods/services to, or monitor the behaviour of, people *in* the EU. I left a placeholder so Legal can state our actual basis.
4. **Breach notice (Art. 33).** 72 hours is correct. I added the "unlikely to result in risk" exception and a "tell Legal first" line, so staff escalate instead of judging it themselves.

Since this goes to ~40 new hires, a quick sign-off from Legal or your DPO is worth it.


## Answer B
Before this goes to 40 people: three of the figures in the draft are wrong, and one of them understates the fine by half. I've fixed them in the version below.

**Slide text (~95 words):**

> **GDPR basics**
>
> GDPR has applied since 25 May 2018. It covers us because we handle personal data of people in the EU.
>
> - **Access requests:** We must respond within **one month**. For complex or numerous requests, we can extend by up to **two more months**, but we must tell the person within the first month.
> - **Data breaches:** We must report to the supervisory authority within **72 hours** of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to put anyone at risk.
> - **Fines:** Processing data without a lawful basis can cost up to **€20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher**.
>
> **When in doubt, ask Legal before collecting new data.**

**What I changed and why**

1. **"30 days" → "one month".** Art. 12(3) GDPR says "within one month of receipt of the request". That is a calendar month, not a fixed 30 days.
2. **"Extend once by another month" → "up to two more months".** Art. 12(3) allows a further two months where necessary, "taking into account the complexity and number of the requests". The person must be told about the extension, with reasons, within the first month.
3. **Fine tier and "lower" → €20m / 4%, "higher".** A breach of the lawful-basis principles (Arts. 5 and 6) falls under Art. 83(5), the upper tier. The €10m / 2% figure is the lower tier in Art. 83(4), which covers things like record-keeping and security obligations. In both tiers the cap is "whichever is higher". "Whichever is lower" is backwards and makes the risk look much smaller than it is.
4. **"EU residents" → "people in the EU".** Art. 3(2) turns on people being in the Union, not on where they are legally resident. If your company is established in the EU, Art. 3(1) applies anyway. Legal may want to state the exact basis.
5. **72 hours:** kept as it was. I added the Art. 33(1) exception for breaches "unlikely to result in a risk", so staff don't assume every incident has to be reported.

I'm confident about these article references. Because this goes out company-wide, it's still worth having Legal/DPO sign off on the slide. They may also want to add your internal breach-reporting contact, which is the part new hires will actually use.
