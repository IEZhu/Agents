It has a huge library but little curation, and flexi dragons are a favourite for render-only uploads and reuploads of other people's designs. A week is plenty of time if you start from a better source and run one small test print first.

I couldn't run live searches here, so I'm not linking specific models or quoting ratings or download counts. Below is where to look and how to filter so you can pick one yourself in about 10 minutes.

## Where to look (in this order)

1. **Printables.com.** This is my first choice for this. Search "articulated dragon" or "flexi dragon", then **sort by Makes**, not by downloads or popularity. Every make is a real photo someone uploaded of a real print, which is exactly the proof you want.
2. **Maker.World (Bambu Lab).** It has lots of flexi animals and a lot of make photos. Many models come with Bambu print profiles. You can ignore those and slice the STL/3MF yourself in Cura or PrusaSlicer for the Ender.
3. **Cults3D / MyMiniFactory.** These sites have many of the best articulated dragon designers, but plenty of the models are paid. A few dollars for a well-tested design is often worth it when you have a deadline.
4. **Thingiverse.** Only use it with **sort by Makes**, and only pick something with lots of make photos.

Designers worth searching by name: **Cinderwing3D** (probably the best-known articulated dragon designer) and **McGybeer** (flexi animals). I'm going from memory on both, not a check done just now, so confirm they're on the platform you're browsing.

## How to tell if it will print right the first time

**Green flags:**
- ✅ **Print-in-place** in the title or description. The whole dragon prints in one go with the joints already formed, with no assembly and no supports.
- ✅ **Many make photos from other people**, not just renders. For a birthday deadline, dozens of makes is a good bar.
- ✅ Make photos that show **ordinary printers** (Ender, Prusa MK3, and so on), not only Bambu X1s.
- ✅ Print settings from the designer (layer height, "no supports", scale).
- ✅ A recent upload, or a designer who still answers comments.

**Red flags:**
- ❌ Only renders, zero makes → risky, skip it.
- ❌ Comments saying "joints fused", "wouldn't break free", or "spaghetti on the tail". **Read the comments.** They're the best QA you'll get.
- ❌ Scaled or "mini" remixes by someone other than the designer. Scaling down shrinks the gaps between joints, and then they fuse.
- ❌ Needs supports. A well-designed flexi dragon shouldn't.

## Ender 3 S1 + PLA: settings that matter

Articulated prints usually fail for three reasons: the first layer, joint clearances, and scaling. They're rarely the model's fault once you've filtered as above.

- **Print at 100% scale**, or only at the scales the designer lists. This matters most.
- **First layer:** level the bed and set Z-offset carefully. Too much squish gives you "elephant's foot", a bulged first layer that welds the bottom joints together. Turn on elephant's foot compensation if your slicer has it (PrusaSlicer does; in Cura it's "Initial Layer Horizontal Expansion", set to about -0.1 to -0.2 mm).
- **Layer height:** 0.2 mm is fine and is what most designs assume.
- **Supports:** off.
- **Infill:** 10–15% is plenty, since it's a toy and not a load-bearing part.
- **Brim:** usually unnecessary. If the tail tips lift, use a brim only on those, or glue stick on the bed.
- **Stringing:** make sure retraction is tuned. Strings between segments are fine and brush off, but heavy stringing can glue joints together.
- **Filament:** plain PLA works well. Silk PLA looks great on dragons, but it's a bit stringier and can fuse joints more easily. If you use silk, run the test first.

## Plan for the week

1. Pick your top 2–3 candidates using the filters above.
2. **Test print a small piece first:** the designer's test file if there is one, or a small flexi model from the same designer. It takes about an hour and tells you if your clearances work.
3. Then print the real dragon. Larger dragons can run 10+ hours (rough estimate), so start mid-week and not the night before.
4. When it comes off the bed, flex each joint gently one by one to break them free. Stiffness at first is normal.
