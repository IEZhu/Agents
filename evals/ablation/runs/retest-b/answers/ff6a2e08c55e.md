You're not imagining it. Thingiverse is the worst place to look for flexi dragons. Much of what turns up there is abandoned, never test-printed, or reuploaded from paid designers, and reuploads often come from broken or rescaled files. Look in these places instead.

## Where to look

1. **Printables.com**: start here. Every model has a "Makes" section with real photos from real printers. Sort by makes, not downloads. A dragon with dozens of FDM makes in PLA is a safe bet.
2. **MakerWorld (Bambu Lab)**: large collection of print-in-place articulated animals, and each one shows print counts and user ratings. Many of these files come with Bambu print profiles. Your Ender 3 S1 can't use those profiles directly, but the STL/3MF slices fine in Cura or PrusaSlicer.
3. **Cults3D**: home of the "real" flexi-dragon designers. As I recall, **Cinderwing3D** and **McGybeer** are the best-known names for articulated dragons and animals (recalled, not verified here, so check their current listings). Some of their designs are paid, usually a few dollars, but they are engineered and test-printed for FDM. With a deadline a week away, paying a few dollars for a proven file beats three failed free prints.

⚠️ If you find a Cinderwing3D-style dragon posted for free on Thingiverse by someone else, it's probably a rip. Beyond the ethics, those copies are often the ones that fail, because they've been rescaled or converted badly.

## How to spot one that will print right the first time

**Green flags ✅**
- **Real make photos**, ideally in PLA on an FDM printer (Ender, Prusa, Bambu), not just the designer's renders
- The listing says **"print-in-place"** and **"no supports"**
- The designer states the **joint clearance** (typically about 0.3–0.4 mm for FDM) and the settings they printed with
- Comments like "printed perfectly, all joints free" from several different people
- Designed to lie flat on the bed, often diagonally to fit a 220×220 bed

**Red flags ❌**
- Only glossy renders and zero makes
- Any mention of resin, or "pre-supported"
- Comments saying "joints fused" or "wouldn't break free" with no reply from the designer
- An uploader who clearly didn't design it (random account, no other work, mismatched photos)

## Getting it right on an Ender 3 S1 with PLA

Most flexi failures come from these settings, not from the model itself:

- **First layer is critical.** If your Z-offset is too low, the first layer squishes out ("elephant's foot") and welds the joints together. Re-level and dial in the Z-offset before this print.
- **Elephant-foot compensation:** in Cura, set "Initial Layer Horizontal Expansion" to around -0.1 to -0.2 mm (recalled setting name; PrusaSlicer calls it "Elephant foot compensation"). This helps a lot with print-in-place joints.
- **Don't scale it down.** Scaling shrinks the joint clearances too, and joints fuse. Scaling up is usually fine. If the model is too big, look for an official smaller version from the designer.
- **Layer height** 0.2 mm, **walls** 2–3, **infill** 10–15%, **supports OFF**.
- **Slow down** to about 40–50 mm/s and run cooling at 100% after the first layer, so joints and overhangs stay clean.
- **Brim/skirt:** a skirt is fine. Avoid a brim; it can grab the thin edges of the wings and tail.
- **After printing,** let it cool fully, then flex each segment gently from head to tail to free it. A stiff joint is normal on the first flex; a fused one won't move at all.
