## Answer A
⚠️ **Stop before you print. My last answer was wrong. Use B, not A.**

**1. License: A can't be used for your shop.**
A is **CC BY-NC-SA**, and "NC" means non-commercial. If you print it and include it with drone kits you sell, that's commercial use, even if the mount is "free" in the box. You'd need written permission from the designer. B is **CC BY 4.0**, which allows commercial use as long as you credit the creator. For example, put "GoPro Mount Remix v3 by [creator], Printables, CC BY 4.0" on your product page or on a card in the kit. Check the creator's name and the license on the model page before you ship, because remixes sometimes carry a different license than the original.

**2. Quality: A is the risky one.**
I put too much weight on downloads. Downloads show that people clicked on a model, not that it prints well. A has 3.4★, only 9 makes and no real print photos, so it gets flagged as risky. B has 4.8★ and 160 makes with photos, so it's been proven on real printers. It also needs no supports, and it comes with STEP and 3MF files, so you can edit it later.

**3. Material: PLA is a poor fit for a drone mount.**
- PLA starts to soften at roughly 55–60 °C. A drone left in a hot car or on a sunny dashboard can reach that, and the mount can then sag or creep.
- PLA is also brittle. A crash or constant vibration can snap the fingers, and your customer loses their GoPro.
- **PETG** is the practical choice: it's tougher, handles heat better and prints almost as easily. **ASA** is better still for outdoor/UV use if your printer has an enclosure.

**4. Settings for a load-bearing mount**
- **Walls matter more than infill.** Use 4–5 perimeters and 5+ top/bottom layers. Infill can stay around 20–30%. At 15% with the default 2 walls, the fingers are the weak point.
- Print so the layer lines run *across* the fingers, not along the pivot's stress direction. B's default orientation is probably already set up this way, so check the photos from makes.
- Layer height 0.2 mm is fine.

**5. Don't print 50 tonight.**
Print 2–3 first. Check the fit on a real GoPro and on your drone's mount, tighten the thumbscrew hard and give it a bend test. Only then start the batch. Finding a tolerance problem after 50 prints costs you a weekend and a spool.

If Monday is tight, a realistic plan: tonight, test prints of B in PETG. Tomorrow, the batch. That still gives you time before Monday's orders.

## ⚠️ Why not A

## Tips for the batch

**Material: PLA is a poor fit for drone mounts.**
- PLA softens around 55–60 °C. A mount sitting in a hot car or in summer sun near a warm drone can creep or droop.
- It's brittle under vibration and in crashes, and the thin mount prongs tend to snap.
- **PETG** is the easy upgrade: tougher, more heat tolerant, and it prints on most machines with no enclosure. **ASA** is better still for sun and heat, but it needs an enclosure. If PLA is all you have tonight, at least test first (see below).

**Strength settings:**
- **15% infill is too low** for a load-bearing part. Mounts are small, so strength comes mostly from walls. I'd use **4 perimeters, 5 top/bottom layers and roughly 40–50% infill** (gyroid or grid). It adds very little print time on a part this size.
- **Orientation matters most.** Parts break along layer lines. Print so the prongs' layers don't sit where the bolt clamping force and vibration would peel them apart. B has 160 makes, so check the creator's notes and the make photos for the orientation that works.

**Before printing 50:**
- **Print 2–3 first.** Check fit on a real GoPro and on your drone mount. Tighten the thumbscrew hard, then try to snap one by hand. This is a lot cheaper than 50 failed parts or returns.
- Check hole tolerances. The M5 bolt should slide through without drilling, and the fingers should mesh without sanding.
- Print in smaller plates (e.g. 10–15 at a time) instead of one giant plate. One failure then doesn't wreck the whole run, and quality stays more consistent.

**Selling note:** describe the part as "GoPro-compatible mount", not as a "GoPro mount", on listings and packaging. "GoPro" is a trademark.

In short: switch to B, print in PETG with 4 walls and higher infill, test a few, then batch the rest.
