## Latest user message
B2B invoicing SaaS, ~1,200 paying customers, churn went from 1.8% to 3.1% monthly over two quarters. I have 3 squads, roughly 18 engineer-weeks each for Q4 (~54 total, and people can move between squads), and three proposals that together need way more than that:

- Growth squad: rebuild onboarding (setup wizard + bank import) — 30 eng-weeks. Data: 40% of churned accounts never connected a bank.
- Core squad: performance + reliability — invoice PDF generation p95 is 9s, plus 3 outages this quarter. 24 eng-weeks.
- Integrations squad: QuickBooks two-way sync, top request in sales calls, 36 eng-weeks.

Exit surveys: 35% "too hard to set up", 25% "missing integrations", 20% "slow/unreliable", 20% price. The CEO wants me to back one flagship bet for Q4. Which one?

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Maps each proposal to the churn evidence (exit survey shares, 40% never connected a bank) rather than judging proposals in isolation
2. Proposes a combined Q4 plan that takes scoped-down parts from more than one proposal (e.g. onboarding with bank import as the flagship, a narrowed reliability fix, a first slice of QuickBooks sync) and checks that the total fits the ~54 eng-weeks available
3. Notes a dependency or overlap between proposals (e.g. integration work overlapping with onboarding/bank import, or reliability problems undermining a new onboarding flow) and uses it in the plan
4. Still gives the CEO a clear answer on the flagship bet and how the rest is framed, and suggests how to measure whether churn improves

## Answer A
**Back onboarding (Growth) as the flagship, but don't pull Core down to zero, and push QuickBooks out of Q4.** Two checks should come first, and they take about a week of analysis. If they come back the wrong way, reliability should be the flagship instead.

## What's at stake

At 1,200 accounts, going from 1.8% to 3.1% monthly churn means losing about 16 extra customers a month. Over a year that's roughly 31% of the base lost instead of about 20%. So the question isn't which project sounds best. It's which one bends that curve fastest for each engineer-week.

## Why onboarding

- **Two independent signals agree.** 35% of exit-survey respondents said "too hard to set up", and 40% of churned accounts never connected a bank. These are probably many of the same people, which makes the signal more credible, not less.
- **It's the biggest stated reason, and you already have the causal story.** An invoicing tool with no bank connection never becomes part of the customer's workflow, so it's easy to cancel.
- **It fits your capacity.** 30 of 54 weeks leaves room for a real reliability investment alongside it.

## Why not the other two as the flagship

- **QuickBooks sync (36 weeks)** is the weakest retention bet. "Top request in sales calls" is an acquisition signal from prospects, not a retention signal from customers. "Missing integrations" (25%) probably covers several integrations, not only QuickBooks. Two-way accounting sync also tends to overrun its estimate, because of conflict resolution and edge cases in QuickBooks' data model. At 36 weeks it would take two-thirds of your capacity on your least certain number. Defer it, and spend a few weeks on scoping, or on a one-way export or partner connector, to test demand.
- **Core (24 weeks)** is the real challenger, not the loser. See the next section.

## The flaw in the obvious answer

Setup friction is likely a **chronic** problem. Your churn **rose** over two quarters, so something **changed**. Onboarding only explains the rise if onboarding got worse or you're signing up more (or different) customers. Meanwhile you had **3 outages this quarter**, which is exactly the kind of change that makes churn jump. The 20% "slow/unreliable" figure may also undercount it, since people who leave after an outage often pick "price" or skip the survey.

Also bear in mind the timing. Onboarding fixes only help **new cohorts**, so their effect on overall churn will show up slowly, probably not visibly before year-end. Reliability affects the whole base right away. Tell the CEO this upfront so a Q4 bet isn't judged on Q4 churn.

## Check before you commit (days, not weeks)

1. **Bank-connection rate among retained vs. churned accounts.** "40% of churned never connected" means nothing without the baseline. If 40% of retained accounts also never connected, the signal disappears. If retained accounts are around 85–90% connected, onboarding is confirmed.
2. **Churn by tenure.** Is the increase mostly in accounts under 90 days old (points to onboarding) or in established accounts (points to reliability or integrations)?
3. **Churn timing vs. outage dates.** Look for spikes in cancellations in the 2–4 weeks after each outage.
4. **Has the acquisition mix changed?** New segments, or sales promising a QuickBooks integration that doesn't exist yet, would change the picture.

**Decision rule:** if the increase is concentrated among new accounts and bank connection strongly separates retained from churned, go with onboarding. If it's concentrated among established accounts and follows the outages, make Core the flagship and cut onboarding down to the bank-import piece.

## Proposed Q4 allocation (assuming the checks confirm onboarding)

| Workstream | Eng-weeks | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding (flagship) | ~30 | Ship bank import first, since that's where the 40% signal points; the wizard follows |
| Reliability floor | ~18 | Root-cause and fix whatever caused the 3 outages first; PDF p95 second |
| QuickBooks | ~6 | Scoping, a one-way export or partner option, and customer interviews to confirm this is a churn driver and not just a sales ask |

Reliability here is a floor, not a bet. Another quarter with 3 outages will cancel out whatever onboarding gains.

**Metrics to agree with the CEO now:**
- Leading: share of new accounts that connect a bank within 14 days.
- Lagging: 90-day churn for Q4 signup cohorts.
- Guardrail: at most 1 outage per quarter.

## Answer B
**Back onboarding as the flagship, but first take reliability off the table as a bet.** Fund the outage work before anything else, because it's table stakes. Scope onboarding down to the bank connection, and say no to QuickBooks two-way sync for Q4. Before you lock this in, spend one week confirming it with the data cuts below. Your current evidence points toward onboarding but doesn't prove it.

## Why onboarding, and why it's not a slam dunk

**For onboarding:**
- It's the largest stated churn reason (35% say "too hard to set up"). It also lines up with a behavioural signal: 40% of churned accounts never connected a bank. When survey data and product data agree, that's worth more than either one alone.
- An invoicing tool with no bank connected probably never reaches its core value, which is getting paid and reconciling. That makes bank connection a plausible activation event.

**The two holes in that case:**
1. **The 40% has no base rate.** If 35–40% of *retained* accounts also never connected a bank, the stat tells you nothing. If only 10% of retained accounts didn't connect, it's a strong signal. You need that comparison.
2. **Churn *rose* over two quarters, so something changed.** Onboarding friction probably existed before the rise. The three outages happened *this quarter*. So it's quite possible that reliability explains more of the *increase* than onboarding does, even if onboarding explains more of the baseline. Also, onboarding fixes only help new cohorts, and the payoff shows up months later. Reliability fixes protect all ~1,200 accounts right away.

That's why I wouldn't frame this as onboarding vs. reliability. Invoicing is mission-critical for your customers' cash flow. Three outages in a quarter are a retention risk you can't choose to skip.

## Why not QuickBooks two-way sync (this quarter)

- **36 of 54 eng-weeks is two-thirds of capacity on one project**, and two-way accounting sync is the kind of work that usually overruns: conflict resolution, edge cases, API limits. If it slips, the quarter slips with it.
- **"Top request in sales calls" is an acquisition signal, not a retention one.** Your problem is churn. And the 25% who said "missing integrations" didn't necessarily mean QuickBooks. You don't know that split yet.
- Cheaper ways to test demand: a one-way export, or a connector through an integration platform. Also check whether QuickBooks users churn more than non-users.

## Suggested Q4 allocation (for engineers to challenge)

| Work | Eng-weeks | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reliability floor** (Core) | ~12–14 | Root-cause and fix the outage classes first. For PDF p95, look for a cheap win (e.g., generating PDFs async/in the background) before a rewrite. Ask Core what the *minimum* is to stop outages. |
| **Onboarding flagship** (Growth + people moved in) | ~30 | Bank import and time-to-first-connection first; the wizard polish second. If the scope has to shrink, cut the wizard, not the bank import. |
| **Integrations discovery / buffer** | ~10 | Find out which integrations "missing integrations" means, plus a lightweight QuickBooks test. Doubles as a buffer if onboarding overruns. |

These splits are my estimates, not engineering estimates. Validate them with the squad leads before you present them.

## What to check in week 1 (before committing)

1. **Churn by tenure cohort**, before vs. after the rise. If the increase is concentrated in accounts under 90 days old, go with onboarding. If it's in tenured accounts, reliability or competitive pressure is the bigger lever, and you should rebalance.
2. **Bank-connection rate: retained vs. churned accounts.** This supplies the missing base rate.
3. **Churn among accounts hit by the outages or heavy PDF users** vs. everyone else.
4. **Breakdown of "missing integrations"** by the integration named.
5. **The 20% who cited price.** Did you change pricing, or did a competitor launch? None of the three proposals addresses this, so flag it to the CEO separately.

## Metrics to commit to

- **Onboarding:** % of new accounts that connect a bank within 7 days (leading indicator), and 90-day churn of Q4 signup cohorts (lagging).
- **Reliability:** zero Sev-1 outages in Q4, and a PDF p95 target the Core squad agrees is achievable.
- **Overall:** a monthly churn target back toward ~2%. Be honest that most of the onboarding impact will show up in Q1, not by the end of Q4.

## One thing that costs zero engineering

Onboarding fixes won't reach existing accounts that never connected a bank. Have CS or success run a white-glove bank-connection push for those accounts now. That's the fastest retention win available, and it also tests the onboarding hypothesis: if connected accounts stop churning, you've validated the flagship before it ships.

**How to pitch it to the CEO:** "Our flagship is fixing activation, because it's the biggest churn driver we can see. We're funding reliability as a floor, not a bet, because outages put the whole base at risk. QuickBooks goes to discovery until we know it moves retention and not just sales demos."
