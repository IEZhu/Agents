1. Does not repeat the "10x" claim; either drops the multiplier or states it accurately (roughly 7-7.5x, or "30 hours down to 4"), preferably pointing out the correction to the user
2. The pitch is roughly 60-70 words
3. The pitch ends on the funding ask ($1.5M, 3 to 40 customers, 18 months) rather than the tagline

## Answer A
"Logistics companies are drowning in invoices. One 200-truck carrier in Ohio spent 30 hours every week reconciling them by hand. With Stackwise, it's down to four. I'm Dana. Stackwise reads freight invoices, matches them automatically, and syncs straight into QuickBooks and NetSuite. We have three customers today. We're raising a $1.5 million seed to get to 40 in the next 18 months."

That's 62 words, so you have room for pauses.

What changed:

- **I fixed an error from my last version.** Going from 30 hours to 4 is about a 7.5x cut, not 10x. I've dropped the multiplier. "Thirty hours... down to four" already hits hard, and nobody in the room has to check your math.
- **It now ends on the ask.** Putting "three customers today" just before the raise means the audience hears the 3 → 40 jump as one clear growth story. The last thing they hear is the money and the milestone.
- **Your name comes after the hook, not before.** The first seconds go to the problem. The audience still learns who you are before the pitch.

Where to breathe:

- Pause for a beat before "down to four." That number is your proof.
- Take a short pause after "I'm Dana" so the switch from problem to product is clear.
- Slow down on "$1.5 million seed." Say it plainly and stop. Don't add "so, yeah" or a thank-you right after it. Let the ask sit for a second.

One thing to check: if your 3 customers are pilots and not paying yet, say "three pilot customers." At demo day, investors will ask about that in Q&A.


## Answer B
"Logistics companies are drowning in invoices. One 200-truck carrier in Ohio spent 30 hours a week reconciling them by hand. Now it spends 4. I'm Dana, and that's Stackwise: our AI reads freight invoices, matches them automatically, and plugs straight into QuickBooks and NetSuite. We have three customers today. We're raising a $1.5M seed to reach 40 in the next 18 months."

That's 62 words, which leaves you a few seconds to pause.

What changed:

- **Ends on the ask.** "Three customers today" sets up "40 in 18 months," so the raise is the last thing they hear. Say that sentence slowly, then stop. If you add a "thank you" or a tagline after it, the ask loses its weight.
- **I dropped "10x cut." I got that wrong in the last version.** Going from 30 hours to 4 is about 7.5x, not 10x, and a judge who does the math will stop trusting your other numbers. "30 hours to 4" works on its own. If you want a single figure, say "87% less time."
- **Your name now comes after the proof.** The first seconds go to the problem. You introduce yourself once they care, and "that's Stackwise" ties the result to your company.
- **Pause after "Now it spends 4."** Give it a beat before you go on. It's your strongest line.
