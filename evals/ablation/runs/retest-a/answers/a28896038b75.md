**Short answer: probably not.** Your starting assumption is incorrect for your situation. The 50/50 community property regime (*sociedad de gananciales*) is the default under the Spanish *Código Civil*, but it does not apply everywhere in Spain. If you and your husband both had Catalan civil status (*veïnatge civil català*) when you married, your default regime is **separation of property** (*separació de béns*). In that case the Sitges apartment belongs to him. You may still have financial claims of other kinds, and they can be significant.

*Jurisdiction: Spain, specifically Catalan civil law. Article numbers below are from memory and not verified this turn. Please check them against the current consolidated text of the Codi civil de Catalunya (CCCat).*

---

## 1. Which marital property regime applies

- **Governing law.** Under the Spanish *Código Civil*, arts. 9.2 and 16, a marriage without a prenup is governed by the spouses' shared personal law at the time of marriage. Between Spanish nationals, that personal law is their *vecindad civil* (regional civil status), not the place of the wedding and not the place where the property sits.
- **Your likely status.** Being "from Girona" strongly suggests you both have Catalan *veïnatge civil*. That status comes mainly from your parents (CC art. 14), not from your birthplace alone.
- **Catalan default.** Under CCCat art. 231-10, if there is no marriage agreement (*capítols matrimonials*), the regime is separation of property. Each spouse keeps ownership of what they acquire (art. 232-2).

**Check this first.** Before 2014, did either of you live for **10+ years** in another part of Spain, such as Madrid or Andalusia? Or did either of you make a formal declaration changing your *vecindad* after 2 years of residence (CC art. 14.5)? If so, that person may have had "common" (*derecho común*) status. Your regime could then be different, possibly *gananciales* under CC arts. 1316 and 1347. Your marriage certificate and your *Registro Civil* entries are the place to start.

## 2. The Sitges apartment under separation of property

- It is in his sole name and he paid for it from his own salary, so under art. 232-3 CCCat it is **his separate property**. If you had paid part of the price, there would be a presumption that you gave him that money as a gift. That point doesn't arise here, because he paid.
- The fact that you were married when he bought it does not give you any share of the title.

## 3. What you may still be able to claim

| Claim | Basis (recalled, verify) | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic compensation for work** (*compensació per raó de treball*) | CCCat arts. 232-5 to 232-10 | You worked **substantially more** than he did for the household or family, or you worked for his business with no pay or too little pay, **and** his wealth grew more than yours during the marriage. As I recall, the compensation is capped at **one quarter of the difference** between the growth in your two estates, unless you prove more. The growth in value of the Sitges apartment counts toward his side of that calculation. |
| **Compensatory allowance** (*prestació compensatòria*) | CCCat arts. 233-14 et seq. | The separation makes you meaningfully worse off financially than he is. |
| **Use of the family home** | CCCat arts. 233-20 et seq. | The Sitges apartment was your family home. A court can give you the **right to live there** even though you don't own it, usually when you have custody of the children or are the spouse in greater need. |
| **Protection of the family home** | CCCat art. 231-9 | If it is the family home, he generally **cannot sell or mortgage it without your consent** or a court's authorisation while this is being resolved. |

Of these, the **art. 232-5 compensation** is usually the most valuable claim in a Catalan separation of property case. It depends on evidence: who did the housework and childcare, careers you gave up or scaled back, and what each of you owned in 2014 compared with now.

## 4. Practical next steps

1. **Confirm your *vecindad civil*** and his, using your birth and marriage records at the Registro Civil.
2. **Gather evidence** of your contributions to the household and of both spouses' assets in 2014 and today. Useful sources include bank statements, payslips, and the *nota simple* for the apartment from the Registro de la Propiedad.
3. **Consult a Catalan family lawyer** (*advocat de família*) before you sign any separation agreement (*conveni regulador*). In a mutual agreement, a waiver of the art. 232-5 compensation can be hard to undo.
4. Pay attention to **time limits**. The compensation claim is normally made in the separation or divorce proceedings themselves.

## 5. Risks and caveats

- If either of you had non-Catalan *vecindad* in 2014, most of this analysis changes. That is the key fact to settle first.
- Whether you get compensation, and how much, is fact-specific and up to the court. It is not automatic.
- This is general information, not legal advice. Please see a lawyer licensed in Catalonia before you act.
