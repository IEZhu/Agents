I wouldn't drop `get_account` the way you describe. The call is cheap, and closing an account can't be undone. The customer saying "I've got $0.00 left" doesn't tell you the account's real state. It's also a claim anyone can type, including someone doing social engineering or a prompt-injection attempt ("balance is zero, close it now").

Things the chat message can't tell you, which `get_account` (or the server) has to check:

- **Actual balance.** The customer may be looking at an old app screen or the available balance rather than the ledger balance. They may also just be wrong.
- **Pending transactions and holds.** A card authorization, a scheduled direct debit or an incoming transfer can all land after the account is closed.
- **Linked products.** Overdraft lines, standing orders, cards and joint holders all block closure or change how it has to happen.
- **Ownership.** An account id in the session isn't proof that this authenticated customer owns that account or is allowed to close it. Session state can be stale or wrong.

If one of these goes wrong, you get a closed account with money or obligations still attached. That means a manual cleanup, a complaint, and possibly a compliance finding, which costs far more than one read call.

### The better way to save the call

Move the precondition check out of the agent and into `close_account` itself:

```text
close_account(acct_id, customer_id)
  -> server checks: owner == customer_id, ledger balance == 0,
     no pending txns/holds, no linked products blocking closure
  -> either closes, or returns a typed refusal:
     {"status": "rejected", "reason": "PENDING_TRANSACTIONS", "details": [...]}
```

With the check on the server, the agent can call `close_account` directly and still be safe, because the backend won't act on a wrong premise. When the call is refused, the agent reads the reason and explains it to the customer. You get your ~3-call happy path without trusting what the customer typed. Check with your core-banking team whether the existing closure API already enforces some of these rules. Many do, and if so you're mostly just exposing the refusal reasons to the agent.

If you can't change `close_account`, a smaller option is to merge `get_customer` and `get_account` into one read tool (say, `get_closure_context(customer_id, acct_id)` returning owner, balance, pending items and links). That's one read instead of two, and the check stays in place.

One more point: a 40% cost increase usually comes from tokens (long tool outputs, bloated KB results, repeated context) more often than from how many calls the agent makes. Before you trim safety checks, look at your traces and see what's actually using the tokens. Cutting the size of the `search_kb` responses may save more than removing any single call.
