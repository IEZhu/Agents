## 1. Wealth tax (IP): €0 to pay, but a return is still likely due

- IP is a state tax (Ley 19/1991) that is ceded to the regions. Madrid uses its regional powers to grant a 100% rebate on the tax due. The tax still exists. The rebate simply cancels the amount owed.
- **Filing obligation, Art. 37 Ley 19/1991** (recalled, not verified; confirm in the current text). You must file if either:
  - the tax due, after deductions and rebates, is positive, **or**
  - even if it isn't, the gross value of your assets and rights (*bienes y derechos*) exceeds **€2,000,000**.
- Your gross assets are around €4.8M, which is well above €2M. **You very likely must file Modelo 714 even though you pay €0.**

## 2. ITSGF: the "solidarity" tax Madrid's rebate doesn't reach

- **Ley 38/2022, Art. 3**, created a state tax on net wealth above €3M. Regions have no power to rebate it. The IP you actually paid is credited against it, but in Madrid you pay €0 IP, so nothing is credited and the full ITSGF is due. Madrid was the intended target.
- It was introduced as "temporary" for 2022 and 2023. As I recall, **RDL 8/2023** extended it for later years pending a review of regional wealth taxation, and the Constitutional Court upheld it against Madrid's challenge (**STC 149/2023**). Both points are recalled and not verified here. Please check that it applies to your tax year.

## 3. Modelo 720: the Swiss portfolio

- Spanish residents must disclose foreign accounts, securities and real estate when each category exceeds **€50,000** (Additional Provision 18 LGT; Modelo 720). A Swiss bank account holding ETFs clearly qualifies.
- Penalties were reduced after **CJEU C-788/19 (27 Jan 2022)** and the reform in Ley 5/2022. They now follow the general LGT regime and are no longer the old disproportionate fines. Filing late is still a real cost.
- The ETFs also matter for **IRPF**: dividends and capital gains are taxed in Spain, and the tax treatment of non-Spanish funds/ETFs differs from Spanish ones.

## Caveats and questions to settle with a Spanish adviser

- **Beckham regime (Art. 93 LIRPF).** If you opted into it, the answer changes a lot. Beckham taxpayers are taxed for IP/ITSGF only on **Spanish-located assets** (*obligación real*) and are exempt from Modelo 720. The Swiss ETFs would drop out, and the flat alone is likely below the thresholds. Few people who move for investment reasons qualify, but check.
- **Your residence date for 2025**, as explained in section 4.
- **Valuation rules.** Listed ETFs are valued at the 31 Dec market value. The flat is valued at the highest of cadastral value, acquisition price, or value assessed for another tax.
- **Your accountant "back home"** is probably not familiar with the ITSGF. It is a very Spain-specific trap. Use a Spanish *asesor fiscal* for these returns.
- All figures and deadlines above come from memory and have not been checked against current AEAT publications. Tax rates, thresholds and the ITSGF's extension should be confirmed for your tax year.
