## 1. Which law governs your marriage

- Spain has several civil-law systems. Catalonia has its own **Codi Civil de Catalunya (CCCat)**, which governs family and property matters for people with Catalan *veïnatge civil* (*vecindad civil*, regional civil status).
- **Art. 9.2 CC** (recalled, not verified here): the effects of a marriage, including the property regime, are governed by the spouses' common personal law at the time of the marriage. For two Spaniards, that is their common *vecindad civil*.
- **Caveat:** *vecindad civil* depends on your parents' *vecindad* and on where you have lived (Art. 14 CC), not simply on being born in Girona. If either of you had a different *vecindad* in 2014, for example from growing up or living for many years in Madrid or Andalusia, the answer could change. Check this first.

## 2. The default regime under Catalan law

- **Art. 231-10 CCCat** (recalled): if there are no marriage articles (*capítols matrimonials*), the regime is **separació de béns**.
- Under separation of property, each spouse owns what they acquire, and property bought for value belongs to the spouse who holds the title (**Art. 232-3 CCCat**, recalled). An apartment registered only in his name and paid only from his income is his.
- Where you married (Barcelona) and where the flat is (Sitges) don't change this. What matters is your personal law.

## 3. What you may still be entitled to

These claims matter a great deal in Catalan separations.

1. **Compensation for work (*compensació econòmica per raó de treball*), Art. 232-5 CCCat.** If you worked for the household, or for his business, substantially more than he did, and this left his assets growing more than yours, you can claim compensation. As I recall, it is capped at **one quarter of the difference between the growth in each spouse's net assets** during the marriage, and courts can go below the cap. The flat's value counts toward his growth, so this is how the apartment can indirectly benefit you. It has to be raised in the separation or divorce proceedings, or it may be lost.
2. **Spousal support (*prestació compensatòria*), Art. 233-14 CCCat and following.** This applies if the break-up leaves you clearly worse off financially than during the marriage.
3. **Use of the family home, Art. 233-20 CCCat.** If the Sitges flat is the family home, a court can give you the right to live in it, especially if you have custody of minor children, even though he owns it. This is a right of use, not ownership.
4. **Your own contributions.** If you paid part of the price, the mortgage or major renovations, there may be grounds for a partial ownership or reimbursement claim. Keep the bank records.
5. **Jointly held or unclear assets.** Items whose ownership can't be proven are presumed to belong to both spouses in equal shares (Art. 232-4 CCCat, recalled).
