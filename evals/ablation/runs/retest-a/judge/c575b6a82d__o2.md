But at about €4.8M you are almost certainly within scope of the separate **state** "solidarity" tax on large fortunes, and Madrid's rebate does not reduce it. You probably also have filing obligations even where no tax ends up payable. Since you became resident last year, some deadlines for that first year may already have passed, so this is worth sorting out now.

Jurisdiction: Spain (state law plus the Comunidad de Madrid). I haven't been able to check current texts or rates in this conversation, so treat the figures below as recalled rather than verified, and confirm them with a Spanish *asesor fiscal*.

## 1. Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio (IP): nothing to pay, but you probably still have to file

- IP is a state tax (Ley 19/1991) whose yield is ceded to the regions. Madrid applies a **100% bonificación**, so the tax due is €0. This is the part your accountant has right.
- **Filing is a separate question.** As I recall, art. 37 Ley 19/1991 requires a return (Modelo 714) if the tax due is positive after deductions and rebates, **or** if the gross value of your assets exceeds **€2,000,000**, even when the rebate brings the tax to zero. At €4.8M gross you are over that threshold, so you would have to file a zero-tax return. (Recalled, not verified. Confirm the current wording of art. 37.)

## 2. Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas (ITSGF): the likely real liability

- **Legal basis:** art. 3 of Ley 38/2022. This is a **state** tax, not a regional one, so the Comunidad de Madrid has no power to rebate it. It was introduced specifically to offset regional wealth tax rebates like Madrid's.
- **Mechanics:** it largely mirrors the IP rules (same asset valuation, same €700,000 personal allowance, and the main-home exemption of up to €300,000). Any IP you actually paid is deducted from it. In Madrid the IP paid is €0, so **none of the solidarity tax is offset.**
- **Scale** (recalled, not verified):

| Taxable base | Rate |
|---|---|
| up to €3,000,000 | 0% |
| €3,000,000 – ~€5,348,000 | 1.7% |
| ~€5,348,000 – ~€10,696,000 | 2.1% |
| above ~€10,696,000 | 3.5% |

- **Status:** it was first enacted as temporary (2022 and 2023). As I recall, it was later extended until the wealth tax is reviewed as part of the reform of regional financing, and it has since been applied year by year. Please check it still applies for your tax year, because this is exactly the kind of rule that changes.

**Rough illustration.** This assumes your €4.8M is net of debts, all of it is taxable, and the Chamberí flat is your habitual residence worth at least €300,000:

- €4,800,000 − €300,000 (main home) − €700,000 (allowance) ≈ **€3,800,000 taxable base**
- (€3,800,000 − €3,000,000) × 1.7% ≈ **€13,600 a year**

That is an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a calculation.

**A possible mitigation to check:** there is a joint cap under which IRPF plus IP plus ITSGF generally can't exceed 60% of your IRPF taxable base. The cap can't cut the wealth taxes by more than 80%, so at least 20% is always payable. If your portfolio produces little *taxable* income (for example accumulating ETFs you haven't sold), this cap could reduce the bill substantially. Ask your adviser to model it.

**Filing:** ITSGF is filed on **Modelo 718**. As I recall, the window is July of the following year, a separate return from the IP return filed in the spring. (Recalled, not verified.)

## 3. Modelo 720: your Swiss portfolio almost certainly triggers it

- As a Spanish tax resident, you must file an informational return on assets held abroad if a category exceeds **€50,000**. Securities held with a Swiss bank fall squarely within the "securities / investment funds" category. Foreign bank accounts are a separate category with the same threshold.
- The return is due by **31 March** of the year after the first year you meet the threshold. After that, you only file again if a category's value increases by more than €20,000 or you close out a position.
- After the CJEU ruling in case C‑788/19 (January 2022), Spain abolished the old disproportionate penalties. Late filing is now sanctioned under the ordinary rules, which are much milder but still real.

## 4. One question that changes the analysis: did you opt into the "Beckham" regime?

If you elected the special regime for inbound workers (art. 93 LIRPF), you are taxed for IP and ITSGF purposes only on assets **located in Spain**. That would take the Swiss portfolio out of both taxes, leaving roughly the Chamberí flat, which would probably fall under the thresholds. The election has to be made within six months of starting work in Spain and has conditions, so check whether you or your employer filed it (Modelo 149).

## Answer B
**I can't confirm that. Your accountant is wrong on two counts.** Madrid does not have "zero wealth tax". It gives a 100% *bonificación* (rebate) on the regional Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio (IP), and that rebate has limits:

1. **You probably still have to file the IP return (Modelo 714)**, even though Madrid's rebate brings the tax to €0.
2. **You are probably liable for the state solidarity tax on large fortunes** (Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas, ITSGF, Modelo 718). It was designed specifically to reach net wealth above €3M in regions like Madrid. At about €4.8M, you are likely inside it.

There is also a third obligation your accountant didn't mention: **Modelo 720**, the foreign-asset disclosure, for the Swiss portfolio.

This analysis covers **Spain, Comunidad de Madrid**, and assumes you are a full Spanish tax resident (*obligación personal*) who is **not** under the Beckham regime. See the caveats at the end on that point.

---

## 1. Wealth tax (IP): €0 to pay, but a return is still likely due

- IP is a state tax (Ley 19/1991) that is ceded to the regions. Madrid uses its regional powers to grant a 100% rebate on the tax due. The tax still exists. The rebate simply cancels the amount owed.
- **Filing obligation, Art. 37 Ley 19/1991** (recalled, not verified; confirm in the current text). You must file if either:
  - the tax due, after deductions and rebates, is positive, **or**
  - even if it isn't, the gross value of your assets and rights (*bienes y derechos*) exceeds **€2,000,000**.
- Your gross assets are around €4.8M, which is well above €2M. **You very likely must file Modelo 714 even though you pay €0.**

## 2. ITSGF: the "solidarity" tax Madrid's rebate doesn't reach

- **Ley 38/2022, Art. 3**, created a state tax on net wealth above €3M. Regions have no power to rebate it. The IP you actually paid is credited against it, but in Madrid you pay €0 IP, so nothing is credited and the full ITSGF is due. Madrid was the intended target.
- It was introduced as "temporary" for 2022 and 2023. As I recall, **RDL 8/2023** extended it for later years pending a review of regional wealth taxation, and the Constitutional Court upheld it against Madrid's challenge (**STC 149/2023**). Both points are recalled and not verified here. Please check that it applies to your tax year.
- **Rough calculation for you.** The figures below are recalled rates and thresholds; confirm them before relying on them.

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Net wealth | ~€4,800,000 |
| Less primary-home exemption (Chamberí flat, if it is your *vivienda habitual*), up to | −€300,000 |
| Less *mínimo exento* (exempt minimum) | −€700,000 |
| Taxable base | ~€3,800,000 |
| Tranche €0 to €3,000,000 | 0% |
| Tranche €3,000,000 to €5,347,998 | 1.7% on ~€800,000 ≈ **€13,600** |

- The combined cap (IRPF + IP/ITSGF can't exceed 60% of your IRPF taxable base, with the reduction limited to 80%) may reduce this a little. With an ETF portfolio that produces modest income, it usually doesn't help much.
- **Probable result: roughly €10k–14k a year of ITSGF.** That is an estimate. The exact amount depends on how the ETFs are valued at 31 Dec, any debts, and whether the flat qualifies as your primary home.

## 3. Modelo 720: the Swiss portfolio

- Spanish residents must disclose foreign accounts, securities and real estate when each category exceeds **€50,000** (Additional Provision 18 LGT; Modelo 720). A Swiss bank account holding ETFs clearly qualifies.
- Penalties were reduced after **CJEU C-788/19 (27 Jan 2022)** and the reform in Ley 5/2022. They now follow the general LGT regime and are no longer the old disproportionate fines. Filing late is still a real cost.
- The ETFs also matter for **IRPF**: dividends and capital gains are taxed in Spain, and the tax treatment of non-Spanish funds/ETFs differs from Spanish ones.
