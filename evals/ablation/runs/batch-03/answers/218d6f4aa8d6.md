I'd drop the lead you've chosen. "We've already spent 8 months and $400k" is the weakest argument you have, and it could sink the rest of the memo. That money is spent whichever way leadership decides. Finance will recognize it as the sunk-cost fallacy right away, and once they do, they'll read everything after it as motivated reasoning.

The only question leadership can act on is this: **from today, which path gets all 11 models onto a feature store that meets 15ms p99 for the least money and risk?** A strong memo answers that question. The spend history can appear, but only as evidence of what you've learned and built, not as a reason in itself.

Before the draft, here are three facts Finance will raise whether or not you do. The memo has to answer them.

- **Pace.** 8 months, 60% done, 2 of 11 models. At the current rate the remaining 40% is about 5–6 more months and about $250–300k ($400k ÷ 8 ≈ $50k/month × ~5.5). Software estimates usually slip, so present that as a floor.
- **Latency.** 40ms against a 15ms target is almost 3x over. The gap isn't closed by "finishing" the remaining 40%. It is its own engineering problem. If you can't say why it's 40ms and what brings it down, this is where the memo fails.
- **The managed option isn't free either.** Moving to Tecton or Databricks means license cost, migration work, re-validating the 2 models already live, and ongoing integration. That's your strongest honest ground, but only if you put numbers on it.
