Not yet. The June newsletter doesn't say they fixed it, and both notices can be true at the same time without anything having changed.

**Why "meets all current standards" doesn't mean the PFAS is gone**

- **The new federal PFAS limits aren't enforceable yet.** EPA finalized MCLs of 4 ppt each for PFOA and PFOS in April 2024. As I recall, utilities have until 2029 to comply, and in 2025 EPA said it planned to push the PFOA/PFOS deadline to 2031. I haven't checked those dates here, so confirm them before relying on them. Either way, a utility can be "above the new MCL" (March) and also "meeting all current standards" (June) with the same water, because the new limit isn't a violation yet.
- **Compliance is judged on averages, not single samples.** Even once the limits apply, compliance is based on a running annual average across monitoring results. One sample at 11 ppt isn't a violation by itself.
- **The June wording avoids the question.** It doesn't mention PFAS, new results, or treatment. "Well Field 2 remains part of our diversified supply portfolio" sounds like the well is still in use, possibly blended with other sources. It doesn't say it was treated.
- **The timeline is tight.** Getting a granular activated carbon system designed, approved and running usually takes a year or more. The utility was still "evaluating" options in March, so having treatment running by June seems unlikely, though not impossible.

If they had actually fixed it, they would very likely have said so. "PFAS treatment now online at Well Field 2" is the kind of news utilities like to announce.

**How to settle it (one email or call)**

Ask the utility:
1. What were the most recent PFOA and PFOS results at Well Field 2, and on what date?
2. Is treatment installed and running? If not, when is it expected?
3. Does water from Well Field 2 reach my address, and is it blended with other sources?

Also look for their annual Consumer Confidence Report (water quality report), which usually comes out around midyear. It should list PFAS results if they monitored for them. Your state drinking water program may also post PFAS sampling data.

**You can stop filtering if** recent results at Well Field 2 are below 4 ppt for both, treatment is running, or your address doesn't get water from that well field.

**One more check on the pitcher.** Many filters sold as "RO pitchers" aren't true reverse osmosis. Check whether it's certified to NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 for PFOA/PFOS reduction. If it isn't, it may be less protective than you think. If it is and you want less hassle, an under-sink RO unit or a certified carbon block filter is much easier to live with.
