I'd drop the sunk-cost argument as your lead. Finance will see through it, and it hurts your credibility on everything that follows. The $400k is spent whether you continue or switch, so it can't be a reason for either choice. Leadership is really asking one thing: **from today, which path gets all 11 models onto a feature store that meets our latency target, at lower cost and risk?** If you lead with "we'd be writing off $400k," a finance partner will hear "we don't have a forward-looking case."

Your facts also make the switch cheaper right now. Only 2 models depend on the system, so migration cost is at its lowest point. You need to address that head-on, not avoid it.

The strongest honest case is a **time-boxed, gated continuation**: fund a short milestone with pass/fail criteria, and run a managed-option evaluation alongside it. That gives leadership a decision they can defend. Here's a draft.

| Criterion | Continue if | Switch if |
|---|---|---|

**Risks we're accounting for**

**Bottom line:** We're not asking for open-ended funding. We're asking for [8] weeks to show that in-house beats managed on latency, coverage and cost. If it doesn't, we switch while migration is still cheap.
