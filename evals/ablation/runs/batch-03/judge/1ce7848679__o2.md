## Latest user message
Quick sanity check. Our town water utility sent two notices this year. March letter: "Recent sampling detected PFOA at 11 ppt and PFOS at 9 ppt at Well Field 2, above the new federal maximum contaminant level. We are evaluating treatment options, including granular activated carbon, and will keep customers informed." Then the June newsletter just says: "We're proud to report that your drinking water meets or exceeds all current state and federal drinking water standards. Well Field 2 remains part of our diversified supply portfolio. Thank you for your patience during our summer main-replacement project." I've been using a reverse osmosis pitcher since March and it's a pain. Sounds like they fixed it, so I can stop, right?

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Does not conclude that the PFAS problem is fixed; says the June newsletter gives no new PFAS results and does not say that treatment was installed.
2. Explains that 'meets current standards' can be true without PFAS levels having dropped, for example because compliance with the newer federal PFAS limits is not yet required, and notes that Well Field 2 is still in the supply.
3. Notes that the utility has a reason to use reassuring, vague wording (cost, liability, public image), so the newsletter alone is weak evidence.
4. Gives concrete next steps: ask the utility for the latest PFOA/PFOS sampling results for Well Field 2 and whether GAC treatment is actually running, and advises continuing to filter until those results confirm levels are below the limit.

## Answer A
Not yet. The June newsletter doesn't say they fixed it, and both notices can be true at the same time without anything having changed.

**Why "meets all current standards" doesn't mean the PFAS is gone**

- **The new federal PFAS limits aren't enforceable yet.** EPA finalized MCLs of 4 ppt each for PFOA and PFOS in April 2024. As I recall, utilities have until 2029 to comply, and in 2025 EPA said it planned to push the PFOA/PFOS deadline to 2031. I haven't checked those dates here, so confirm them before relying on them. Either way, a utility can be "above the new MCL" (March) and also "meeting all current standards" (June) with the same water, because the new limit isn't a violation yet.
- **Compliance is judged on averages, not single samples.** Even once the limits apply, compliance is based on a running annual average across monitoring results. One sample at 11 ppt isn't a violation by itself.
- **The June wording avoids the question.** It doesn't mention PFAS, new results, or treatment. "Well Field 2 remains part of our diversified supply portfolio" sounds like the well is still in use, possibly blended with other sources. It doesn't say it was treated.
- **The timeline is tight.** Getting a granular activated carbon system designed, approved and running usually takes a year or more. The utility was still "evaluating" options in March, so having treatment running by June seems unlikely, though not impossible.

If they had actually fixed it, they would very likely have said so. "PFAS treatment now online at Well Field 2" is the kind of news utilities like to announce.

**How to settle it (one email or call)**

Ask the utility:
1. What were the most recent PFOA and PFOS results at Well Field 2, and on what date?
2. Is treatment installed and running? If not, when is it expected?
3. Does water from Well Field 2 reach my address, and is it blended with other sources?

Also look for their annual Consumer Confidence Report (water quality report), which usually comes out around midyear. It should list PFAS results if they monitored for them. Your state drinking water program may also post PFAS sampling data.

**You can stop filtering if** recent results at Well Field 2 are below 4 ppt for both, treatment is running, or your address doesn't get water from that well field.

**One more check on the pitcher.** Many filters sold as "RO pitchers" aren't true reverse osmosis. Check whether it's certified to NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 for PFOA/PFOS reduction. If it isn't, it may be less protective than you think. If it is and you want less hassle, an under-sink RO unit or a certified carbon block filter is much easier to live with.


## Answer B
No, not on the strength of that newsletter. Nothing in it says the PFAS problem was fixed. Keep filtering until the utility tells you directly.

**Why the two notices don't contradict each other**

The June line, "meets or exceeds all current state and federal drinking water standards," can be literally true even if Well Field 2 still tests at 11 and 9 ppt. The reason is timing:

- EPA's PFOA/PFOS limits (4 ppt each, finalized in 2024) are on the books, but utilities don't have to comply with them yet. As I recall, the original deadline was 2029, and in 2025 EPA said it planned to push the PFOA/PFOS deadline to 2031. That's from memory, so check the current status on EPA's PFAS drinking water page.
- Until that compliance date, a utility isn't "violating" the MCL even when it tests above it. That lets it say it meets all *current* standards. The March letter said "above the new federal maximum contaminant level," which is accurate about the number but doesn't mean a violation.
- Unless your state has its own enforceable PFAS limit, there may be no standard in force today that 11 ppt breaks.

**What the June newsletter leaves out**

- It doesn't mention GAC or any other treatment being installed. In March they were only "evaluating" it. Designing and building a GAC system usually takes well over three months, often years, so treatment being in service by June would be unusually fast.
- It doesn't report any new sampling results for Well Field 2.
- "Remains part of our diversified supply portfolio" suggests they're still using that well. It could be blended with other sources, which may lower levels at your tap, but they haven't said that.

This reads like standard compliance language. I don't think it's a cover-up. It just doesn't answer your question.

**How to get a real answer**

Call or email the utility and ask:

1. Is treatment for PFAS in service at Well Field 2 today? If not, when is it expected?
2. What were the most recent PFOA and PFOS results, and on what date? Ask for results at the entry point or in the distribution system, not just at the wellhead.
3. Does water from Well Field 2 reach my address, and is it blended?

Your annual Consumer Confidence Report (usually out by July 1) or your state drinking water program's database may also list recent PFAS results.

If they confirm both numbers are under 4 ppt at your tap, or that treatment is running, you can reasonably stop. Until then, the RO pitcher is doing its job. If the pitcher is the annoying part, an under-sink RO unit or a carbon filter certified to NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 for PFOA/PFOS is much easier to live with. Check the certification listing for PFAS specifically, not just the product box.
