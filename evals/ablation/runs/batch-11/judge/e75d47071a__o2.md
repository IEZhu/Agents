## Latest user message
We're moving our file share (12 TB, lots of mid-sized files, NFS) from the old on-prem NAS to a new one in another building. The link between sites is 1 Gbps but we can only use about 40% of it during business hours without people complaining, and honestly nights aren't much better because backups run then, so assume 40% the whole time. The data changes by roughly 3% per day (people overwrite project files constantly). Management approved a maintenance window Sunday 01:00-03:00 for the cutover, and my manager's plan is: start the rsync Friday at 20:00 when everyone leaves, it'll be done by Sunday, then flip the DNS/mounts in the window. Is this plan OK? If not, what should the actual schedule look like? I need something I can take back to him with numbers.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Computes the initial copy time correctly: 40% of 1 Gbps is about 50 MB/s, so 12 TB takes roughly 65-75 hours (about 67 h in decimal TB, about 73 h in TiB), concludes that the Friday 20:00 start (29 hours before the window) cannot finish, and says the initial copy must start roughly three or more days before the Sunday window.
2. Accounts for data changing during the long copy (about 360 GB/day at 3%) and proposes repeated incremental/delta sync passes that shrink the outstanding changes, with rough durations for those passes.
3. Checks the final step against the fixed 01:00-03:00 window: notes that one full day of changes (~360 GB) alone takes about 2 hours at 50 MB/s, i.e. the entire window, so a delta pass must run shortly before the write freeze and only a few hours of changes may remain for the final in-window sync.
4. Includes a verification step around the cutover (e.g. checksum or dry-run comparison, test mounts) and a rollback option if the final sync or checks do not complete inside the window.

## Answer A
**No, the plan won't work.** Even at the theoretical maximum of your 40% share, copying 12 TB takes about **67 hours**. Friday 20:00 to Sunday 01:00 is **29 hours**. By the time the window opens, less than half the data (about 5 TB) will be on the new NAS. The fix is to start the initial copy about a week earlier, run incremental catch-up passes every night, and use the Sunday window only for a short final sync plus the switchover.

## The numbers

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Usable bandwidth | 1 Gbps × 40% = 400 Mbps ÷ 8 | **50 MB/s** (theoretical ceiling) |
| Throughput per day | 50 MB/s × 86,400 s | ~4.3 TB/day |
| Full initial copy (ideal) | 12 TB ÷ 50 MB/s | 240,000 s ≈ **67 h (2.8 days)** |
| Full initial copy (realistic, ~80% efficiency) | 12 TB ÷ 40 MB/s | ≈ **83 h (3.5 days)** |
| Manager's copy window | Fri 20:00 → Sun 01:00 | **29 h**, which moves ~5.2 TB at best |
| Rate needed to finish in 29 h | 12 TB ÷ 104,400 s | ~115 MB/s ≈ **920 Mbps**, i.e. the entire link at 100% |
| Daily churn | 3% × 12 TB | **~360 GB/day** |
| Time to catch up one day's churn | 360 GB ÷ 50 MB/s | **~2 h** (assuming whole files get re-sent) |

Two notes on these figures:
- The 80% efficiency figure is an assumption, not a measurement. Overhead from NFS, rsync and per-file handling varies a lot with file size and hardware. A pilot run (below) replaces it with your real number.
- If "12 TB" is actually 12 TiB, add ~10% to every time estimate (≈73 h at the ideal rate).

The good news is that churn isn't a problem. 360 GB/day uses about 2 hours of link time per day, so incremental passes easily keep up and the gap between the two NAS boxes shrinks quickly once the initial copy is done.

## Everything wrong with the current plan

1. **The copy can't finish in time.** Covered above. The shortfall is roughly 2.3×, which a bit of tuning won't close.
2. **There is no final sync in a frozen state.** Even if the copy finished Sunday morning, anything written between when rsync passed a directory and the cutover would be lost. Flipping DNS without a last rsync after freezing writes on the old share means you silently lose data.
3. **Time spent scanning files is ignored.** With "lots of mid-sized files" you probably have millions of files. An rsync pass has to stat every file on both sides before it knows what to send, even if almost nothing changed. Over NFS that scan alone can take tens of minutes to hours. **This is the biggest unknown for the 2-hour window**, and you have to measure it (see the pilot).
4. **There is no go/no-go point or rollback plan.** If something goes wrong at 02:30, nobody has decided who calls it off or how.

## Recommended schedule

Working backward from Sunday 01:00 (call it T):

| When | Step | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Now / this week** | **Pilot:** rsync a representative ~200–500 GB subset at the 40% cap. Also run a no-change rsync over the full tree (`--dry-run`) to time the scan | Get your *real* MB/s, file count and full-tree scan time. Every other number here depends on this |
| **T − 8 days** (the previous Friday, 20:00) | **Start the initial copy** at the bandwidth cap | Even at 83 h this finishes around Tuesday morning, leaving 3+ days of buffer for restarts or slow directories |
| **T − 5 → T − 2** (Tue–Fri nights) | **Nightly incremental rsync passes** | Each moves ~360 GB (~2 h plus scan time). Log how long each pass takes, because the late ones are your rehearsal for the cutover |
| **Thu or Fri** | **Dry-run the cutover** on one test client: mount the new NAS, check permissions/UIDs, open files from key applications | Catch UID/GID mapping, ACL and export-option problems before the night itself |
| **T − 5 h** (Sat 20:00) | **Final pre-sync** | Keeps the last delta down to a few hours of weekend changes |
| **Sun 01:00** | **Freeze:** make the old export read-only or unexport it; confirm no active writers | Required for a consistent final copy |
| **01:05 – ~01:50** | **Final rsync with `--delete`** | Moves only the changes since Saturday 20:00. Data volume is small (5 h of *weekday*-rate churn ≈ 75 GB ≈ 25 min), so time is mostly the scan |
| **~02:00** | **Go/no-go gate** | If the final sync hasn't finished cleanly by now, abort: re-enable the old share read-write and reschedule. No harm done |
| **02:00 – 02:40** | **Verify** (file counts, checksums on a sample of critical directories, permission spot checks), then **switch DNS/mounts** | |
| **02:40 – 03:00** | **Smoke test from real clients**, then close the window | Buffer |
| **After cutover** | **Keep the old NAS read-only for 1–2 weeks** | Rollback path, and a place to recover anything that turns out to be missing |

## Technical details that matter for the numbers

- **Run rsync between the two NAS hosts (over ssh or as an rsync daemon), not from one machine that mounts both shares over NFS.** When both paths are local, rsync switches to whole-file mode by default and does no delta transfer. It also pulls every byte over NFS reads and then pushes it again as NFS writes (recalled rsync behavior, confirm in `man rsync` under `--whole-file`). My 2 h/day churn figure assumes whole-file transfer anyway, since apps that "overwrite project files" often rewrite them completely. Delta transfer can only make it better.
- **Cap bandwidth in rsync itself** with `--bwlimit` rather than hoping it stays at 40%. The value is in KiB/s by default in the versions I know, so about `--bwlimit=48000` gives ~400 Mbps. Check the man page for your version.
- **Preserve ownership correctly:** use `-aHAX --numeric-ids` (hard links, ACLs, xattrs, raw UIDs), plus `--delete` on the catch-up and final passes so deleted files don't come back to life on the new NAS.
- **If the pilot shows a scan time over ~45 min:** run several rsyncs in parallel, one per top-level directory. That helps with scanning more than with throughput, because the bandwidth cap is shared. Alternatively, if both NAS boxes are from the same vendor, use its native snapshot replication (block-level, no per-file scan), which usually beats rsync for exactly this kind of cutover.
- **Ask for a temporary exception for the window itself.** At 01:00 on Sunday, pausing the backup job and using the full 1 Gbps for 90 minutes is probably an easy request, and it doubles your safety margin for the final sync.

## Answer B
**No, the plan doesn't work.** At 40% of 1 Gbps, copying 12 TB once takes about 3 days of nonstop transfer. Friday 20:00 to Sunday 01:00 is only 29 hours, so by the start of the window you'd have roughly 40–45% of the data on the new NAS. Start the initial copy about a week before cutover. Then run repeated incremental syncs so the final sync inside the window is small, and measure how long it takes before the night.

## The numbers

| Item | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Usable bandwidth | 1 Gbps × 40% = 400 Mbps | **~50 MB/s** (theoretical ceiling) |
| Realistic throughput | ~85–90% of ceiling after TCP/rsync/SSH overhead and per-file costs | **~43–45 MB/s** |
| Data per day | 50 MB/s × 86,400 s | **~4.3 TB/day** at best, ~3.8 TB/day realistic |
| Full initial copy (12 TB) | 12,000,000 MB ÷ 45–50 MB/s | **~67–80 hours (≈3 days)** |
| Manager's window (Fri 20:00 → Sun 01:00) | 29 h × ~45–50 MB/s | **~4.7–5.2 TB copied (≈40–43%)** |
| When a Friday-started copy would really finish | Fri 20:00 + 67–80 h | **Monday ~15:00 to Tuesday ~04:00** |
| Daily change (3% of 12 TB) | 360 GB/day | **~15 GB/hour** |
| Time to re-sync one day of changes | 360 GB ÷ 50 MB/s | **~2 hours** (transfer only, not counting the scan) |

The bytes assume the worst case: a changed file gets re-sent in full. rsync's delta transfer may send less, but when an application saves by rewriting the whole file, most of the file usually changes anyway. So plan on full-file transfers.

## A second problem: the file scan

With "lots of mid-sized files" you probably have millions of files. At an average of 5 MB that's about 2.4 million. Every rsync pass, including the final one, first walks the whole tree on both sides over NFS to find what changed. On a large tree this can take anywhere from tens of minutes to more than an hour, even when very little data changed. I can't give you a real number for your setup. You have to measure it (see below). In a 2-hour window, the scan time may matter more than the bandwidth.

## Recommended schedule (cutover Sunday 01:00, call it day D)

| When | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **D−7 (previous Sunday/Monday)** | Start the initial copy (the "seed") | Throttle to your 40% limit (`rsync --bwlimit`; check the units in your man page, they're KiB/s). Use `-aHAX --numeric-ids` so hard links, ACLs and xattrs carry over and NFS UID/GIDs map 1:1. |
| **D−4 (~Thursday)** | Seed finishes (expected ~3–3.5 days) | This is the first go/no-go. If the seed isn't done by Thursday evening, move the cutover to the next weekend. |
| **D−4 → D−3** | Catch-up pass | Picks up everything that changed during the seed. Worst case about 1 TB, so ~6 h of transfer plus the scan. |
| **D−3 → D−1** | Daily incremental passes (or back-to-back) | Log the `--stats` output and the wall-clock time of every pass. This replaces guesses with your real delta size and scan time. |
| **Saturday (D−1) evening** | Dress rehearsal | Run an incremental exactly like the final one. Time scan + transfer. **Second go/no-go: it must finish in under ~60 min.** Keep incrementals running until just before the window so the final delta stays small. |
| **Sun 01:00** | Freeze the source | Export read-only or unmount clients. No writes after this point. |
| **01:00–~01:45** | Final rsync **with `--delete`** | Changes since the last pass: ~3 h × 15 GB/h ≈ 45 GB ≈ 15 min of transfer, plus the measured scan time. |
| **~01:45–02:15** | Verify, then flip DNS/mounts | Spot-check file counts and sizes plus a checksum sample on key project directories. Update DNS/automount and remount a test client. |
| **02:15–02:45** | Smoke test | Open, save and check permissions as a few representative users/groups. |
| **02:45** | Final go/no-go | Rollback: the old NAS is untouched and authoritative. Point DNS back and set it read-write again. |

Budget for the 2-hour window: at 50 MB/s you can move at most ~360 GB in 2 hours. Realistically only ~60 minutes are available for the final sync, which means ~180 GB minus the scan time. The rest is needed for verification, the cutover and a rollback reserve. That's why the delta has to be kept small beforehand.

## Things that would make this much safer

1. **Ask for the full link during the window.** At 01:00 on a Sunday, with backups paused for two hours, 1 Gbps gives ~110 MB/s, so the final sync goes about 2.5× faster. This is the cheapest risk reduction available.
2. **Check whether the NAS pair supports native replication.** Snapshot-based replication (e.g. ZFS send/receive, or the vendor's own replication feature) doesn't need a full file-tree walk for incrementals, which removes the scan problem. Whether it's available depends on your hardware.
3. **Split the rsync by top-level directory.** Running a few rsyncs in parallel won't beat the bandwidth cap, but it can cut the scan time a lot on a large tree.
4. **Seed the data physically if the week is too tight.** Copying 12 TB to a disk at ~150–200 MB/s takes ~17–22 h each way. That isn't faster than the network, but it keeps the link free and removes the throttling issue. After that, only the incrementals go over the wire.
