## Bottom line first

I've written the runbook below, but I don't think the plan fits the window as it stands, and the on-call team should know that before Saturday. Your rehearsal measured only the **restore** (3h50m). Nobody has timed the **dump** against the live primary, and on the night the dump runs first, in series, inside the same 6-hour window. The runbook therefore includes a **hard go/no-go at 00:15**. I'd also push to change the migration method (see "Better option" below). If that isn't possible this time, run one more rehearsal that times the whole pipeline.

## Why the numbers don't close

Window: 23:00 to 05:00 = **360 min**.

| Step | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Drain + maintenance mode + final checks | 15 min | typical |
| `pg_dump -Fd -j 8` of ~1.2 TB | **60–150 min (unmeasured)** | see below |
| `pg_restore -j 8` | 230 min | your rehearsal |
| ANALYZE (pg_restore does not carry planner statistics) | 15–40 min | depends on stats target and `-j` |
| Secret switch, pod restarts, Celery | 15–20 min | typical |
| Smoke test + go/no-go | 30 min | typical |
| Rollback reserve (revert secret, restart, lift maintenance) | 20–30 min | must always be kept free |
| **Total** | **~385–515 min** | vs. 360 available |

The biggest issue is that `-j 8` does not help your biggest table. Parallel dump and parallel restore split work **per table**. The 480 GB table is one `COPY` stream on the way out and one on the way back in, and its indexes are built after it loads. That one table, plus its largest index build, is probably most of your 3h50m restore, and it will be a single-threaded bottleneck in the dump too. Throughput will depend on disk, network (is the source on-prem or in EC2?), and especially on **pg_dump compression**. `-Fd` compresses by default, and that is CPU-bound per table. I haven't measured any of this for your setup; the ranges above are order-of-magnitude.

Other gaps between the rehearsal and the real night:
- The rehearsal was one month ago. Data has grown since then.
- Dumping from a snapshot copy is not the same as dumping from the live primary. Disk, cache and network paths differ.
- The rehearsal doesn't say whether the restore target was the **same Aurora instance class** you'll use on the night.

## Better option (strongly recommended)

| Option | Downtime | Pros | Cons | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Native logical replication** (PG 12 publisher → Aurora PG 15 subscriber) | ~15–30 min | Initial copy runs days ahead with no deadline. Cutover = stop writes, wait for lag 0, fix sequences, switch. Easy rollback before writes open. | Needs `wal_level=logical` on the source, which **requires a restart**. Tables without a PK/replica identity can't replicate UPDATE/DELETE. DDL and sequences don't replicate. Large objects aren't supported. | You can freeze schema changes for a week and all hot tables have PKs |
| **B. AWS DMS (full load + CDC)** | ~15–30 min | Managed, with monitoring | Another moving part. Type-fidelity quirks (LOBs, JSON, arrays) need validating. Extra cost. | Logical replication is blocked organizationally |
| **C. Dump/restore (current plan), tuned** | 6h+ | Simplest, and already rehearsed | Doesn't fit the window with margin. No way to recover time mid-night | Data is small or the window is long |

My recommendation is **A**. It turns a 6-hour high-risk night into a short cutover, and the risky part (the 480 GB copy) moves out of the window. The costs are one restart on the source to set `wal_level=logical` and a check for tables without primary keys (`SELECT ... FROM pg_class LEFT JOIN pg_constraint ...`). As I recall, Aurora PostgreSQL 15 works fine as a subscriber to a community PostgreSQL publisher, but confirm the network path and `pg_hba` on the source before you commit to it.

If you must keep C this weekend, do these **before Saturday**:
1. **Time a full dump against the live primary** (or the replica) at an off-peak hour, with the exact flags you'll use. This is the missing number.
2. Use **pg_dump/pg_restore 15 client binaries**. A newer client dumping an older server is supported; the reverse is not.
3. Try `-Z 0` (no compression) if the dump host has the disk space. It often speeds up the single-table bottleneck a lot.
4. Run the dump **from an EC2 host in the same region/AZ as Aurora** with fast local or gp3 storage, so the restore reads locally.
5. **Temporarily scale the Aurora writer up** for the restore, raise `maintenance_work_mem` in its parameter group, and don't attach readers until afterwards. Index builds on the big table benefit most.
6. If the 480 GB table is append-only (audit, events, history), pre-load its historical rows before the window and move only the delta on the night. This alone could make C fit.

## Runbook: PostgreSQL 12 → Aurora PostgreSQL 15 (dump/restore)

**Roles:** Migration Lead (IC, makes go/no-go calls) · DBA (dump/restore) · Platform (EKS, secrets) · App owner (smoke tests) · Comms.
**Channel:** `#<migration-channel>` · **Bridge:** `<link>` · **Status page owner:** `<name>`

### Principle
The source database is **never modified** during the night. Until traffic opens on Aurora, rollback means pointing back at the source, and **no data is lost**. After traffic opens, rollback means losing or reconciling new writes, so 04:45 is the **point of no return**.

Notice how thin this is: with a 60-minute dump there are about **15 minutes of slack** in the whole night. If P5 comes back at 90+ minutes, the plan does not fit, and you should know that on Wednesday, not at 00:15 on Sunday.

### Rollback (any time before 04:45)

1. If set: `ALTER DATABASE <db> RESET default_transaction_read_only;` on the source.
2. Revert the `DATABASE_URL` secret to the source (if it was changed), `rollout restart` the web and Celery deployments.
3. Confirm connections land on the source, then run a quick smoke test.
4. Start Celery beat, turn maintenance mode OFF.
5. Budget: **20–30 min.** Leave the Aurora cluster as-is for the post-mortem.

**After 04:45**, rollback means writes on Aurora are lost or need manual reconciliation. Only the IC can call it, and only for a severe issue.

### Post-night (Monday)
- Keep the PG 12 primary and replica **read-only and running for at least 7 days**, then take a final backup before decommissioning.
- Review Aurora slow-query log. Plans can change between PG 12 and PG 15.
- Confirm Aurora backups/PITR retention and snapshot schedule.
