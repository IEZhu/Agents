**No, the plan doesn't work.** At 40% of 1 Gbps, copying 12 TB once takes about 3 days of nonstop transfer. Friday 20:00 to Sunday 01:00 is only 29 hours, so by the start of the window you'd have roughly 40–45% of the data on the new NAS. Start the initial copy about a week before cutover. Then run repeated incremental syncs so the final sync inside the window is small, and measure how long it takes before the night.

## The numbers

| Item | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Usable bandwidth | 1 Gbps × 40% = 400 Mbps | **~50 MB/s** (theoretical ceiling) |
| Realistic throughput | ~85–90% of ceiling after TCP/rsync/SSH overhead and per-file costs | **~43–45 MB/s** |
| Data per day | 50 MB/s × 86,400 s | **~4.3 TB/day** at best, ~3.8 TB/day realistic |
| Full initial copy (12 TB) | 12,000,000 MB ÷ 45–50 MB/s | **~67–80 hours (≈3 days)** |
| Manager's window (Fri 20:00 → Sun 01:00) | 29 h × ~45–50 MB/s | **~4.7–5.2 TB copied (≈40–43%)** |
| When a Friday-started copy would really finish | Fri 20:00 + 67–80 h | **Monday ~15:00 to Tuesday ~04:00** |
| Daily change (3% of 12 TB) | 360 GB/day | **~15 GB/hour** |
| Time to re-sync one day of changes | 360 GB ÷ 50 MB/s | **~2 hours** (transfer only, not counting the scan) |

The bytes assume the worst case: a changed file gets re-sent in full. rsync's delta transfer may send less, but when an application saves by rewriting the whole file, most of the file usually changes anyway. So plan on full-file transfers.

## A second problem: the file scan

With "lots of mid-sized files" you probably have millions of files. At an average of 5 MB that's about 2.4 million. Every rsync pass, including the final one, first walks the whole tree on both sides over NFS to find what changed. On a large tree this can take anywhere from tens of minutes to more than an hour, even when very little data changed. I can't give you a real number for your setup. You have to measure it (see below). In a 2-hour window, the scan time may matter more than the bandwidth.

## Recommended schedule (cutover Sunday 01:00, call it day D)

| When | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **D−7 (previous Sunday/Monday)** | Start the initial copy (the "seed") | Throttle to your 40% limit (`rsync --bwlimit`; check the units in your man page, they're KiB/s). Use `-aHAX --numeric-ids` so hard links, ACLs and xattrs carry over and NFS UID/GIDs map 1:1. |
| **D−4 (~Thursday)** | Seed finishes (expected ~3–3.5 days) | This is the first go/no-go. If the seed isn't done by Thursday evening, move the cutover to the next weekend. |
| **D−4 → D−3** | Catch-up pass | Picks up everything that changed during the seed. Worst case about 1 TB, so ~6 h of transfer plus the scan. |
| **D−3 → D−1** | Daily incremental passes (or back-to-back) | Log the `--stats` output and the wall-clock time of every pass. This replaces guesses with your real delta size and scan time. |
| **Saturday (D−1) evening** | Dress rehearsal | Run an incremental exactly like the final one. Time scan + transfer. **Second go/no-go: it must finish in under ~60 min.** Keep incrementals running until just before the window so the final delta stays small. |
| **Sun 01:00** | Freeze the source | Export read-only or unmount clients. No writes after this point. |
| **01:00–~01:45** | Final rsync **with `--delete`** | Changes since the last pass: ~3 h × 15 GB/h ≈ 45 GB ≈ 15 min of transfer, plus the measured scan time. |
| **~01:45–02:15** | Verify, then flip DNS/mounts | Spot-check file counts and sizes plus a checksum sample on key project directories. Update DNS/automount and remount a test client. |
| **02:15–02:45** | Smoke test | Open, save and check permissions as a few representative users/groups. |
| **02:45** | Final go/no-go | Rollback: the old NAS is untouched and authoritative. Point DNS back and set it read-write again. |

Budget for the 2-hour window: at 50 MB/s you can move at most ~360 GB in 2 hours. Realistically only ~60 minutes are available for the final sync, which means ~180 GB minus the scan time. The rest is needed for verification, the cutover and a rollback reserve. That's why the delta has to be kept small beforehand.

## Things that would make this much safer

1. **Ask for the full link during the window.** At 01:00 on a Sunday, with backups paused for two hours, 1 Gbps gives ~110 MB/s, so the final sync goes about 2.5× faster. This is the cheapest risk reduction available.
2. **Check whether the NAS pair supports native replication.** Snapshot-based replication (e.g. ZFS send/receive, or the vendor's own replication feature) doesn't need a full file-tree walk for incrementals, which removes the scan problem. Whether it's available depends on your hardware.
3. **Split the rsync by top-level directory.** Running a few rsyncs in parallel won't beat the bandwidth cap, but it can cut the scan time a lot on a large tree.
4. **Seed the data physically if the week is too tight.** Copying 12 TB to a disk at ~150–200 MB/s takes ~17–22 h each way. That isn't faster than the network, but it keeps the link free and removes the throttling issue. After that, only the incrementals go over the wire.

## Short version for your manager

> "12 TB over a 400 Mbps budget is about 3 days of nonstop copying. Starting Friday night we'd be under half done when the window opens. Plan: start the bulk copy the Sunday or Monday before, finish by Thursday, sync the changes every day (~360 GB/day ≈ 2 h), rehearse the final sync Saturday, and in the Sunday window freeze the old NAS, do the last ~45 GB sync, verify and switch. Go/no-go on Thursday (seed done) and Saturday (rehearsal under 60 min). The old NAS stays untouched as rollback."
