## Latest user message
I'm consolidating my household finances into one spreadsheet. Target format: date as YYYY-MM-DD, description, amount where negative = money leaving me, positive = money coming in. I have exports from 4 accounts, here's the header plus one or two sample rows from each:

A) US checking:
Date,Description,Amount
03/04/2024,GROCERY OUTLET,-54.20
03/15/2024,PAYROLL,2450.00

B) German current account:
Buchungstag;Verwendungszweck;Betrag
03.04.2024;Miete April;-1.150,00

C) UK credit card:
Transaction Date,Details,Debit,Credit
2024-04-03,TESCO STORES,23.10,
2024-04-20,PAYMENT RECEIVED,,300.00

D) US credit card:
Posted Date,Payee,Amount
04/05/2024,AMAZON MKTP,45.99
04/25/2024,AUTOPAY PAYMENT,-500.00

For each one tell me exactly how to convert it. I'm also adding two more accounts next month and a friend wants to do the same with her banks, so I'd rather not have to puzzle out each new bank's format from zero.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Provides a reusable, bank-independent checklist or procedure for any new export (covering at least delimiter, date format, number format and sign convention) that the user and friend can apply to future accounts.
2. Handles B correctly: semicolon delimiter, DD.MM.YYYY dates, period as thousands separator and comma as decimal, so -1.150,00 becomes -1150.00 on 2024-04-03.
3. Handles the sign conventions correctly: for C amount = Credit minus Debit (Tesco -23.10, payment +300.00); for D the sign must be flipped (Amazon -45.99, autopay +500.00), while A is already in the target convention.
4. Converts the US MM/DD/YYYY dates correctly (e.g. A 03/04/2024 -> 2024-03-04) and does not silently treat them as DD/MM.

## Answer A
**Short answer:** A and C need only simple reformatting. B needs a date swap and German number parsing. D is the trap: its signs are **reversed** relative to your target, so every amount has to be multiplied by −1. Two more things will affect the combined sheet whichever method you use: **mixed currencies** and **transfers between your own accounts**, which get counted twice.

## Per-account conversion

| | Date → YYYY-MM-DD | Description | Amount → (− out / + in) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** US checking | `MM/DD/YYYY` → `03/04/2024` = **2024-03-04** (March 4) | `Description` | `Amount` as-is (already − = out) |
| **B** German current | `DD.MM.YYYY` → `03.04.2024` = **2024-04-03** (April 3) | `Verwendungszweck` | `Betrag`: delete `.` (thousands), then `,` → `.` → `-1.150,00` = **-1150.00**; sign already correct |
| **C** UK credit card | already ISO, keep | `Details` | `Credit − Debit`, blank = 0 → TESCO = **-23.10**, payment = **+300.00** |
| **D** US credit card | `MM/DD/YYYY` | `Payee` | **`−1 × Amount`** → AMAZON = **-45.99**, AUTOPAY = **+500.00** |

### Details that will bite you

- **A and D in a spreadsheet app:** if your Excel/Sheets locale is non-US, it will auto-parse `03/04/2024` as 3 April, and it does this silently. Import date columns **as text**, then convert with a formula:
  - A/D: `=DATE(RIGHT(A2,4), LEFT(A2,2), MID(A2,4,2))`
  - B: `=DATE(RIGHT(A2,4), MID(A2,4,2), LEFT(A2,2))`
  - Format the result as `yyyy-mm-dd`.
- **B file format:** the delimiter is `;`, not `,`. German bank exports often include a few preamble lines (account number, period, balance) above the header, and are often encoded as Windows-1252/ISO-8859-1 rather than UTF-8. Umlauts showing up as `Ã¼` means the encoding is wrong. Amount formula with Betrag imported as text: `=VALUE(SUBSTITUTE(SUBSTITUTE(C2,".",""),",","."))`. That formula assumes your spreadsheet locale uses `.` as the decimal separator.
- **C:** `=N(D2)-N(C2)`, where C = Debit and D = Credit. Refunds land in Credit and come out positive, which is correct.
- **D's sign:** your two sample rows strongly suggest that positive = charge (the card issuer's point of view). Before trusting that, confirm it against one known refund or payment on the statement.

## Two issues that affect the whole sheet

1. **Currency:** A and D are USD, B is EUR, C is GBP. Add a `currency` column. Summing −54.20 USD with −1150.00 EUR gives a meaningless number. Either keep totals per currency or add a converted column using a documented rate (by date or monthly average).
2. **Double counting:** paying off a credit card appears twice. For example, D's `AUTOPAY PAYMENT +500.00` probably has a matching −500.00 in checking A, and C's `PAYMENT RECEIVED` matches an outflow from whichever account paid it. Add a `category` or `is_transfer` column and exclude transfers from spending and income totals. Otherwise your spending looks higher by the full value of every card payment.

## Making new banks painless: describe each bank with 6 answers, not new logic

Every bank export differs from the others in only these ways:

| # | Question | How to answer it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Delimiter, encoding, header/preamble rows to skip | Open the file in a plain text editor |
| 2 | Date format | Find a row where the day is > 12. That settles DD/MM vs MM/DD. |
| 3 | Decimal and thousands separators | Look for an amount ≥ 1,000 |
| 4 | One signed amount column, or split Debit/Credit (or an amount plus a "D/C"/"S/H" indicator column) | Header row |
| 5 | Sign convention: whose point of view? | Check one known purchase and one known payment/refund. Credit cards are often reversed. |
| 6 | Currency | Account statement |

Put those answers in a small config table and use one converter for all banks. Adding a bank, or your friend's banks, then means adding one profile rather than writing new logic:

```python
import pandas as pd

# One profile per account. Adding a bank = adding one entry here.
PROFILES = {
    "A_us_checking": dict(file="a.csv", sep=",", encoding="utf-8", skiprows=0,
                          date="Date", date_fmt="%m/%d/%Y", desc="Description",
                          amount="Amount", dec=".", thou=",", sign=1, currency="USD"),
    "B_de_current":  dict(file="b.csv", sep=";", encoding="cp1252", skiprows=0,
                          date="Buchungstag", date_fmt="%d.%m.%Y", desc="Verwendungszweck",
                          amount="Betrag", dec=",", thou=".", sign=1, currency="EUR"),
    "C_uk_card":     dict(file="c.csv", sep=",", encoding="utf-8", skiprows=0,
                          date="Transaction Date", date_fmt="%Y-%m-%d", desc="Details",
                          debit="Debit", credit="Credit", dec=".", thou=",", sign=1, currency="GBP"),
    "D_us_card":     dict(file="d.csv", sep=",", encoding="utf-8", skiprows=0,
                          date="Posted Date", date_fmt="%m/%d/%Y", desc="Payee",
                          amount="Amount", dec=".", thou=",", sign=-1, currency="USD"),
}

def to_number(s, dec, thou):
    s = s.astype(str).str.strip().str.replace(r"[^\d,.\-]", "", regex=True)  # drop currency symbols/spaces
    if thou:
        s = s.str.replace(thou, "", regex=False)
    if dec != ".":
        s = s.str.replace(dec, ".", regex=False)
    return pd.to_numeric(s, errors="raise")

def convert(name, p):
    df = pd.read_csv(p["file"], sep=p["sep"], dtype=str, encoding=p["encoding"],
                     skiprows=p["skiprows"])
    df.columns = df.columns.str.strip()
    out = pd.DataFrame()
    # format= makes it fail loudly on a wrong date format instead of guessing
    out["date"] = pd.to_datetime(df[p["date"]].str.strip(), format=p["date_fmt"]).dt.strftime("%Y-%m-%d")
    out["description"] = df[p["desc"]].str.strip()
    if "amount" in p:
        amt = to_number(df[p["amount"]], p["dec"], p["thou"])
    else:  # split debit/credit columns; blank = 0
        amt = (to_number(df[p["credit"]].fillna("0"), p["dec"], p["thou"])
               - to_number(df[p["debit"]].fillna("0"), p["dec"], p["thou"]))
    out["amount"] = (amt * p["sign"]).round(2)
    out["currency"] = p["currency"]
    out["account"] = name
    print(f"{name}: {len(out)} rows, net {out['amount'].sum():.2f} {p['currency']}")
    return out

all_tx = pd.concat([convert(n, p) for n, p in PROFILES.items()], ignore_index=True)
all_tx.sort_values("date").to_csv("combined.csv", index=False)
print("Saved: combined.csv")
```

I haven't run this against your actual files. The `cp1252` encoding for B and the absence of preamble rows are guesses, so adjust `encoding`/`skiprows` if the read fails. Pandas and `format=` are strict on purpose: a mismatched profile raises an error rather than producing wrong dates.

## Validate each account once, when you add it

- **Row count** matches the export.
- **Net sum per account** matches the statement's closing balance minus its opening balance. For a credit card, the balance owed moves the opposite way, so the net should equal minus the change in balance owed. A sign error on one account shows up here straight away.
- **Spot-check 2–3 rows**, including one where the day is > 12 and one amount ≥ 1,000.

- **D's sign.** A purchase showing as `+45.99` means the card reports from the bank's side: the amount is what you owe. Flipping it gives purchase = −45.99 and payment = +500.00.

## Two things to add to your target format

1. **A `currency` column (and an `account` column).** You have USD, EUR and GBP. Without a currency column, one sum mixes all three and means nothing. Convert to one currency later, as a separate step, and record which rate and date you used.
2. **A way to mark transfers.** `AUTOPAY PAYMENT` (+500 on card D) probably has a matching −500 in checking A. The same goes for the UK card payment and whichever account paid it. Each of these moves your own money between your own accounts, so it isn't income or spending. If you leave both sides in, your totals count the payment once and the card purchases again. Add a `category` or `is_transfer` column, tag these rows, and leave them out of income and spending totals.

## Reusable method for new banks (yours and your friend's)

For any new export, answer these five questions, using one or two transactions you already know (rent, salary, a purchase):

1. **File:** what delimiter (`,` `;` or tab), what encoding (UTF-8, or Windows-1252 for many European banks), and are there extra lines above the header row? Some banks put account info or a balance there.
2. **Date:** which column, and what order? Find a row with a day above 12 to settle DD/MM vs MM/DD. If the export has both a transaction date and a posting date, pick one and use the same kind for every account.
3. **Numbers:** which character is the decimal separator and which is the thousands separator? Watch for currency symbols, trailing minus signs (`50,00-`) and parentheses (`(50.00)`).
4. **Amount structure:** one signed column, a Debit/Credit pair, or an amount plus a separate indicator column (`S`/`H` in German exports, `DR`/`CR`, `Debit`/`Credit`)?
5. **Sign:** find a known purchase. If it isn't negative once parsed, flip the sign. Credit cards are the usual offenders.

Then check the output every time:
- The row count in equals the row count out.
- The sum of converted amounts for a statement period matches the statement's net change, or the change in the card balance with the sign flipped.
- Known transactions have the sign you expect.
- There are no duplicate rows where two exports overlap in dates.

## Config-driven converter

Each account is one config entry. Adding a bank means answering the five questions and adding an entry, with no code changes. It reads everything as text and parses it explicitly, so a wrong format raises an error instead of producing wrong numbers.

```python
import pandas as pd

# One entry per account. File paths and encodings are placeholders; adjust them.
ACCOUNTS = [
    {"name": "us_checking", "file": "us_checking.csv", "sep": ",", "encoding": "utf-8-sig",
     "skiprows": 0, "date_col": "Date", "date_fmt": "%m/%d/%Y", "desc_col": "Description",
     "amount_type": "single", "amount_col": "Amount",
     "decimal": ".", "thousands": ",", "flip_sign": False, "currency": "USD"},
    {"name": "de_current", "file": "de_current.csv", "sep": ";", "encoding": "cp1252",
     "skiprows": 0, "date_col": "Buchungstag", "date_fmt": "%d.%m.%Y", "desc_col": "Verwendungszweck",
     "amount_type": "single", "amount_col": "Betrag",
     "decimal": ",", "thousands": ".", "flip_sign": False, "currency": "EUR"},
    {"name": "uk_card", "file": "uk_card.csv", "sep": ",", "encoding": "utf-8-sig",
     "skiprows": 0, "date_col": "Transaction Date", "date_fmt": "%Y-%m-%d", "desc_col": "Details",
     "amount_type": "split", "debit_col": "Debit", "credit_col": "Credit",
     "decimal": ".", "thousands": ",", "flip_sign": False, "currency": "GBP"},
    {"name": "us_card", "file": "us_card.csv", "sep": ",", "encoding": "utf-8-sig",
     "skiprows": 0, "date_col": "Posted Date", "date_fmt": "%m/%d/%Y", "desc_col": "Payee",
     "amount_type": "single", "amount_col": "Amount",
     "decimal": ".", "thousands": ",", "flip_sign": True, "currency": "USD"},
]


def parse_amount(col, decimal, thousands):
    s = col.astype(str).str.strip()
    s = s.str.replace(" ", "", regex=False).str.replace(" ", "", regex=False)
    if thousands:
        s = s.str.replace(thousands, "", regex=False)
    if decimal != ".":
        s = s.str.replace(decimal, ".", regex=False)
    s = s.replace("", "0")               # blank Debit/Credit cells count as 0
    return pd.to_numeric(s)              # raises on anything unparsed (e.g. "$", "50,00-")


def convert(cfg):
    df = pd.read_csv(cfg["file"], sep=cfg["sep"], encoding=cfg["encoding"],
                     skiprows=cfg["skiprows"], dtype=str, keep_default_na=False)
    df.columns = df.columns.str.strip()
    out = pd.DataFrame()
    # A strict format raises if a date doesn't match, e.g. DD/MM mistaken for MM/DD.
    out["date"] = pd.to_datetime(df[cfg["date_col"]].str.strip(),
                                 format=cfg["date_fmt"]).dt.strftime("%Y-%m-%d")
    out["description"] = df[cfg["desc_col"]].str.strip()
    if cfg["amount_type"] == "single":
        amt = parse_amount(df[cfg["amount_col"]], cfg["decimal"], cfg["thousands"])
    elif cfg["amount_type"] == "split":
        amt = (parse_amount(df[cfg["credit_col"]], cfg["decimal"], cfg["thousands"])
               - parse_amount(df[cfg["debit_col"]], cfg["decimal"], cfg["thousands"]))
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"unknown amount_type: {cfg['amount_type']}")
    if cfg["flip_sign"]:
        amt = -amt
    out["amount"] = amt.round(2)
    out["currency"] = cfg["currency"]
    out["account"] = cfg["name"]
    assert len(out) == len(df), f"{cfg['name']}: row count changed"
    print(f"{cfg['name']}: {len(out)} rows, {out['date'].min()} to {out['date'].max()}, "
          f"net {out['amount'].sum():.2f} {cfg['currency']}")
    return out


combined = pd.concat([convert(c) for c in ACCOUNTS], ignore_index=True)
dupes = combined.duplicated(keep=False).sum()
print(f"Total rows: {len(combined)}, exact duplicate rows: {dupes}")
combined.sort_values(["date", "account"]).to_csv("combined.csv", index=False)
print("Saved: combined.csv")
```

Notes on the script:
- **German encoding.** `cp1252` is my guess based on what German banks commonly use, not something I checked against your bank. If umlauts come out garbled, or the read fails, try `utf-8-sig`.
- **Indicator-column exports** (`S`/`H`, `DR`/`CR`) need a third `amount_type`. The pattern is the same: parse the number, then negate it where the indicator says debit.
- **Debit columns that already hold negative numbers.** Some banks do this, and there Credit − Debit gives the wrong sign. Rule 5 in the checklist (check a known purchase) catches it.
- **Your friend's banks.** She can use the script unchanged and only write her own `ACCOUNTS` entries.

I haven't run this against your real files, only worked through your sample rows by hand. Run it once and check the printed nets against your statements before you trust the combined sheet.
