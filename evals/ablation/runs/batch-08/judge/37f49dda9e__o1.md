We're about to make an offer on a house. Loan would be $420,000, 30-year fixed. What would my monthly payment be at today's average 30-year fixed rate? Also add property tax ($6,000/yr) and homeowners insurance ($1,800/yr) so I see the full monthly number. I want to know if we stay under $3,400/month total.

## Answer A
**Bottom line:** You stay under $3,400/month as long as your rate is below about **6.84%**. Above that, you go over.

I can't look up today's average 30-year fixed rate from here, so I haven't used a single "today" number. The table below covers the likely range. Find your rate in it once you have the current average or, better, an actual quote from your lender.

### Fixed costs
- Property tax: $6,000 / 12 = **$500/mo**
- Homeowners insurance: $1,800 / 12 = **$150/mo**
- Fixed add-on: **$650/mo**, which leaves **$2,750/mo** for principal and interest (P&I) under your cap.

### Monthly payment on $420,000, 30-year fixed

| Rate | P&I | + Tax & insurance | Total | vs. $3,400 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.50% | $2,385 | $650 | **$3,035** | $365 under |
| 6.00% | $2,518 | $650 | **$3,168** | $232 under |
| 6.25% | $2,586 | $650 | **$3,236** | $164 under |
| 6.50% | $2,655 | $650 | **$3,305** | $95 under |
| 6.75% | $2,724 | $650 | **$3,374** | $26 under |
| **~6.84%** | $2,750 | $650 | **$3,400** | break-even |
| 7.00% | $2,794 | $650 | **$3,444** | $44 over |
| 7.25% | $2,865 | $650 | **$3,515** | $115 over |

Formula: P&I = L·r / (1 − (1+r)^−360), where r = annual rate / 12. Each 0.25% change in the rate moves your payment by about $70/mo.

### Where to get the real rate
- The benchmark most people quote is Freddie Mac's weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS). Mortgage News Daily publishes a daily index. Check one of them right before you make the offer.
- Your own rate can be well above or below the average, depending on your credit score, down payment, points and loan type. **Get a written Loan Estimate from your lender. That rate is the one that decides this.**

### Costs this number doesn't include
The $3,400 test can look fine and still fail once these are added:
- **PMI** if you're putting down less than 20%. It often adds roughly $100–300/mo on a loan this size, but that's a rough range, so ask your lender for the actual figure.
- **HOA dues**, if the property has any.
- **Tax reassessment.** Check whether the $6,000 figure reflects the current owner's assessment or what the tax will be at your purchase price. It often goes up after a sale.
- Insurance premiums that rise at renewal, and flood insurance if the home is in a flood zone.

If your quoted rate is at or above about 6.75%, you're within roughly $25 of the cap, and any one of these costs would push you over. In that case, consider buying the rate down with points, lowering the loan amount, or locking the rate as soon as the offer is accepted.


## Answer B
**Short answer: you stay under $3,400/month as long as your rate is below about 6.84%.** Above that, you go over.

I can't look up today's average 30-year fixed rate from here, so I don't have a live number to give you. Below is the full payment at a range of rates. Check Freddie Mac's weekly PMMS survey or your lender's quote, then find your row.

### Fixed monthly costs (don't depend on the rate)
- Property tax: $6,000 / 12 = **$500**
- Homeowners insurance: $1,800 / 12 = **$150**
- Together: **$650/month**

That leaves **$2,750/month** for principal and interest if you want to stay under $3,400.

### Full monthly payment on a $420,000, 30-year fixed loan

| Rate | Principal & interest | + Tax & insurance | **Total** | Under $3,400? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.00% | $2,518 | $650 | **$3,168** | Yes ($232 of room) |
| 6.25% | $2,586 | $650 | **$3,236** | Yes ($164 of room) |
| 6.50% | $2,655 | $650 | **$3,305** | Yes ($95 of room) |
| 6.75% | $2,724 | $650 | **$3,374** | Yes, just barely ($26) |
| 7.00% | $2,794 | $650 | **$3,444** | No (+$44) |
| 7.25% | $2,865 | $650 | **$3,515** | No (+$115) |
| 7.50% | $2,937 | $650 | **$3,587** | No (+$187) |

The break-even rate is about **6.84%**. Each 0.25% change in the rate moves your payment by roughly **$70/month**.

For context, and I haven't checked this: my last reliable data has national averages in the low-to-mid 6% range. If that still holds, you'd likely land around $3,200–$3,300 and be under budget. But rates may have moved, so check the current number before you rely on this.

### What could still push you over $3,400
- **PMI.** If you're putting down less than 20%, private mortgage insurance often adds something like $100–$300/month on a loan this size (a rough estimate). That alone could put you over at 6.5% or higher.
- **HOA dues**, if the property has them. They aren't included above.
- **Your actual quote vs. the average.** Credit score, down payment, points and loan type can put your rate noticeably above or below the published average. Budget on the rate you're actually quoted.
- **Tax reassessment after the sale.** Many areas reassess property value when a home sells. If $6,000 is the seller's current tax bill, yours could be higher.
- **Insurance.** Get a real quote for this specific house. Premiums vary a lot by location.

If you're right at the edge (a quoted rate of about 6.75% or higher), you could buy down the rate with points or lock in a rate before you make the offer. Or you could lower the offer: each $10,000 less borrowed cuts roughly $65/month at around 6.75%.
