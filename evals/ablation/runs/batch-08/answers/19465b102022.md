**Bottom line:** You stay under $3,400/month as long as your rate is below about **6.84%**. Above that, you go over.

I can't look up today's average 30-year fixed rate from here, so I haven't used a single "today" number. The table below covers the likely range. Find your rate in it once you have the current average or, better, an actual quote from your lender.

### Fixed costs
- Property tax: $6,000 / 12 = **$500/mo**
- Homeowners insurance: $1,800 / 12 = **$150/mo**
- Fixed add-on: **$650/mo**, which leaves **$2,750/mo** for principal and interest (P&I) under your cap.

Formula: P&I = L·r / (1 − (1+r)^−360), where r = annual rate / 12. Each 0.25% change in the rate moves your payment by about $70/mo.

### Where to get the real rate
- The benchmark most people quote is Freddie Mac's weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS). Mortgage News Daily publishes a daily index. Check one of them right before you make the offer.
- Your own rate can be well above or below the average, depending on your credit score, down payment, points and loan type. **Get a written Loan Estimate from your lender. That rate is the one that decides this.**

### Costs this number doesn't include
The $3,400 test can look fine and still fail once these are added:
- **PMI** if you're putting down less than 20%. It often adds roughly $100–300/mo on a loan this size, but that's a rough range, so ask your lender for the actual figure.
- **HOA dues**, if the property has any.
- **Tax reassessment.** Check whether the $6,000 figure reflects the current owner's assessment or what the tax will be at your purchase price. It often goes up after a sale.
- Insurance premiums that rise at renewal, and flood insurance if the home is in a flood zone.

If your quoted rate is at or above about 6.75%, you're within roughly $25 of the cap, and any one of these costs would push you over. In that case, consider buying the rate down with points, lowering the loan amount, or locking the rate as soon as the offer is accepted.
