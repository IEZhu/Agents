**Short answer: you stay under $3,400/month as long as your rate is below about 6.84%.** Above that, you go over.

I can't look up today's average 30-year fixed rate from here, so I don't have a live number to give you. Below is the full payment at a range of rates. Check Freddie Mac's weekly PMMS survey or your lender's quote, then find your row.

### Fixed monthly costs (don't depend on the rate)
- Property tax: $6,000 / 12 = **$500**
- Homeowners insurance: $1,800 / 12 = **$150**
- Together: **$650/month**

That leaves **$2,750/month** for principal and interest if you want to stay under $3,400.

The break-even rate is about **6.84%**. Each 0.25% change in the rate moves your payment by roughly **$70/month**.

For context, and I haven't checked this: my last reliable data has national averages in the low-to-mid 6% range. If that still holds, you'd likely land around $3,200–$3,300 and be under budget. But rates may have moved, so check the current number before you rely on this.

### What could still push you over $3,400
- **PMI.** If you're putting down less than 20%, private mortgage insurance often adds something like $100–$300/month on a loan this size (a rough estimate). That alone could put you over at 6.5% or higher.
- **HOA dues**, if the property has them. They aren't included above.
- **Your actual quote vs. the average.** Credit score, down payment, points and loan type can put your rate noticeably above or below the published average. Budget on the rate you're actually quoted.
- **Tax reassessment after the sale.** Many areas reassess property value when a home sells. If $6,000 is the seller's current tax bill, yours could be higher.
- **Insurance.** Get a real quote for this specific house. Premiums vary a lot by location.

If you're right at the edge (a quoted rate of about 6.75% or higher), you could buy down the rate with points or lock in a rate before you make the offer. Or you could lower the offer: each $10,000 less borrowed cuts roughly $65/month at around 6.75%.
