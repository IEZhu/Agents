## Latest user message
82 kg guy, lifting 5x/week at 6:30pm after work, lights out around 11pm. I already drink 2 regular coffees before noon. Just bought a pre-workout: one scoop = 350 mg caffeine + 3.2 g beta-alanine + 6 g citrulline malate. Label says 1 scoop 30 min before training. Plan is to take a full scoop before every session. Anything I should change? Want the best performance but I also don't want to wreck my sleep.

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Computes or flags total daily caffeine (about 350 mg plus two coffees, i.e. over roughly 400 mg/day) and relates the per-session dose to body weight (commonly cited effective range about 3-6 mg/kg) or suggests a reduced dose
2. Addresses timing relative to sleep: caffeine taken around 6pm with an 11pm bedtime can impair sleep given its multi-hour half-life, and proposes a concrete change (lower dose, half scoop, caffeine-free training days or earlier cut-off)
3. Explains that beta-alanine works through chronic muscle carnosine loading rather than acutely, so consistent daily intake (including rest days) matters more than pre-workout timing, and that tingling (paresthesia) is harmless and can be reduced by splitting doses
4. Gives explicit stop or adjust conditions (e.g. worsening sleep, palpitations, jitteriness, elevated blood pressure) and/or addresses caffeine tolerance with periodic breaks or cycling

## Answer A
Yes. The caffeine dose and its timing are the problems. The other two ingredients are fine, but beta-alanine works differently from how the label suggests.

## 1. Caffeine: too much, too late for an 11 pm bedtime

**The math at your schedule:**
- Scoop at ~6:00 pm = 350 mg.
- Caffeine's half-life averages about 5 h in healthy adults, but it varies a lot between people, roughly 3 to 7+ h (recalled, not verified here). Genetics (CYP1A2) accounts for much of that.
- At an average rate, about **~175 mg is still in your system at 11 pm**. That's close to drinking two coffees as you get into bed. If you clear caffeine slowly, it's more.
- Your two morning coffees add roughly 150–200 mg a day (estimate, since coffee varies widely). Adding a full scoop brings you to **~500–550 mg/day**, above the commonly cited 400 mg/day guideline for healthy adults (recalled, not verified here).

**Why "I fall asleep fine" isn't a good test:** caffeine taken within about 6 h of bed reduces deep (slow-wave) sleep and total sleep time even when you don't notice it. As I recall, a well-known study (Drake et al., 2013) found that 400 mg taken 6 h before bed still cut about an hour of sleep. Treat the details as recalled, not verified. Worse sleep over time costs you more recovery and progress than a pre-workout adds in one session.

**You also don't need 350 mg.** The performance benefit of caffeine starts at about **2–3 mg/kg**, and higher doses mostly add side effects (recalled from the ISSN caffeine position stand, not verified here). For you at 82 kg, that's **~160–250 mg**.

## 2. Beta-alanine: timing doesn't matter, daily dosing does

Beta-alanine doesn't work acutely. It builds up muscle carnosine over **4+ weeks of daily intake** (typically 3.2–6.4 g/day, recalled). This means:
- Taking it 30 min pre-workout gives no extra benefit.
- You'd need it **on rest days too**, and you don't want 350 mg of caffeine on those days.
- The tingling (paresthesia) is harmless. Splitting the dose (e.g., 2 × 1.6 g with meals) reduces it.

## 3. Citrulline malate: fine as is

6 g, 30–60 min pre-workout, falls within the dose range used in studies (~6–8 g). The evidence for a performance benefit is **mixed and small**. It might help you get a rep or two more on high-rep sets, but don't expect much from it.

## What I'd change

**Option A: keep this product (simplest)**
- Take **half a scoop** (~175 mg caffeine) about 30 min before training.
- Use it **only for your 2–3 hardest sessions per week**, not all 5. This keeps your caffeine sensitivity high and your weekly sleep load lower.
- Downside: it also halves beta-alanine and citrulline, below their effective doses.

**Option B: split the ingredients (better long-term)**
| Ingredient | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Beta-alanine (plain powder) | 3.2 g/day, split 2 × 1.6 g | With meals, **every day** |
| Citrulline malate | 6–8 g | 30–60 min pre-workout |
| Caffeine | 0–200 mg (e.g., 100–200 mg tablet or a coffee) | Pre-workout, key sessions only, **not after ~5 pm** if possible |

For your 6:30 pm sessions, the cleanest option is a **stim-free pre-workout on most days**, with a small caffeine dose saved for your heaviest day.

**Also consider:**
- Move one of your morning coffees earlier or drop it on days you use caffeine pre-workout, so you stay ≤400 mg/day in total.
- **Test it on yourself:** track sleep for 2 weeks (a wearable or just a sleep diary noting sleep onset, wake-ups and morning energy) with caffeine pre-workout vs. without. Your own data beats population averages here.
- Stop and see a doctor if you get palpitations, chest discomfort or a noticeably raised resting heart rate. That matters more with evening dosing on top of morning coffee.

**Bottom line:** 350 mg at 6 pm, five days a week, on top of two coffees, will very likely cut into your deep sleep even if you don't feel it. Cut the caffeine to ≤200 mg (or none) on most days, and take beta-alanine daily as a separate supplement. That keeps nearly all the performance benefit and protects your sleep.

*Not medical advice. Consult a doctor if pain or cardiovascular symptoms occur.*


## Answer B
Yes. The caffeine is the problem. The other two ingredients are fine but taken the wrong way. A full scoop at 6:00 pm with lights out at 11:00 pm will very likely cost you sleep, and over a training block, worse sleep will probably cost you more performance than the caffeine adds.

## Why a full scoop at 6 pm doesn't work

**Timing and half-life.** Caffeine's half-life averages about 5 h, with a wide individual range of roughly 2–10 h depending on CYP1A2 genetics, smoking, liver function and so on (recalled, not verified). If you take 350 mg at 6:00 pm, about 175 mg is probably still circulating at 11:00 pm. That's close to two coffees' worth at bedtime.

**Sleep data.** From what I recall of the research (not verified here, so check the sources):
- Drake et al. 2013 found 400 mg taken 6 h before bed still cut total sleep by roughly 1 h.
- A 2023 meta-analysis (Gardiner et al., *Sleep Medicine Reviews*) suggested a pre-workout-sized dose of about 200+ mg should be taken around 13 h before bed to avoid reducing sleep. For an 11 pm bedtime, that means before about 10 am.

Either way, a 5 h gap is far too short. You may still fall asleep fine, because people often don't notice the lost deep sleep, but sleep quality usually drops anyway.

**Daily total.** Two regular coffees are roughly 150–250 mg, depending on size and brew. Add 350 mg and you're at about 500–600 mg per day on training days. That's above the ~400 mg/day commonly cited as the upper safe intake for healthy adults (FDA/EFSA figure, recalled).

**Dose.** The performance benefit usually shows up at about 3 mg/kg, which for you is ~250 mg, and some studies see effects from ~2 mg/kg (~165 mg). You don't need 350 mg on top of your morning coffees.

## Other ingredients

**Beta-alanine (3.2 g).** Timing before training doesn't matter. It works by slowly raising carnosine levels in muscle, not as an immediate boost. What counts is the total daily intake over weeks:
- Take about 3.2–6.4 g per day, **every day including rest days**, for at least 4 weeks.
- Split it into 1.6 g doses taken with meals if the tingling (paresthesia, which is harmless) bothers you.
- At 5 scoops a week you'd get about 16 g per week instead of about 22–45 g, so loading would take longer.
- Expect the most benefit on high-rep work (sets of about 1–4 min), not heavy triples.

**Citrulline malate (6 g).** This is a reasonable dose, typically 6–8 g taken 45–60 min before training. The performance evidence is mixed: a small benefit at best for high-rep and repeated-set work. It's cheap and low-risk, so it's fine to keep.

## What I'd do

**Default for your 6:30 pm sessions:**
1. **Drop the evening caffeine entirely.** Use a **stim-free** pre-workout, or buy citrulline malate and beta-alanine separately; both are cheap as bulk powders.
   - ~5:30–5:45 pm: citrulline malate 6–8 g in water.
   - Beta-alanine: 1.6 g with breakfast and 1.6 g with dinner, 7 days a week.
2. **Keep your morning coffees and finish them by noon,** as you already do. If you want an afternoon lift, have ≤100 mg by about 1–2 pm at the latest.
3. **Use the current scoop as a special-occasion tool,** not your everyday pre-workout:
   - Take **half a scoop** (175 mg caffeine, about 2 mg/kg) at most once a week, for a test day or a session that really matters, and accept that night's sleep may be worse.
   - On those days, skip the second morning coffee so your total stays around 300 mg.
   - Using caffeine occasionally also keeps its effect stronger than taking it 5 days a week would.

**If you really want caffeine for every session:** a better long-term fix is training earlier, such as at lunch or before work, with caffeine 45–60 min before. If that's not possible, 6:30 pm plus caffeine means trading sleep for performance, and I'd make that trade only rarely.

## How to check it's working (2–3 weeks)

- **Sleep:** it should take you less than 20–30 min to fall asleep, you shouldn't wake up during the night more than usual, and you should wake feeling rested. If you use a wearable, compare resting heart rate and HRV on caffeine days and non-caffeine days.
- **Training:** reps or load on your main lifts at the same RPE. If these don't drop after going stim-free, the caffeine wasn't adding much.
- **Stop taking caffeine and cut back if you notice:**
  - palpitations or an irregular heartbeat
  - noticeably raised resting blood pressure
  - anxiety or jitteriness
  - taking more than 30 min to fall asleep on training nights
  - needing more coffee in the morning to feel normal (a sign the evening dose is hurting your sleep)

Not medical advice. If you have high blood pressure, heart rhythm issues or anxiety, or take medications (some antidepressants and antibiotics slow how fast you clear caffeine), check with a doctor before using high-caffeine products.
