## Answer A
Here are the numbers. I've kept only two caveats, both specific to your amlodipine and the beer, because they change how you should run this.

## What the study found (recalled, not verified here)

Laukkanen et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2015, Kuopio (KIHD) cohort: about 2,300 Finnish men aged 42–60, followed for about 20 years. The comparison was against men who used the sauna once a week:

- **4–7 sessions a week:** roughly 63% lower risk of sudden cardiac death, about 50% lower cardiovascular mortality, and about 40% lower all-cause mortality.
- **2–3 sessions a week:** smaller reductions, roughly 20–25%.
- **Session length:** sessions over about 19 minutes were linked to lower risk than sessions under about 11 minutes.
- **Temperature:** the average was around 79°C.

This is an observational study. Men healthy enough to sit in a sauna daily may also differ in other ways, so the "4–7x" effect is probably partly confounding. The mechanism is still plausible: repeated heat exposure raises heart rate to about 100–150 bpm, similar to moderate exercise, improves endothelial function and arterial compliance, and lowers blood pressure over time.

## Protocol

| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Temperature | 80–90°C. Your gym's 85°C is fine. |
| Session length | Weeks 1–2: 10–12 min. Weeks 3–4: 15 min. Week 5 onward: 15–20 min. |
| Frequency | Weeks 1–2: 3x/week. Weeks 3–4: 4x/week. Week 5 onward: 4–7x/week. |
| Rounds | 1 round of 15–20 min, or 2 rounds of 8–10 min with a 5-min cool-down between them. |
| Bench height | Start on the lower bench, which runs about 10–15°C cooler than the top. |
| Cool-down | 5–10 min at room temperature or a lukewarm shower. Skip ice plunges for the first month. |
| Hydration | 500 ml of water in the 30 min before and 500 ml after. You'll lose about 0.5–1 kg of sweat per session. |
| Timing | After training is fine. Leave 10 min after your last hard set so your heart rate settles first. |
| Exit rule | Get out if you feel lightheaded or nauseous or your heart rate goes above about 150. Don't push through to hit a time. |

## The two things that change the numbers

**1. Amlodipine.** It's a vasodilator, and so is heat. Together they make a blood-pressure drop when you stand up more likely. Its half-life is long, about 30–50 hours (recalled), so the timing of your morning dose doesn't matter much. What to do:
- Sit up for 1–2 minutes before standing to leave.
- Walk out slowly.
- Log your blood pressure before and 15 minutes after a few sessions. If it's under about 100/60 or you feel dizzy, shorten your sessions and tell your prescriber.

A blood pressure of 130/85 isn't a barrier to sauna use. Regular sauna may even bring it down a few mmHg over months, which is worth mentioning at your next check.

**2. Beer, then sauna. Swap the order.** Alcohol is the most consistent factor in sauna-related deaths in the Finnish data. It adds a third vasodilator on top of the heat and amlodipine, dehydrates you, and dulls your sense of when to get out. What to do on Saturdays:
- Workout, then sauna, then rehydrate with 500 ml of water, then the beer.
- If you've already started drinking, skip the sauna that day.

## Tracking

- **Resting heart rate and HRV** from a wearable: expect a gradual improvement over 8–12 weeks.
- **Home blood pressure:** take readings on 3 mornings a week.
- **Reassess at 12 weeks.**

*Not medical advice. Because of the amlodipine, your prescriber should know you're doing this.*


## Answer B
Here are the numbers. Two of them (the beer and the stand-up) apply to you because of your amlodipine and your Saturday routine. That's why they're in the table, not in a lecture.

**Target from the study** (Laukkanen et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2015, Finnish KIHD cohort, about 2,300 middle-aged men followed for about 20 years. I'm recalling these details, not verifying them, so check the paper.)
- **Frequency:** 4–7 sessions/week. In the study this group had roughly half the risk of dying from cardiovascular disease compared with men who used the sauna once a week.
- **Duration:** more than 19 min per session did better than less than 11 min.
- **Temperature:** the average was about 79°C, so your 85°C is in range.
- It's an observational study. Men who could sauna daily were probably healthier and had more free time to begin with. Treat the effect as likely real but probably smaller than 50%.

**Ramp-up**

| Weeks | Sessions/week | Time per session at ~85°C | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 3 | 10–12 min | 1 round, lower bench (roughly 10°C cooler) |
| 3–4 | 4 | 15 min | 1 round, or 2 × 8 min with a 5 min break |
| 5+ | 4–7 | 15–20 min | 1–2 rounds, upper bench if you tolerate it |

**Per-session numbers**
- **After training:** wait 10 min, until your heart rate is roughly back to resting + 20 bpm, then go in.
- **Water:** 400–500 ml during or right after. If you weigh yourself before and after, replace about 1–1.5 L for every kg lost.
- **Getting out:** sit up for 60 seconds before you stand, then stand slowly. Amlodipine dilates blood vessels and heat does the same, so the first minute on your feet is when a blood-pressure drop is most likely.
- **Cool-down:** 2–5 min of a lukewarm-to-cool shower. Skip the ice plunge for the first month.
- **Leave early** if you get lightheaded, feel your heart pounding irregularly, or feel nauseous.

**Saturday: switch the order.** Sauna first, then 500 ml of water, then the beer(s). Alcohol before or during the sauna is one of the most consistent factors in Finnish sauna deaths. A beer after the sauna costs you nothing.

**Blood pressure:** 130/85 on amlodipine 5 mg is controlled, and that's generally compatible with regular sauna use. Check your home BP before and after a few sessions in the first 2 weeks. If you're regularly under 100/60 or getting dizzy afterward, tell whoever prescribes the amlodipine. Are you taking anything else (other BP meds, diuretics, ED meds)? Any of those would change the stand-up and water numbers.

Not medical advice. It's worth a quick yes from your doctor, since you're on a BP medication.
