Yes. The caffeine is the problem. The other two ingredients are fine but taken the wrong way. A full scoop at 6:00 pm with lights out at 11:00 pm will very likely cost you sleep, and over a training block, worse sleep will probably cost you more performance than the caffeine adds.

## Why a full scoop at 6 pm doesn't work

**Timing and half-life.** Caffeine's half-life averages about 5 h, with a wide individual range of roughly 2–10 h depending on CYP1A2 genetics, smoking, liver function and so on (recalled, not verified). If you take 350 mg at 6:00 pm, about 175 mg is probably still circulating at 11:00 pm. That's close to two coffees' worth at bedtime.

**Sleep data.** From what I recall of the research (not verified here, so check the sources):
- Drake et al. 2013 found 400 mg taken 6 h before bed still cut total sleep by roughly 1 h.
- A 2023 meta-analysis (Gardiner et al., *Sleep Medicine Reviews*) suggested a pre-workout-sized dose of about 200+ mg should be taken around 13 h before bed to avoid reducing sleep. For an 11 pm bedtime, that means before about 10 am.

Either way, a 5 h gap is far too short. You may still fall asleep fine, because people often don't notice the lost deep sleep, but sleep quality usually drops anyway.

**Daily total.** Two regular coffees are roughly 150–250 mg, depending on size and brew. Add 350 mg and you're at about 500–600 mg per day on training days. That's above the ~400 mg/day commonly cited as the upper safe intake for healthy adults (FDA/EFSA figure, recalled).

**Dose.** The performance benefit usually shows up at about 3 mg/kg, which for you is ~250 mg, and some studies see effects from ~2 mg/kg (~165 mg). You don't need 350 mg on top of your morning coffees.

## Other ingredients

**Beta-alanine (3.2 g).** Timing before training doesn't matter. It works by slowly raising carnosine levels in muscle, not as an immediate boost. What counts is the total daily intake over weeks:
- Take about 3.2–6.4 g per day, **every day including rest days**, for at least 4 weeks.
- Split it into 1.6 g doses taken with meals if the tingling (paresthesia, which is harmless) bothers you.
- At 5 scoops a week you'd get about 16 g per week instead of about 22–45 g, so loading would take longer.
- Expect the most benefit on high-rep work (sets of about 1–4 min), not heavy triples.

**Citrulline malate (6 g).** This is a reasonable dose, typically 6–8 g taken 45–60 min before training. The performance evidence is mixed: a small benefit at best for high-rep and repeated-set work. It's cheap and low-risk, so it's fine to keep.

## What I'd do

**Default for your 6:30 pm sessions:**
1. **Drop the evening caffeine entirely.** Use a **stim-free** pre-workout, or buy citrulline malate and beta-alanine separately; both are cheap as bulk powders.
   - ~5:30–5:45 pm: citrulline malate 6–8 g in water.
   - Beta-alanine: 1.6 g with breakfast and 1.6 g with dinner, 7 days a week.
2. **Keep your morning coffees and finish them by noon,** as you already do. If you want an afternoon lift, have ≤100 mg by about 1–2 pm at the latest.
3. **Use the current scoop as a special-occasion tool,** not your everyday pre-workout:
   - Take **half a scoop** (175 mg caffeine, about 2 mg/kg) at most once a week, for a test day or a session that really matters, and accept that night's sleep may be worse.
   - On those days, skip the second morning coffee so your total stays around 300 mg.
   - Using caffeine occasionally also keeps its effect stronger than taking it 5 days a week would.

**If you really want caffeine for every session:** a better long-term fix is training earlier, such as at lunch or before work, with caffeine 45–60 min before. If that's not possible, 6:30 pm plus caffeine means trading sleep for performance, and I'd make that trade only rarely.

## How to check it's working (2–3 weeks)

- **Sleep:** it should take you less than 20–30 min to fall asleep, you shouldn't wake up during the night more than usual, and you should wake feeling rested. If you use a wearable, compare resting heart rate and HRV on caffeine days and non-caffeine days.
- **Training:** reps or load on your main lifts at the same RPE. If these don't drop after going stim-free, the caffeine wasn't adding much.
- **Stop taking caffeine and cut back if you notice:**
  - palpitations or an irregular heartbeat
  - noticeably raised resting blood pressure
  - anxiety or jitteriness
  - taking more than 30 min to fall asleep on training nights
  - needing more coffee in the morning to feel normal (a sign the evening dose is hurting your sleep)

Not medical advice. If you have high blood pressure, heart rhythm issues or anxiety, or take medications (some antidepressants and antibiotics slow how fast you clear caffeine), check with a doctor before using high-caffeine products.
