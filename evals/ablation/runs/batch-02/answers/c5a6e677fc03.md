Yes. The caffeine dose and its timing are the problems. The other two ingredients are fine, but beta-alanine works differently from how the label suggests.

## 1. Caffeine: too much, too late for an 11 pm bedtime

**The math at your schedule:**
- Scoop at ~6:00 pm = 350 mg.
- Caffeine's half-life averages about 5 h in healthy adults, but it varies a lot between people, roughly 3 to 7+ h (recalled, not verified here). Genetics (CYP1A2) accounts for much of that.
- At an average rate, about **~175 mg is still in your system at 11 pm**. That's close to drinking two coffees as you get into bed. If you clear caffeine slowly, it's more.
- Your two morning coffees add roughly 150–200 mg a day (estimate, since coffee varies widely). Adding a full scoop brings you to **~500–550 mg/day**, above the commonly cited 400 mg/day guideline for healthy adults (recalled, not verified here).

**Why "I fall asleep fine" isn't a good test:** caffeine taken within about 6 h of bed reduces deep (slow-wave) sleep and total sleep time even when you don't notice it. As I recall, a well-known study (Drake et al., 2013) found that 400 mg taken 6 h before bed still cut about an hour of sleep. Treat the details as recalled, not verified. Worse sleep over time costs you more recovery and progress than a pre-workout adds in one session.

**You also don't need 350 mg.** The performance benefit of caffeine starts at about **2–3 mg/kg**, and higher doses mostly add side effects (recalled from the ISSN caffeine position stand, not verified here). For you at 82 kg, that's **~160–250 mg**.

## 2. Beta-alanine: timing doesn't matter, daily dosing does

Beta-alanine doesn't work acutely. It builds up muscle carnosine over **4+ weeks of daily intake** (typically 3.2–6.4 g/day, recalled). This means:
- Taking it 30 min pre-workout gives no extra benefit.
- You'd need it **on rest days too**, and you don't want 350 mg of caffeine on those days.
- The tingling (paresthesia) is harmless. Splitting the dose (e.g., 2 × 1.6 g with meals) reduces it.

## 3. Citrulline malate: fine as is

6 g, 30–60 min pre-workout, falls within the dose range used in studies (~6–8 g). The evidence for a performance benefit is **mixed and small**. It might help you get a rep or two more on high-rep sets, but don't expect much from it.

## What I'd change

**Option A: keep this product (simplest)**
- Take **half a scoop** (~175 mg caffeine) about 30 min before training.
- Use it **only for your 2–3 hardest sessions per week**, not all 5. This keeps your caffeine sensitivity high and your weekly sleep load lower.
- Downside: it also halves beta-alanine and citrulline, below their effective doses.

**Option B: split the ingredients (better long-term)**
| Ingredient | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Beta-alanine (plain powder) | 3.2 g/day, split 2 × 1.6 g | With meals, **every day** |
| Citrulline malate | 6–8 g | 30–60 min pre-workout |
| Caffeine | 0–200 mg (e.g., 100–200 mg tablet or a coffee) | Pre-workout, key sessions only, **not after ~5 pm** if possible |

For your 6:30 pm sessions, the cleanest option is a **stim-free pre-workout on most days**, with a small caffeine dose saved for your heaviest day.

**Also consider:**
- Move one of your morning coffees earlier or drop it on days you use caffeine pre-workout, so you stay ≤400 mg/day in total.
- **Test it on yourself:** track sleep for 2 weeks (a wearable or just a sleep diary noting sleep onset, wake-ups and morning energy) with caffeine pre-workout vs. without. Your own data beats population averages here.
- Stop and see a doctor if you get palpitations, chest discomfort or a noticeably raised resting heart rate. That matters more with evening dosing on top of morning coffee.

**Bottom line:** 350 mg at 6 pm, five days a week, on top of two coffees, will very likely cut into your deep sleep even if you don't feel it. Cut the caffeine to ≤200 mg (or none) on most days, and take beta-alanine daily as a separate supplement. That keeps nearly all the performance benefit and protects your sleep.

*Not medical advice. Consult a doctor if pain or cardiovascular symptoms occur.*
