**There are 116 valid assignments, so 116/720 = 29/180 ≈ 16.1% of shuffles are valid.** On average your randomizer needs about **6.2 shuffles** to hit one.

I counted it two independent ways and both give 116.

### Method 1: count the bad cases and subtract (inclusion–exclusion)

Start from the shuffles where nobody draws themselves. For 6 people there are D(6) = **265** of these. Then remove the ones where someone draws their partner. There are four "partner" events: A→B, B→A, C→D, D→C.

- **One event fixed, e.g. A→B:** the other 5 people fill the remaining 5 slots, and C, D, E, F can't draw themselves. B can't draw B anyway, because B's slot is already taken. That gives 5! − 4·4! + 6·3! − 4·2! + 1! = 120 − 96 + 36 − 8 + 1 = **53**. With 4 such events: 4 × 53 = 212.
- **Two events fixed:**
  - Both halves of one couple (A→B and B→A): the remaining 4 people must be deranged, D(4) = 9. Two couples give 2 × 9 = 18.
  - One event from each couple (e.g. A→B and C→D): the remaining 4 people are B, D, E, F, drawing A, C, E, F. Only E and F are restricted, so 4! − 2·3! + 2! = 14. There are 4 such pairs: 56.
  - Total for pairs: 74.
- **Three events fixed (e.g. A→B, B→A, C→D):** the remaining people D, E, F draw C, E, F with E and F not drawing themselves. That gives 3! − 2·2! + 1 = 3. There are 4 such triples: 12.
- **All four events fixed:** E and F must swap, which is 1 way.

Bad cases = 212 − 74 + 12 − 1 = **149**, so valid = 265 − 149 = **116**.

### Method 2: rook polynomial (a separate check)

Draw a 6×6 grid of giver × receiver and mark the forbidden cells:
- a full 2×2 block for {Anna, Ben} (AA, AB, BA, BB)
- a full 2×2 block for {Carla, Dev}
- the single cells EE and FF

The rook polynomial of the forbidden cells is:

```
(1 + 4x + 2x²)² · (1 + x)² = 1 + 10x + 37x² + 64x³ + 56x⁴ + 24x⁵ + 4x⁶
```

The number of valid assignments is Σ (−1)ᵏ rₖ (6−k)!:

```
720 − 10·120 + 37·24 − 64·6 + 56·2 − 24·1 + 4·1
= 720 − 1200 + 888 − 384 + 112 − 24 + 4
= 116  ✓
```

As a sanity check, the same method with only the 6 self-cells, (1+x)⁶, gives 265, which is the known derangement count. It also makes sense that the answer is well below 265: the couple rule removes a lot of cases, because it blocks 4 of the 30 non-self edges.

### What this means for your retry loop

Each shuffle is an independent trial that succeeds with probability p = 29/180 ≈ 0.161. The number of shuffles you need follows a geometric distribution.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Expected shuffles | 1/p = 180/29 ≈ **6.2** |
| P(still not done after k shuffles) | (151/180)ᵏ ≈ 0.839ᵏ |
| Done within 18 shuffles | ≥ 95% |
| Done within 27 shuffles | ≥ 99% |
| Done within 60 shuffles | ≈ 99.997% |

Rejection sampling is fine here. It's cheap, and every valid assignment comes up with equal probability (1/116 each), so you don't need anything smarter. If your two hand counts came out as 265 and something else, the 265 was probably plain derangements without the couple rule applied.
