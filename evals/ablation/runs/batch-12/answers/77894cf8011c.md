**There are 116 valid assignments, so 116/720 = 29/180 ≈ 16.1% of shuffles are valid.** On average your randomizer needs 180/29 ≈ **6.2 shuffles**.

I counted it two different ways and got 116 both times.

## Method 1: Inclusion–exclusion with a forbidden board

Write the forbidden (giver → receiver) pairs as cells in a 6×6 grid:

- Anna and Ben can't draw anyone in {Anna, Ben}. That's a full 2×2 block.
- Carla and Dev can't draw anyone in {Carla, Dev}. That's another full 2×2 block.
- Eli can't draw Eli, and Fran can't draw Fran. Those are two single cells.

These pieces share no rows or columns, so the board's rook polynomial is the product of the pieces' polynomials. $r_k$ is the number of ways to place $k$ forbidden pairs that don't clash:

- 2×2 block: $1 + 4x + 2x^2$
- Single cell: $1 + x$

$$(1+4x+2x^2)^2(1+x)^2 = 1 + 10x + 37x^2 + 64x^3 + 56x^4 + 24x^5 + 4x^6$$

Valid count $= \sum_k (-1)^k r_k (6-k)!$:

| k | $r_k$ | $(6-k)!$ | term |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 720 | +720 |
| 1 | 10 | 120 | −1200 |
| 2 | 37 | 24 | +888 |
| 3 | 64 | 6 | −384 |
| 4 | 56 | 2 | +112 |
| 5 | 24 | 1 | −24 |
| 6 | 4 | 1 | +4 |

Total: **116**

## Method 2 (independent check): start from derangements

Six people can be assigned so that nobody draws themselves in $D_6 = 265$ ways. Next, remove the ones where someone draws their partner. There are four such events: A→B, B→A, C→D, D→C.

- **Single events:** Of the derangements, 53 have A→B, and the same goes for each of the other three. Sum: 4 × 53 = 212
- **Pairs of events:**
  - A→B and B→A: the other 4 people form a derangement, so 9. C→D with D→C also gives 9.
  - One arc from each couple, e.g. A→B and C→D: Ben, Dev, Eli and Fran must go to Anna, Carla, Eli and Fran, with Eli≠Eli and Fran≠Fran. That's 24 − 6 − 6 + 2 = 14 ways, and there are 4 such pairs, so 56.
  - Sum: 9 + 9 + 56 = 74
- **Triples of events,** e.g. both Anna/Ben arcs plus C→D: Dev, Eli and Fran go to Carla, Eli and Fran with no one drawing themselves. That's 6 − 2 − 2 + 1 = 3 ways, and there are 4 such triples, so 12.
- **All four events:** Eli and Fran must swap, so 1.

Bad derangements = 212 − 74 + 12 − 1 = 149, so valid = 265 − 149 = **116** ✓

## For your retry estimate

- P(one shuffle is valid) = $p = 29/180 \approx 0.161$
- The number of shuffles is geometric, with mean $1/p \approx 6.2$
- P(still no valid shuffle after $n$ tries) $= (151/180)^n$:
  - after 10 tries: about 17%
  - after 20 tries: about 3%
  - after 27 tries: under 1%
  - after 50 tries: about 0.015%

Rejection sampling works fine at this size. It also gives you a uniformly random valid assignment, which is a good property to keep. If one of your hand counts was **265**, that's the number of derangements, where only "nobody draws themselves" applies and the couple rule is left out.
