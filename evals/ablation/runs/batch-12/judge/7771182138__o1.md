## Answer A
I can't keep every rule exactly as written, because four of them contradict each other. That's the most likely reason the bot behaves erratically: on each turn the model picks whichever rule it's paying attention to. Below are the conflicts, how I resolved each one, and the cleaned prompt. Check the resolutions before you ship. They are business decisions, not wording fixes.

## Conflicts and how I resolved them

| # | Conflict | Resolution in the prompt | Your call? |
|---|----------|--------------------------|------------|
| 1 | "Under 60 words" vs. "walk through the full 5-step checklist" | One step per reply, then wait for the result. Both rules hold. | Confirm the bot can stop early once the issue is fixed. The original says "full" checklist, but going through all five steps after a fix makes no sense. |
| 2 | "Never mention competitors" vs. "give an honest comparison with Netgear/TP-Link" | Never bring up competitors yourself. If the customer asks, give a brief, factual, non-disparaging comparison, using only facts you supply. Otherwise, say so and point to the specs page. | **Yes.** If legal wants zero competitor talk, delete the comparison rule. Don't keep both. |
| 3 | "Refunds allowed within 30 days" vs. "never promise refunds" | These can both hold. The bot may state the policy but may not confirm that a specific customer qualifies. It always escalates to billing. | No |
| 4 | "Casual" vs. "formal" | "Friendly, clear, professional. Plain language, no slang." | Minor |

**A safety problem you didn't ask about:** a factory reset (step 3) wipes the customer's Wi-Fi name, password and settings. It currently comes *before* the firmware update and the ISP outage check, so customers may wipe their router over an outage on the ISP's side. I kept your order because you asked me to keep the rules. I did add a mandatory warning and confirmation before the reset. I'd recommend this order instead: outage check → power cycle → WAN light → firmware → reset last. Changing it is one edit in the checklist.

## Cleaned prompt

```markdown
# Role
You are Max, the customer support assistant for Brightline Routers.

# Style
- Friendly, clear, professional. Plain language, no slang, no jargon without a short explanation.
- Every reply is 60 words or fewer. If a topic needs more, split it across turns.

# Connectivity troubleshooting
When a customer reports a connectivity problem, guide them through these steps in order.
Give ONE step per reply, then ask for the result before moving on.
Stop as soon as the problem is solved.

1. Unplug the router, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in.
2. Check the WAN light and ask the customer what it shows.
3. Factory reset: hold the pinhole button for 10 seconds.
   BEFORE this step, warn that a reset erases the Wi-Fi name, password and all settings,
   and get the customer's explicit OK. If they decline, skip to step 4.
4. Update the firmware from the Brightline app.
5. Check the ISP's outage page.

If all steps fail, apologize and tell the customer you're escalating to a human agent.

# Refunds
- Policy: refunds are available within 30 days of purchase. You may state this policy.
- Never promise or confirm a refund, and never say a specific customer qualifies.
- For any refund question, direct the customer to billing@brightline.example.

# Competitors
- Never bring up competitors on your own.
- If the customer asks how Brightline compares to Netgear or TP-Link, give a brief,
  factual, non-disparaging comparison using ONLY the facts listed under "Comparison facts".
- If the answer isn't in those facts, say you don't have a verified comparison
  and suggest the customer check each product's spec page.

# Comparison facts
[Fill in approved, current facts, or delete the Competitors section and replace it with:
"If asked about competitors, politely say you can only help with Brightline products."]

# Unknowns
If you don't know something or it isn't covered here, say so plainly. Never guess
specs, prices, policies or dates. Offer to connect the customer to a human agent.

# Priority if rules seem to conflict
Safety warnings > refund rules > 60-word limit > everything else.
```

## Changes and why

- **Sections and bullets instead of one paragraph.** GPT-4o-class models tend to follow rules in separate, labeled sections more reliably than rules mixed into prose. This is a general tendency, not something I tested on your bot.
- **Explicit priority line.** It gives the model a way to decide when two rules pull against each other, instead of picking at random.
- **Comparison facts slot.** Without it, "give an honest comparison" asks the model to recall competitor specs, which is where it's most likely to make things up.
- **Escalation paths** for failed troubleshooting and for unknowns. The original told the bot to admit ignorance but not what to do next.

## Answer B
I can't keep every rule exactly as written, because four of them contradict each other. That's probably most of your erratic behavior: the model resolves each conflict differently from one chat to the next. Below is a tightened prompt with my suggested resolution for each conflict. **You need to confirm three of these decisions** (marked ⚠) before you ship it.

## Conflicts in the original

| # | Conflict | What the bot does now | Proposed resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ⚠ | "Never mention competitors" vs. "give an honest comparison with Netgear/TP-Link" | Flips between refusing and comparing | **You decide.** Both options are in the prompt below. Delete one. |
| 2 | "Under 60 words" vs. "walk through the full 5-step checklist" | Crams all 5 steps into one reply, or breaks the limit | One step per reply, confirm the result, then move to the next. Every reply stays under 60 words and the full checklist still gets covered. |
| 3 | "Refunds allowed within 30 days" vs. "never promise refunds" | Sometimes tells customers "you'll get a refund" | Bot may state the policy but never confirms eligibility. It always escalates to billing. |
| 4 | "Friendly and casual" vs. "professional and formal" | Unpredictable tone | Warm, polite, plain language. No slang, no stiff formality. |

## Other problems

- **⚠ Step order is risky.** The factory reset (step 3) wipes the customer's settings, yet it comes before two harmless checks: the firmware update and the ISP outage page. I moved the outage check first and the reset last, and the bot must get the customer's confirmation before a reset. Also, updating firmware from the app probably needs a working connection, so it may fail while the connection is down. Please check this with your support team.
- **⚠ "Always walk through the full checklist"** would make the bot keep going after the problem is fixed. I changed it to stop once the connection works. Revert this if you really want all steps every time.
- **The WAN light step has no meaning attached.** The bot doesn't know what the colors mean, so it will invent them. I added a placeholder for you to fill in.
- **"Helpful assistant" is filler.** I dropped it. The role line now states the scope.

## Cleaned-up prompt

```
## Role
You are Max, customer support assistant for Brightline Routers. You help customers with Brightline router setup, connectivity, and account questions.

## Style
- Keep every reply under 60 words.
- Tone: warm, polite, plain language. No slang, no jargon unless the customer uses it.
- If you don't know something, say so. Do not guess about product specs, policies, or order details.

## Connectivity problems
Guide the customer through these steps in order, ONE step per reply. After each step, ask whether the connection works. Stop as soon as it does.
1. Check the ISP outage page for their area.
2. Power-cycle the router: unplug it for 30 seconds, then plug it back in.
3. Check the WAN light. [FILL IN: what each light state means and what to do]
4. Update firmware from the Brightline app.
5. Factory reset: hold the pinhole button for 10 seconds. BEFORE this step, warn that a reset erases all settings (Wi-Fi name, password, custom config) and get the customer's explicit OK.
If all steps fail, say so and [FILL IN: escalation path, e.g. support ticket or phone number].

## Refunds
- Policy: refunds are available within 30 days of purchase. You may state this policy.
- Never promise or confirm that a specific customer will get a refund, even if they appear to be within 30 days.
- Direct all refund requests and questions to billing@brightline.example.

## Competitors
[KEEP ONE OPTION, DELETE THE OTHER]
Option A: Do not name or discuss competitors. If asked to compare, politely decline and offer to explain Brightline features instead.
Option B: If asked to compare with Netgear or TP-Link, give a brief, honest, factual comparison. Do not disparage competitors. If you are unsure of a competitor's specs, say so.
```
