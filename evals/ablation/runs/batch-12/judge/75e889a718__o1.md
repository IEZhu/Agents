## Latest user message
I'm the designer on a B2B project management tool (web app, ~40k weekly active users, mostly teams of 5-30). Support got 180 tickets last quarter from people who deleted tasks or whole boards by accident, and there's currently no way to restore them. Our PM wants a fix in the next sprint: every delete action (single task, bulk delete of selected tasks, board, comment) opens a modal where the user has to type the item's name (or "DELETE" for bulk) before the button enables. Engineering says it's 3 days of work. I need to write the spec for this tomorrow. Can you help me define the modal's copy, states and edge cases?

## Rubric (what a strong answer does)
1. Raises, before or alongside the spec, that applying a typed confirmation to every delete (including frequent ones like single tasks and comments) will likely become habitual and be clicked or typed through without reading, reducing its protective value over time
2. Identifies at least one further downstream effect of the added friction beyond annoyance, e.g. users avoid deleting and boards fill with stale tasks, or users look for workarounds (such as archiving or moving items instead of deleting)
3. Points out that the underlying problem is irreversibility and recommends a recoverable path (undo toast, trash/recently deleted with a retention window, or soft delete), with heavy confirmation reserved for high-impact, hard-to-reverse actions such as deleting a board
4. Still addresses the actual request with usable content: modal copy and at least a few concrete states or edge cases for whichever confirmations remain

## Answer A
Yes, I can help. First, though, I'd push back on the scope before you write the spec, because I think the PM's plan will cost you real usability and probably won't make the tickets go away.

## The problem: the fix doesn't match the complaint

The complaint in those 180 tickets is "I deleted something and **can't get it back**." A type-to-confirm modal only makes deleting slower. It doesn't let anyone restore anything. Three reasons I doubt it will work well:

1. **People get used to confirmations.** When every delete shows a hard modal, people learn to push through it on autopilot. This is a well-documented pattern (Raskin describes it in *The Humane Interface*). Typing a name slows the habit down but doesn't stop it, and people still delete the wrong board on purpose, then realise their mistake.
2. **It adds friction to your most common actions.** Deleting a single task or comment is routine in a project management tool. If users have to type a task name every time they remove a duplicate or a typo'd comment, that's a lot of repeated effort across 40k weekly users. The support tickets about the friction will replace the tickets about lost data.
3. **Typing "DELETE" for bulk actions doesn't check what matters.** The risk with bulk delete is having the wrong items selected. Typing a fixed word checks that the user can type, not that the selection is right.

The heuristics point the same way. **User control and freedom (#3)** asks for an undo, not a stronger gate. **Error prevention (#5)** says heavy confirmation should be reserved for actions that are rare, high-impact, and can't be reversed.

## What I'd recommend instead: match the protection to the risk

| Action | How often | Impact | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delete comment | High | Low | No modal. Soft delete + **Undo toast** |
| Delete single task | High | Low–medium | No modal. Soft delete + **Undo toast** + Trash |
| Bulk delete tasks | Medium | Medium–high | Simple confirm modal that shows the count and a preview, + Undo toast + Trash |
| Delete board | Low | High (affects the whole team) | **Type-to-confirm modal** + Trash |

"Soft delete" means the item is marked as deleted and hidden rather than erased, so it can come back. That is the part that actually fixes the tickets.

**About the 3-day estimate:** that covers the modal only. Soft delete (a `deleted_at` flag, filtering deleted items out of queries, a restore endpoint, a purge job, a Trash view) is a bigger change. I can't estimate it for your codebase, so ask engineering. If it doesn't fit in one sprint, a phased plan could work:

- **Sprint 1:** soft delete on the backend, the Undo toast for tasks, comments and bulk deletes, and the board type-to-confirm modal.
- **Sprint 2:** a Trash view with restore, plus the purge job.

Even without the Trash view, admins or support could restore soft-deleted items manually, which would already answer most of those 180 tickets.

**Before tomorrow, if you can:** sort 20–30 of the tickets by what was lost (task, bulk, board, comment) and how it happened (wrong item, wrong selection, regret afterwards). That gives you evidence to present to the PM instead of a matter of opinion.

Below is the spec for both parts: the recommended pattern, and the type-to-confirm modal for boards. If the PM still wants type-to-confirm everywhere, the modal section works for every item type.

## 1. Undo toast (tasks, comments, bulk)

**Copy**
- Single task: `Task deleted.` **[Undo]**
- Comment: `Comment deleted.` **[Undo]**
- Bulk: `24 tasks deleted.` **[Undo]**
- After undo: `Task restored.` / `24 tasks restored.`
- Undo failed: `Couldn't restore the task. Try again or restore it from Trash.` **[Retry]**
- If Trash exists, add a link to the toast: `View in Trash`.

**Behaviour**
- The item disappears from the UI immediately (optimistic delete) and is soft-deleted on the server.
- Keep the toast open long enough to act on it, and keep it open while it has hover or keyboard focus. WCAG's timing criterion (2.2.1) points this way, but I'm recalling it from memory, so check the exact wording. Pick a duration and test it; don't hard-code a number without testing.
- A new delete replaces the toast. Decide whether Undo reverses only the most recent delete (simplest) or whether toasts stack. I'd go with most recent only, and let Trash handle anything older.
- Keyboard shortcut: optionally support Ctrl/Cmd+Z right after a delete, as long as it doesn't clash with undo inside text fields.

**Toast edge cases**
- **The user navigates away:** Undo is no longer available, so the item must be findable in Trash (or restorable by support).
- **Someone else edits a task the user just deleted:** because it's a soft delete, their edit either fails cleanly (`This task was deleted by Alex. Restore it?`) or is kept. Decide which, and write the message for it.
- **A deleted task is referenced elsewhere** (dependency, subtask, mention): show a greyed `Deleted task`, and restore the link if the task is restored.

## 2. Bulk delete confirm (no typing)

**Copy**
- Title: `Delete 24 tasks?`
- Body: a list of the first 5 task names, then `and 19 more`. After that: `You can undo this right after, or restore them from Trash for 30 days.` (Use your real retention period. If there's no Trash yet, give the honest version.)
- Buttons: `Cancel` · `Delete 24 tasks` (destructive style)

Putting the count on the button makes the user look at the scope, which is the actual risk. If the PM insists on typing for bulk, have users **type the number (`24`)** rather than `DELETE`, because the number checks the selection.

**Edge cases**
- **The selection spans several pages or has filters applied:** say so, e.g. `Includes tasks not shown in the current filter.` This is a common way to delete the wrong thing.
- **Some selected tasks are ones the user can't delete:** `20 of 24 tasks will be deleted. You don't have permission to delete 4.` with an option to see which.
- **Partial failure:** `21 tasks deleted. 3 couldn't be deleted.` **[Retry]** **[See which]**

## 3. Type-to-confirm modal (boards)

**Copy**
- Title: `Delete board "Q3 Launch"?`
- Consequences, with real numbers: `This deletes the board and its 142 tasks, 38 comments and 12 attachments for everyone on this team.`
- Recovery line (choose one, and never claim recovery that doesn't exist):
  - With Trash: `You can restore it from Trash for 30 days.`
  - Without Trash: `This can't be undone.`
- Input label (a visible label, not a placeholder): `Type the board name to confirm`, with the name shown above the field in a monospace style that can be selected: `Q3 Launch`
- Buttons: `Cancel` · `Delete board`

**States**

| State | UI |
|---|---|
| Default | Input empty and focused. Delete button inactive. Cancel is always available |
| Typing, no match | No error while the user is still typing |
| Match | Delete button becomes active (destructive colour + label, not colour alone) |
| Submit with no match (Enter or click) | Inline error: `The name doesn't match. Type "Q3 Launch" exactly.` Linked to the input via `aria-describedby` |
| Submitting | Button shows a spinner and reads `Deleting…`. Input and buttons locked. Esc disabled so the request isn't left half-done |
| Success | Modal closes, user goes to the boards list, toast: `Board "Q3 Launch" deleted.` (+ `Undo` / `View in Trash` if soft delete exists) |
| Error: network/server | Modal stays open with the text kept: `Couldn't delete the board. Check your connection and try again.` |
| Error: no permission | `Only board admins can delete this board.` This should really be prevented upstream by hiding or disabling the menu item with a reason |
| Error: already deleted | `This board was already deleted by Alex.` **[Close]** |

**Disabled-button note:** a natively `disabled` button can't be focused and gives screen-reader users no reason. Use `aria-disabled="true"` so the button stays focusable, and show the "doesn't match" message if it's activated. Alternatively, keep it enabled and validate on submit.

**Matching rules (write these into the spec explicitly)**
- Trim spaces at the start and end. Collapse repeated internal spaces.
- Matching is **case-insensitive**. Case sensitivity adds frustration without adding safety.
- Normalise Unicode (NFC), so accented names typed on different keyboards still match.
- **Allow paste.** Blocking paste mostly punishes password-manager users and people with motor impairments. The friction comes from reading the name and deciding to delete, not from typing it.
- **Very long names** (over ~40 characters): consider asking for the first N characters, or letting users type `delete board` instead.
- **Names made of emoji or unusual scripts:** these are hard to type on some keyboards, so fall back to a typed phrase.
- **Duplicate board names:** show extra context in the modal (team, owner, number of tasks) so the user knows which board it is.
- **Localisation:** any typed keyword must be translated and must match the UI language, not English-only `DELETE`.
- **The board changes while the modal is open** (someone adds tasks or renames it): re-check on submit. If it was renamed, show `This board was renamed to "X". Type the new name to confirm.`

## Accessibility checklist

- Modal: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, `aria-labelledby` pointing at the title, `aria-describedby` pointing at the consequences text.
- Focus moves to the input when the modal opens, stays trapped inside the modal, and returns to the element that triggered the delete when it closes. If that element was deleted, focus moves to the nearest sensible element (the next task or the list heading).
- Esc and Cancel both close the modal (except while submitting).
- Toasts use `role="status"` / `aria-live="polite"`. The Undo button can be reached by keyboard without hunting for it; consider a shortcut and announce it.
- Destructive meaning is carried by the label text (`Delete board`), not red alone. Text contrast is at least 4.5:1, including on the destructive button.
- Touch targets are at least 44px, which matters for the toast's Undo button on mobile.

## Testing recommendation

- **Before launch:** run 5 short sessions with real customers covering four tasks: delete a task and undo it, bulk delete with a filter applied, delete a board, and restore something from Trash. Watch whether people read the board consequences text or skip it.
- **After launch, track:**
  - accidental-delete tickets per quarter (baseline: 180)
  - Undo click rate. A high rate confirms these were accidents and that the toast is working.
  - restores from Trash
  - time taken to complete board deletion
  - drop-off at the modal

If you'd like, I can also draft a one-paragraph rationale for the PM that sets out the tiered approach against the original plan.


## Answer B
I'll give you the full spec below. First, though, I'd push back on the premise before you write it up. A type-to-confirm modal on every delete probably won't reduce those 180 tickets much, and it will slow down 40k people every day.

## Why "type to confirm everywhere" is likely the wrong fix

- **The tickets are about recovery, not confirmation.** People aren't writing in because deleting was too easy. They're writing in because once they made a mistake, *there was no way to restore anything*. A modal still leaves recovery impossible (Nielsen #3, user control and freedom; #9, help users recover from errors).
- **Friction on frequent actions leads to habituation.** Deleting tasks and comments is routine. If every delete needs typing, people learn to go through it on autopilot. Typing "DELETE" becomes muscle memory, and the protection is gone for the one delete that mattered. Most accidental deletes are "I meant to, but it was the wrong one," and people rarely catch that at the confirmation step.
- **It doesn't cover the other failure modes.** A modal can't help when a teammate deletes something you needed, or when someone realises a day later that the board still mattered. In a team tool with 5–30 people per team, I'd guess (not verified) that these make up a real share of the tickets.
- **Standard > custom.** The common pattern in collaboration tools is a tiered approach: undo plus a trash for routine deletes, with type-to-confirm reserved for rare, high-blast-radius actions like deleting a whole board or workspace.

**Before the spec is locked, ask support for a breakdown of the 180 tickets:** boards vs. bulk vs. single tasks vs. comments, and "I deleted it" vs. "someone else deleted it." That tells you where the protection has to go. It's about an hour of work and it settles the argument with the PM using evidence rather than opinion.

## Recommended approach: friction scaled to impact, recovery everywhere

| Action | Confirmation | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Delete comment | None | Undo toast |
| Delete single task | None | Undo toast + Trash |
| Bulk delete tasks | Simple confirm modal showing the count (no typing) | Undo toast + Trash |
| Delete board | Type-to-confirm with the board name | Trash |

**Trash** means soft delete: the item gets a `deleted_at` flag, is hidden from views, can be restored for N days (30 is a common default), and is then purged.

**About the 3-day estimate:** it covers the modal only. Soft delete, a restore endpoint, a Trash view and a purge job are more work. Ask engineering to estimate them separately. If they don't fit this sprint, a realistic split is:
- **Sprint 1:** soft delete on the backend, undo toasts, and the board type-to-confirm modal. This alone gives you recovery, even if support has to run restores by hand at first.
- **Sprint 2:** a self-serve Trash view.

If the PM insists on shipping only modals this sprint, confine type-to-confirm to boards and use the simple count modal for bulk deletes. That keeps most of the protection with far less daily friction.

## Spec: board delete modal (type-to-confirm)

### Wireframe

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Delete board "Q3 Launch"?                 ✕ │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ This deletes the board and everything in it │
│ for everyone in Acme Workspace:             │
│   • 142 tasks  • 38 comments  • 12 files    │
│                                             │
│ You can restore it from Trash for 30 days.  │
│                                             │
│ Type Q3 Launch to confirm                   │
│ ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐ │
│ │                                         │ │
│ └─────────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│                                             │
│                   [ Cancel ] [Delete board] │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Copy

- **Title:** `Delete board "{Board name}"?`
- **Body:** `This deletes the board and everything in it for everyone in {Workspace}: {n} tasks, {n} comments, {n} files.`
- **Recovery line:** `You can restore it from Trash for 30 days.` If there's no Trash yet, use this instead: `This can't be undone.` Don't imply recovery that doesn't exist.
- **Field label:** `Type {Board name} to confirm`. Show the name visually distinct, for example in bold or a code style, so users know exactly what to type.
- **Primary button:** `Delete board`. Name the action, not "OK" or "Confirm".
- **Secondary button:** `Cancel`
- **Success toast:** `Board "{name}" deleted.` with a `View Trash` link, or `Undo` if you support it.
- **Error, network or server:** `Couldn't delete "{name}". Check your connection and try again.` Keep the typed input.
- **Error, permission:** `You don't have permission to delete this board. Ask a workspace admin.`
- **Error, already deleted:** `This board was already deleted by {user}.` with a `View Trash` link.

Leave out a count that is zero, for example "0 files."

### States

1. **Default:** field empty, primary button disabled, focus in the field.
2. **Partial or non-matching input:** button stays disabled. Don't show an error while the user is still typing.
3. **Match:** button enabled.
4. **Submitting:** button reads `Deleting…` with a spinner. Field, buttons and ✕ are disabled so a double-submit can't happen.
5. **Success:** modal closes, the toast appears, the user goes back to the boards list, and focus moves to a sensible element (the list heading or the next board).
6. **Error:** inline alert at the top of the modal, input kept, button enabled again.
7. **Conflict** (board deleted, renamed or permission changed while the modal was open): show the matching error. For a rename, update the expected name and clear the field.

### Matching rules (write these explicitly in the spec)

- Trim leading and trailing whitespace.
- Case-insensitive match. Typing is meant to force attention, not test spelling.
- Allow paste. Blocking paste frustrates users and doesn't meaningfully improve safety.
- Long names (for example over about 40 characters) or names with emoji or unusual Unicode are hard to type. Fall back to typing `DELETE`, or allow paste with a visible copy button.
- Apply Unicode normalization (NFC) before comparing, so accented names typed on different keyboards still match.
- IME input (CJK languages): evaluate the match only after composition ends.
- In localized UIs, the fallback keyword should be the localized word, and the label must say exactly which word to type.

## Spec: bulk delete modal (count, no typing)

- **Title:** `Delete {n} tasks?`
- **Body:** `These tasks, their subtasks and comments will move to Trash. You can restore them for 30 days.`
- **Buttons:** `Cancel` / `Delete {n} tasks`
- **Selection spanning views:** if the selection includes tasks the user can't currently see (filtered out, collapsed, or on other pages), say so: `{n} tasks selected, including {m} not shown in the current view.` This is probably a real source of accidental bulk deletes.
- **Partial permission:** `You can delete {x} of {n} selected tasks. {y} are owned by others and will be skipped.` Button: `Delete {x} tasks`.
- **Partial failure:** `{x} tasks deleted. {y} couldn't be deleted.` with a `Retry` action.
- **Large selections:** if you want extra friction, add type-to-confirm only above a threshold (for example 50+ tasks). Treat that number as a guess and tune it with data.

## Spec: undo toast (single task, comment, bulk)

- **Copy:** `Task deleted.` / `Comment deleted.` / `{n} tasks deleted.`, each followed by an `Undo` button.
- **Duration:** long enough to notice and act, probably 8–10 seconds (my judgment, not a standard). Pause the timer on hover and on keyboard focus.
- **Keyboard:** reachable via a shortcut, for example Ctrl/Cmd+Z when focus isn't in a text field. Announce it through an `aria-live="polite"` region.
- **Stacking:** a new delete replaces the toast, but the earlier delete must stay restorable from Trash. Never let a toast timing out be the only chance to recover.
- **Implementation:** the server should soft-delete immediately and `Undo` should restore. Don't delay the delete request on the client until the toast expires. That approach loses data or leaves ghost items if the tab closes.

## Edge cases checklist

**Content and relationships**
- Parent task with subtasks: say that subtasks are included, and restore them together.
- Restoring a task whose board is in Trash: either restore the board first or block the restore with a clear message.
- Restoring a task whose assignee has left the workspace: restore it unassigned and note that.
- Dependencies, links or automations that point to a deleted board or task: warn in the board modal if any exist, for example "3 automations use this board and will stop running."
- Comments with attachments: attachments follow the comment's delete and restore.

**Collaboration**
- Another user has the board open when it's deleted: show them a non-blocking banner, "This board was deleted by {user}", with a link to Trash if they have permission.
- Two users deleting the same item at the same moment: the second gets the "already deleted" message, not a generic error.
- Notifications: consider notifying the board owner or admins when a board is deleted. It helps them catch the "someone else deleted it" case early.

**Permissions and Trash**
- Who can see and restore Trash: the person who deleted the item, plus admins, is a reasonable default. Confirm this with PM.
- Items in Trash shouldn't appear in search, reports or exports. Specify whether they count against plan limits.
- Purge: say what happens at day 30, and whether an admin can empty Trash early. That action gets its own type-to-confirm modal, because it truly can't be undone.
- Audit log entry for deletes and restores.

**Interaction**
- Enter submits only when the button is enabled. Escape and ✕ cancel, except while submitting.
- Clicking outside the modal: don't close it if the user has typed anything.
- Delete triggered from a keyboard shortcut (for example Backspace on a selected task) should get an undo toast, since accidental key presses are a likely cause of these deletes.

## Accessibility checklist

- Use `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"`, `aria-labelledby` pointing to the title, and `aria-describedby` pointing to the body.
- Trap focus inside the modal. On open, focus the input (board modal) or `Cancel` (bulk modal), so Enter can't delete by accident. On close, return focus to the trigger, or to a logical element if the trigger no longer exists.
- Disabled button: native `disabled` buttons can't be focused and don't explain themselves. Either:
  - use `aria-disabled="true"` and keep the button focusable, with helper text such as "Type the board name to enable", or
  - keep the button enabled and show an inline validation message on click.

  Either is defensible. Pick one and use it consistently.
- Link the field's label and helper text with `aria-describedby`, and don't rely on placeholder text.
- Destructive red button: white text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast. The disabled state must still be recognisable through something other than colour, such as reduced opacity plus the helper text.
- Errors use `role="alert"`. Toasts use a polite live region. Toast actions are reachable by keyboard and aren't removed while focused.
- Touch targets at least 44px. The modal must work at about 320px width, with buttons stacked full-width on mobile.

## Testing recommendation

- **Before shipping:** with the ticket breakdown in hand, run 5 moderated sessions with existing customers. Tasks: delete a board, bulk-delete a filtered set, and "you deleted the wrong task, get it back." Watch for whether they notice the counts, whether they find Undo and Trash, and whether the board modal feels proportionate.
- **After shipping:** track accidental-delete tickets per quarter (target: a steep drop). Also track restore usage, undo clicks within 10 seconds (a direct measure of accidental deletes), and time-to-delete for boards.
- If the PM still wants type-to-confirm on everything, propose an A/B or staged rollout measured on those same metrics. That turns the disagreement into a test rather than a debate.
